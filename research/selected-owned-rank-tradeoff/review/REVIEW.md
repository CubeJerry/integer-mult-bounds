## Mathematical mechanism and adversarial checks

Nonzero normalized public coefficient gives complex-array surjectivity because
the frame map is invertible. On a fixed dyadic grid that argument does not
provide surjectivity: an odd numerator may obstruct division. The corrected
proof uses the right weaker fact. Images of admissible scaled physical input
basis vectors span the owned array space, and equality of linear maps on that
set implies equality everywhere. No unit-radius constant-one image claim is
needed. The norm approximation result concerns the induced owned-input
operator; it is not automatically a final-caller error bound in another norm.

For a replacement with one data entrance, track its input block in every
current field. Initially its largest row support is1; independent dirty/zero
scratch has support0. A monomial/scalar/COPY cannot enlarge the maximum.
Each binary ADD doubles it at most; a rank-s Gaussian child mixes at most
2^(sf) rows. Hence every output row has support at most2^(g+f sum s_j),
even with arbitrary coefficients, nonnested monomial maps and serial branching.

A row of the prescribed positive Gaussian return has2^(rf) equal-magnitude
entries, using the source's unit-modulus boundary maps. Missing coordinates
therefore contribute at least max(0,1-2^(g-(r-s)f)) to squared row error.
The spectral norm dominates row Euclidean norm. When g<(r-s)f, integer
separation yields squared error at least1/2. Exactness consequently forces
g>=(r-s)f. This is sound; a replacement receiving external correlated fields
would violate the one-input premise and is expressly outside the result.

For0<t<=1, sum s_j^t >= (sum s_j)^t. A strictly cheaper paid moment than
r^t therefore has s<r and requires at least f ADDs. A fixed finite template
cannot do so at all f. Under the separately declared literal full-pass tariff,
these cost Omega(Vf), which exceeds O(V f^t polylog(f)) for t<1. This is not
a general Turing-machine lower bound or a ban on fused/partial-field algorithms.
Its source relevance uses the all-column finite-template contract, not an
unproved conversion of scalar counts to arbitrary algorithmic steps.

The scalar escape is deliberately admitted: doing one butterfly per binary
coordinate uses rf ADDs and zero Gaussian children. Thus the obstruction
does not falsely claim that a positive Gaussian child is algebraically
indispensable regardless of ordinary work.

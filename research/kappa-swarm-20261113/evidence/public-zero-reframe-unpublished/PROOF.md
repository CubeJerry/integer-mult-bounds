# Same-owner stored-zero frame continuation: conditional interface audit

Prepared for CubeJerry with substantial OpenAI assistance. Apache-2.0.
This is a frozen source audit and narrowly conditional rule, not an admitted
selected opcode, a changed supplier, a paid-rank deletion or a κ claim.

## Predecessor credit and exact scope

The generic fact that an exact zero can be retagged is inherited, not new.
Canonical retained copies of #5423 `deferred-k-query-sharing-component` and
#5425 `fanout-optimality-support` are pinned in `SOURCES.json`, with their
notices. Their attributed heads are respectively
`bb81dd1f8aa34d5ee89a43f3c19d4b50606d6287` and
`00005c8c5e81c7f27b5769f9309ff88758e26f51`; snapshots here are explicitly
the canonical-main copies, not a claim of byte equality to those heads.

#5423's checker requires owned c0, an immediately preceding CLEAR and a
zero symbolic row before ZERO_RETAG. Its proof calls this a same-zero chart
annotation, not error/residual erasure; its ordinary census counts the retags.
#5425 similarly requires initialized private exact zero immediately after
CLEAR. Its construction contract says no live nonzero field receives a
zero-retag and counts every CLEAR/retag episode. Both leave physical routing,
storage, precision and compiler bills separate. Their statements do not by
themselves admit a live-public zero transition in #7481.

## Precisely sufficient physical premises

Let one live owner o have a complete payload buffer, unchanged backing and
occurrence path. Let A and B be two ideal invertible frame operators on the
same whole-array vector space. Require all of the following, rather than
inferring them from GF2 span equality or rank:

1. The **actual stored** payload is the canonical all-zero integer bit pattern.
   It is not merely an ideal zero plus an unknown rounded-child residual.
2. Both views use exactly the same record order, byte/bit layout, component
   widths, common quantum, complex-component convention, selected root factor,
   complete spectator/row range, volume and alignment. Their zero encodings
   coincide. No codec, packing or field-capacity conversion is performed.
3. Payload addresses, allocated backing, parent occurrence descriptor, all
   borrowed controls and head positions are identical before and after. The
   owner stays live; no original input is freed, replaced or newly credited.
4. The continuation's frame annotation can be changed in compiler proof state.
   If the actual ABI also stores any frame descriptor bytes or needs a
   descriptor lookup, those operations have a separately proved, charged
   implementation and restore the same parent/head state.

Under these premises, the identity physical payload trace is a correct typed
continuation from `(o,A,0)` to `(o,B,0)`: A and B both send the zero vector to
zero. All other physical bytes, owners, spectators and heads are identical.
The argument does not claim A=B, that their signed tableaux/phases agree, or
that a nonzero field may be retyped for free. It applies to a public owner
because it preserves that owner; it does not use owned erasure.

The payload trace has exactly zero reads, writes, moves, allocations,
initializations, erasures or recursive children. If the annotation is fully
compiled out, there is no extra payload service. A separate finite-control
join can be fused into the predecessor's terminal control state; if a chosen
machine representation emits a distinct join instruction, charge that fixed
control transition. This is a construction choice under premise4, not a
measured selected-machine tariff. A stored descriptor needs its actual
access/serialization/write cost; it is not automatically one tick or free.
No full zero scan is implicitly free: a runtime scan that checks the payload
would cost its complete record-volume service. A static certified cancellation
can supply the zero premise without that additional scan.

## Correlated stored-zero certificate

On the retained common signed-integer grid, COPY with coefficient1 into a
fresh owned zero buffer stores the same actual component integers as the
public source. A subsequent subtraction of that unchanged, bit-identical
snapshot with coefficient−1 writes canonical zero componentwise, provided
the two records have the same format and arithmetic has no overflow. No
operation may modify either operand between the certified snapshot and
subtraction. Their backing must be disjoint and the scalar service must
complete before a consumer. If stored source is ideal v plus previous error
e, both operands contain v+e; subtraction is exactly zero, not an appeal to
independent-error cancellation.

The corresponding ideal COPY/subtraction also gives v−v=0. Thus a newly
proved correlated rule could set both ideal mass and actual error to zero at
this cut. The retained positive triangle envelope does **not** prove that
reset: its generic ADD rule adds absolute error bounds and its zero tracker
discards ADD destinations. A lineage-aware certificate and a new precision
rule must be explicitly joined. Merely deleting a rounded MOVE when its
ideal argument is zero is not justified; the retained MOVE rule adds a
residual and says it need not preserve exact zero.

## What source inspection establishes and leaves unsupported

The pinned native checker uses frame IDs from the literal program and an
off-line `cur` map for type/frame validation. The physical replay obtains
each actual operator from fixed `frames` data and the instruction operands.
The common-grid proof explicitly describes its formal expansion as proof
state, not a runtime data structure, and supplies one common grid across
contexts. These sources support treating the **semantic frame annotation**
as static proof/compiler state; they do not specify a final selected tape ABI
that proves every descriptor access absent.

The separately pinned occurrence-layout theorem explicitly carries physical
descriptors containing ordered coordinates, complement generators, parent
orders and inverse-return words. Children push and restore these descriptors;
their construction/access and parent-return costs are counted. Its COST note
absorbs polynomial per-record descriptor work under an explicit cutoff after
counting template events. A semantic frame label is not automatically the
same as this physical layout descriptor. The proposed identity rule requires
the **entire latter descriptor unchanged**, not merely equal numerical frame
dimensions. It introduces no new route, occurrence injection, parked owner or
row split. If a final emitter couples the semantic frame to descriptor bytes,
their charged update is an unresolved interface premise.

The selected/#7481 grammar currently has INIT, CLEAR, MOVE, ADD, COPY and
SCALE only. INIT/CLEAR are owned-only, COPY is into fresh owned exact zero,
and every admitted public MOVE is a positive-rank nested paid operation.
There is no `ZERO_REFRAME` public branch in the checker or precision engine.
Therefore this audit does **not** establish that an existing #7481 word may
simply omit a paid MOVE. It proves a conditional physical identity lemma and
identifies the exact source/typing join that remains missing.

## Weakest joining obligation and bill

A sufficient local compiler addition would validate a same-owner transition
with: an immutable stored-zero lineage certificate; identical full numerical
format and occurrence/layout/backing descriptor; live unchanged public owner;
the previous instruction's exact source frame A and next consumer's frame B;
and the literal identity machine continuation (or separately billed descriptor
update). Its precision simulation rule must transport exact stored/ideal
zero without creating or suppressing a child residual. The ordinary ledger
must count any finite control/descriptor event, plus the separately paid COPY,
subtraction, owned backing lifetime, zeroing certificate production and future
cache fill. The full changed word still owes public endpoints, caller
restoration, new scalar/grid bounds and every inherited compiler assumption.
No blanket numerical fee is certified here.

This keeps all five assumptions: native all-column endpoints, owned
initialization/erasure, compiler, precision/tape closure, and analytic recovery.
The identity-on-zero lemma is independent of owned erasure; the surrounding
backup/cache lifetime still uses its retained contract.

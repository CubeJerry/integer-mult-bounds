# Why a common maximum-distance-separable source cannot carry the full native sum across the tight atlas

Use the signed 111-role table at commit e31d437b489a416446fdd7e294bc2238829c5925. A native donor j has a line R_j. Receiver i needs the sum of its foreign donors N_i={j:R_j is perpendicular to R_i}. Its facet is F_i=R_i-perpendicular. Complete the roots to the 120-line reflection system. Each parent A2 plane H contains three parent lines and each donor belongs to 28 such planes. For each j in N_i exactly 16 of its planes lie in F_i. A plane lies in 45 D4 middle charts, of which exactly 15 lie in F_i whenever H does. Each H→D4 port carries one scalar symbol. This is the tight full-native layout, before any additional inlet or middle ports are paid.

**Theorem (common source dictionary).** Let the demanded input groups at each role be copies of a common finite abelian group G. Let A be an alphabet with m≥2 symbols and |G|=m^16. Each bundle level can, for example, group 15 physical symbols over an alphabet of size q into m=q^15. Suppose the donor-to-plane coordinate belongs to a common family of functions f_c:G→A, up to an arbitrary bijective relabelling of that coordinate's alphabet. Any 16 distinct labels in the dictionary jointly determine the input. Each donor chooses 28 distinct labels and assigns one to each of its incident planes. Each plane forms outputs from the bundles at its native lines. Receiver i can get information from H only through the 15 outputs visible to i, jointly of cardinality at most m. No other path carries a donor's varying inputs to i except these 16 visible source-plane bundles per neighbour. Then no deterministic zero-error downstream coding can realize all 111 foreign sums.

This includes any common scalar MDS arc, any common linear array MDS dictionary with 16-wise direct-sum row spaces, and extended generalized Reed–Solomon evaluations in a common coefficient basis with arbitrary distinct parameters at each donor. A common invertible input basis or row-wise output bijections do not affect the theorem. The nine genuine binary canonical packets can also be supplied to receivers if their values are neutralized as specified below. Additional source edges, different local coordinates without a common MDS dictionary, or changes to the port capacities need separate analysis.

## Saturation fixes the missing coordinate

Fix i. For each native donor j in F_i, write S_j(i) for its set of 16 visible labels. These give a bijection from G to A^16 by cardinality. Fix an eligible H and two native donors j,k on H. Fix all other visible donors' bundles and all the visible coordinates of j and k away from H. The possible original values of j form a fibre U of size m, and those of k form an independently chosen fibre V of size m. Other source planes visible to i now have fixed inputs. Varying U,V can alter the receiver's H input in at most m ways, while the required outputs include a translate of U+V. Therefore |U+V|≤m.

For completeness, |U|=|V|=m with this bound implies that U and V are cosets of the same subgroup of order m. All translates U+v must coincide for v in V. Thus V−v_0 lies in the stabilizer of U. The stabilizer has at most m elements since U is a union of its cosets, giving equality. Holding one fibre fixed while varying the other shows that **all** the fifteen-coordinate fibres of each donor are cosets of a single common subgroup for this i,H. Every retained coordinate function of either donor is constant on its cosets. If their two sets of 15 retained labels had union of size ≥16, then 16 distinct dictionary functions would be constant on a coset of size m, violating joint injectivity. Consequently

    S_j(i) minus its label at H = S_k(i) minus its label at H.

The conclusion also holds when H has only two native lines. A one-native-line H imposes no cross-donor comparison.

For the binary native demand, arbitrary access to the nine canonical messages has a separate source justification. Their restriction to inputs *outside* N_i has binary rank nine for every i, as the independent [source check](../review/CERTIFICATE.json) verifies. For each assignment on N_i we can choose outside inputs making all nine canonical values zero, coordinatewise. The native self-plus-neighbours row belongs to their span; hence x_i equals the desired neighbour sum in that slice. Outside donors have no visible H inside F_i. This argument extends to binary vector banks and characteristic-two field banks. For a different abelian group the theorem uses the isolated additive sum contract directly.

## The native geometry forces one palette per receiver

Fix a label λ and a receiver i. Say that a donor in N_i is missing when λ is absent from S_j(i). Suppose j has the label and k misses it, and j,k are nonorthogonal. The unique A2 plane through j,k lies in F_i. The equality of the two retained sets forces λ to be exactly j's label at that plane. Hence if two missing donors are nonorthogonal to j through *different* A2 planes, j must be missing too. The distinction between different planes matters when two other lines lie in a common A2.

Start with each of the 185,428 two-element seeds across all 111 neighbourhoods. This rule exhausts the corresponding N_i for every seed in at most three simultaneous rounds. The [integer proof checker](closure.cpp) computes dots of signed native roots and identifies the mate line of each possible pair, including the case of a punctured third line. The [independent review](../review/REVIEW.md) recomputes the closures using a Gram-matrix condition. Its controls include all inert singleton seeds. Thus a label occurring anywhere in N_i occurs at least d_i−1 times, where d_i=|N_i|≥55. There are 16d_i available label occurrences, so at most floor(16d_i/(d_i−1))=16 distinct labels occur there. Every donor therefore has exactly the same 16-element palette S_i.

For H in F_i the missing-label equality now makes the assigned label at H identical for each of its native donors. Every active H has native perpendiculars, so it acquires one global label. For each active H and each arbitrary native donor j, the exact incidence check finds at least nine native i perpendicular to H and j. The label of H belongs to S_i, so it occurs at j. Every global label therefore occurs exactly once at each donor. There are 28 labels in total, and its m_c planes for any one label partition the 111 native lines. Each part has at most three lines, hence m_c≥37; the sum over labels is the number of active H, namely 1119. At most floor((1119−28·37)/(55−37))=4 labels have 55 or more planes.

Each of the other at least 24 labels must occur in *every* N_i. If none of that label's planes lay in F_i, every one of its planes would contain at most one line of N_i, since two distinct lines span the plane. Fewer than 55 planes cannot cover the d_i≥55 native neighbours by such intersections. Thus every S_i would contain at least 24 labels, contradicting its size 16. This is a source obstruction on a real finite native geometry, valid for all admitted alphabet sizes and arbitrary deterministic downstream functions.

## A fixed-node obstruction survives every input gauge

For the nominal base implementation use GF(64)=F2[z]/(z^6+z+1), with integers denoting their six polynomial coefficients. Each donor assigns parameters 0 through 27 in increasing order of its A2 planes' 120-bit role masks. Take H={7,8,9}, donors 7 and 8 and
the 16 receivers 0,5,49,18,106,17,6,80,79,51,34,91,109,107,108,110. For i in this list let p_{j,i} be the monic degree-15 polynomial vanishing at the other fifteen visible parameters of j. The [second exact certificate](GAUGE.json) uses their coefficient vectors. At donor 7 these 16 polynomials span dimension 15; at donor 8 they span 16. The independent certificate evaluates them at sixteen other points, obtaining an explicit dependency at donor 7 and a nonzero determinant at donor 8.

On fifteen stripes the varying H spaces are p_{j,i} tensor F^15, whose sum over this list has dimension 225 or 240. Arbitrary invertible changes of all 240 input coordinates at either donor preserve those dimensions. Relabeling the 15 port outputs likewise preserves their fibres. Saturation would require equality of the two H-variation spaces for each i and hence equality of the dimensions of their sums, an impossibility. Scalar extension preserves both ranks. The field, node indexing and shared node across stripes are specific to this second assertion. Different independently transformed and relabelled point configurations remain candidates.

## 1. Universal cyclic-flag interface

Let k be an integer with2<=k<=8. Let v_0,...,v_(k−1) in F2^9 satisfy

    v_i dot v_j = delta_ij.

Thus the v_i are independent, each initial source line L_i=span(v_i) is
excluded from its own final facet F_i=v_i^perp, and every other L_j lies
in F_i. Put P=span(v_0,...,v_(k−1)), of dimension k. Initially x_i,b_i
are authentic independent V-wide fields on L_i, and y_i,d_i are authentic
independent fields on chart0. The desired local paired map is

    x_i'=x_i, d_i'=d_i, y_i'=y_i+x_i, b_i'=b_i−d_i.          (1)

All other independent inputs may remain present. Scalar operations act
coordinatewise for every V>=1 over any field. In the native application
all selected ports are hosted, x_i is the genuine current a_i=x_i, and
the inherited arithmetic is characteristic zero.

For indices read cyclically, define the interval chart

    C_(i,l) = span(v_(i+1),...,v_(i+l)),  0<=l<=k−1,

where C_(i,0)=0. This is a strict increasing receiver flag and
C_(i,k−1) is a proper subspace of F_i. For a fixed source j, the charts

    C_(j−l,l),  l=1,...,k−1,

are the nested cyclic suffixes ending at j. They start at the actual L_j
and can continue to P and then the dimension-nine hub. At level l, receiver
i and source j=i+l occupy exactly the same C_(i,l). These two families of
flags realize every ordered pair i!=j without an extra moving copy.

This geometry is essential. It does not assume that every other original
source is available at one receiver chart. Each required copy is acquired
at its actual scheduled interval chart, using an authentic source field.

## 2. Complete paired chronology, including the adjoint

Initialize q=0 at chart0 and acquire q=sum_i d_i before any d_i leaves0.
Keep q there. For l=1,...,k−1 move each D_i to C_(i,l) and move each
X_j,B_j along its suffix flag to the same level. At the actual joint chart
C_(i,l), j=i+l, do both operations

    initialize stationary z_(i,j)=0;  z_(i,j) += x_j,
    b_j += d_i.                                             (2)

There are exactly k(k−1) real stationary caches. Original D values are
never changed. After the last level, send each D_i to F_i and each X_j,B_j
to P. Move q from0 to P by one owned rank-k call and do b_j-=q for every j.
The value of B_j is now

    b_j +sum_(i!=j)d_i −sum_i d_i = b_j−d_j.

Retire q at its actual chart P under the inherited owned-erasure service.
Its nonzero value is explicitly recorded; this is not a clean-zero claim.

At P initialize w=0 and acquire w=sum_j x_j from the authentic X fields.
Move w from P to0 by one owned rank-k call. Each still-original Y_i reads
y_i+=w at0. Retire w at0, again recording its nonzero source-dependent value.
Now each Y_i follows C_(i,1),...,C_(i,k−1). At C_(i,l), j=i+l, perform
y_i-=z_(i,j), then retire that actual cache after its only required read.
Finally send Y_i to F_i. Its value is

    y_i +sum_j x_j −sum_(j!=i)x_j = y_i+x_i.

Every selected X and B can now continue from P to Hub. This proves(1) for
all independent inputs and every positive V. The new reverse sum q is an
actual supplied adjoint, not a free row inferred from the forward formula.

The chronology has no circular source requirement. All stationary x copies
are made while X is still at its source flags. The new forward sum is made
at P before X reaches Hub. The selected Y fields wait at0 until w arrives.
The other native forward Gram modes may be constructed later at Hub without
changing this module. All nonzero scratch retirement is covered only by the
programme's retained owned-erasure premise; public x and d remain intact.

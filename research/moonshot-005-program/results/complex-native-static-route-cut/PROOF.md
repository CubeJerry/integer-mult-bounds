This holds even after every local scalar operation and every transported payload is redesigned, every old movement is retimed, and arbitrary local histories and zero-initialized owners are granted. A static chart cut gives two separate deficits of 82 field units, in opposite directions. Their sum counts each additional movement at most once.

At exponent 199/200, retaining the old route bill and adding these 164 units costs at least 3,417.637809671082... . The complete caller's limiting allowance is 3,317.516539247334... . Thus every such fixed-route completion misses by more than **100.121270423747** per private copy. This closes the fixed-route multi-phase recoding attempt, including the earlier single-episode repair class.

A rewrite can escape by changing the old route multiset. The same cut gives a quantitative requirement: its removed or retargeted old movements must supply more than 100.121270423747... units of tariff discount after debiting the crossing capacity they remove. In particular, at least **13 old movement occurrences must be removed or retargeted** to have any chance of meeting the 0.005 allowance. This is necessary only; it does not construct a 13-change repair.

## 3. Two information cuts, with interaction and arbitrary recoding

First set all input fields born in T to zero. At the start, T has no dependence on the 172 independent S fields. At any time, the dependence of all information retained in T on those fields lies in the span of the S-to-T messages received so far. Cochart operations, copies, initialized zeros, erasures and communication within T do not enlarge that span. A return message to S followed by a reply is still covered: the reply is another counted S-to-T message. Each complete transmitted field can add at most V dimensions.

The demanded outputs in T have rank 90V on these inputs by (1). The old list provides at most eight such arrivals, regardless of payload or retiming. If a redesign adds a_ST new S-to-T movements, then

\[
90V\le (8+a_{ST})V,
\qquad a_{ST}\ge82.
\tag{3}
\]

Apply the same argument after instead setting all inputs born in S to zero. Required output dependence in S has rank 146V, while the old T-to-S list has capacity 64V. Hence

\[
146V\le(64+a_{TS})V,
\qquad a_{TS}\ge82.
\tag{4}
\]

A directed movement crosses in at most one direction. Therefore at least

\[
a_{ST}+a_{TS}\ge164
\tag{5}
\]

new whole-field movements are necessary. This addition does not double-count an interactive transcript: the two inequalities concern two disjoint classes of actual directed transmissions. It is legitimate to derive them under different input restrictions because the same fixed linear source word must implement E on every input.

The ranks tensor with the width-V identity, while arbitrary local coordinate mixing is already allowed in the information argument. The result consequently holds for all stated V, with no assumption of commuting scalar encoders. Retained raw histories, early acquisitions, changes at many separated times, and arbitrary recoding of all old movement payloads are included. A source that brings fresh input-dependent information in through other paid routes has changed the stipulated route interface and must account for those routes.

## 5. Quantified escape through changing the old routes

Suppose a new construction retains only a subset of the old movement occurrences. Let D be the total old moment removed from its bill, and let c_ST,c_TS be the numbers of removed old occurrences crossing in the two directions. A retargeted occurrence is treated as removing its old directed arc and adding its actual new arc. This bookkeeping grants every retained old occurrence arbitrary recoding and retiming as before.

The two capacity inequalities now require at least

\[
164+c_{ST}+c_{TS}
\]

new crossing movements. Put c=c_ST+c_TS. Even optimistically charging every new movement at rank one gives

\[
Q\ge M-D+164+c
 =M+164-(D-c).
\tag{7}
\]

Accordingly, an affordable completion must satisfy the strict discount condition

\[
D-c>M+164-A_\infty
 >100.121270423747.
\tag{8}
\]

Removing a crossing saves its old tariff but also removes one unit of available cut capacity, explaining the debit c. This is not the raw total tariff of changed instructions.

Every old rank-nine movement crosses the cut. Its discount after the debit is 9^t-1<8^t, by strict concavity/subadditivity for 0<t<1. Any old move of rank at most eight has discount at most 8^t, regardless of whether it crosses. Thus removal or retargeting of k old occurrences gives D-c<=k8^t. The exact price enclosure verifies

\[
12\,8^{199/200}<M+164-A_\infty<13\,8^{199/200}.
\]

At least 13 old occurrences must therefore change. Satisfying that count or discount inequality does not establish correct payload acquisition, public rank preservation, target rows, timing or any supplier foundation. It identifies an explicit price obligation for a new route construction.

If one additionally insists on no additional movement occurrences at all, the cut alone requires at least 164 old noncrossing occurrences to be converted into crossing occurrences. That separate count assumes the number of movements stays at most 803; it is not asserted for a cheaper source that uses more low-rank calls.

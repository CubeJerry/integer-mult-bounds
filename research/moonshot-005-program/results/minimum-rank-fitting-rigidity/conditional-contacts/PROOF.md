## 1. Rank factorization through B

Because the B roots are pairwise orthogonal and every diagonal block is identity, K_BB=I_(8V). The rank hypothesis therefore gives

    K=C R,  C=K[:,B], R=K[B,:].

For instance, block Gaussian elimination leaves the Schur complement K_nonB,nonB-C_nonB R_nonB, whose rank must be zero. Forbidden zeros against B imply C_ip=R_pi=0 outside the support S_i of g_i. At an allowed contact write

    C_ip=g_ip X_ip,    R_pi=g_ip Y_pi,

where X_ip,Y_pi are invertible.

## 2. Exact ratio graphs glue the partial restrictions

For coordinates p!=q and an outside root i containing them, put sigma_i=g_ip/g_iq in{+1,-1}. If i,j have support intersection exactly{p,q} and G_ij=0, the fitting zero equation gives

    X_ip Y_pj=X_iq Y_qj,
    X_iq^(-1) X_ip=Y_qj Y_pj^(-1).

Consider the bipartite graph with left vertices X_i and right vertices Y_j, for every outside root containing p,q, and an edge for precisely those source pairs. The exact source certificate checks all28 coordinate pairs. Each graph has exactly two connected components: one contains all left vertices with sigma_i=+1 and all right vertices with sigma_j=-1; the other reverses the signs. Consequently there are invertible operators lambda_pq^sigma such that

    X_iq^(-1)X_ip=lambda_pq^(sigma_i),

Define lambda_qp^sigma=(lambda_pq^sigma)^(-1). The operators need not commute.

## 3. Comparing row and column triangles removes the sign choice

For one source root i with p,q,r in S_i, write sigma=g_ip/g_iq and tau=g_iq/g_ir. The X ratios obey the ordered identity

    lambda_qr^tau lambda_pq^sigma=lambda_pr^(sigma*tau).

The Y ratios obey

    lambda_qr^(-tau) lambda_pq^(-sigma)=lambda_pr^(-sigma*tau).

For every outer pair p,r the certificate supplies a source support quad and two actual rows i,j whose p/q and q/r signs are opposite. Applying the X identity to j and the Y identity to i gives the same ordered left side and respectively the two signs of lambda_pr on the right. Hence

    lambda_pr^+=lambda_pr^-.

## 4. Global matrix potentials and the diagonal

The X identity implies X_ip D_p^(-1)=T_i independent of p in S_i. The Y identity implies D_p Y_pi=P_i independent of p in S_i. For B rows define the same potentials by T_bp=D_p^(-1), P_bp=D_p. Therefore, including zero contacts,

    C_ip=g_ip T_i D_p,
    R_pj=g_jp D_p^(-1)P_j.

Their product is

    K_ij=sum_p g_ip g_jp T_i P_j=G_ij T_i P_j.

The diagonal is identity and G_ii=1, so T_i P_i=I_V. Hence P_i=T_i^(-1), proving the theorem. Conversely, block-diagonal conjugation preserves fitting zeros, diagonal blocks and rank, and makes every allowed B contact a nonzero scalar times an invertible matrix.

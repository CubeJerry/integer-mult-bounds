## Canonical slice and source separation

The native canonical code has rank nine after restriction to the complement
of every receiver's foreign set. Thus setting it to zero leaves all foreign
inputs independently variable. The corresponding self input varies so that
the true native target equals their sum. Proper increasing chart paths do
not convey those adjusted outside inputs to the receiver. Original Y values
can be fixed at zero. At a native line the incoming Y has not visited another
native source line, so on this slice it carries only a fixed ground baseline.
Consequently the tuple of early X and late Y departures at `(j,H)` is a
function of `X_j` alone, even if its full late operation is nonlinear in Y.
This is the correct restricted map; no global linearity of the operation is
deduced. The source claim presumes that ground's varying X information is
the retained canonical bank, not an additional unlisted raw-input channel.

On a tight `(j,i)` pair, the sixteen ordinary bundles have product capacity
equal to the donor's full input cardinality. Correct recovery makes the
tuple injective and therefore bijective. If two tight donors share a plane,
vary their independent fibres at that one coordinate while fixing the other
fifteen. Only the selected plane's visible H-to-D output tuple can vary;
later snapshots and recoding cannot bypass this already crossed boundary.
The sumset bound therefore forces both fibres to be cosets of one subgroup
of the specified size. Holding one fibre fixed while changing the other's
fixed coordinates makes this subgroup independent of the selected fibres.
The resulting translations preserve all other coordinates on every input.

The fifteen translation subgroups at a clean receiver sum directly: isolate
one increment in a putative zero sum, and use preservation of its coordinate
by every other increment. Its own translation already preserves the other
fifteen coordinates, so full-tuple injectivity forces it to be zero. Their
sum has exactly the cardinality of a fibre of the remaining H coordinate.
All those fibres are therefore cosets of the same kernel. This proves the
binary-subspace conclusion at every arbitrary positive record width.

## Weighted source construction and costs

Let `U_H` be the proposed quotient row space and `a_H` its required number
of early records. An invertible early source bank must select `a_H`
independent rows in each `U_H`, jointly independent across all H. Hence every
subset requires rank at least its total selected count. Repeating each
space `a_H` times reduces sufficiency to independent representatives.
The tight-sublist/quotient induction is valid over the original field and
does not need a scalar extension. The selected rows extend locally to bases,
leaving the stated late additions. This chooses a new source stage; it does
not preserve an arbitrary previous interior code's chronological coordinates.

The nonlinear necessity is also correct: any subtuple of an invertible
early tuple has its full product range, while functions of the supplied
linear quotients cannot have more range than their joint rank permits.
The independent controls exhaust all 256 ordered pairs of subspaces of
`F2^3` at weights `(2,1)` and all 4,096 ordered triples at weights `(1,1,1)`.
They compare all subset-rank inequalities against actual independent-row
choices, and retain the shared-plane counterexample to singleton-only checks.

For binary-expanded field lanes, two eliminations cost at most
`2B^2+6B`, raw copying costs B ADD, and R late parities cost at most BR ADD.
Thus the displayed `2B^2+BR+7B` bound, B INIT, B dirty CLEAR, and B stationary
owners are valid finite bounds for this source and its own inverse. The
downstream inverse, canonical formation, storage, precision and outer caller
are correctly additional. Tensoring fixed binary lane operations by arbitrary
body width is distinct from compiling a general nonlinear quotient at that
width, and the author states that limitation.

# Independent review of the commutative two-channel odd-cycle theorem

**Disposition: the universal theorem in Sections 1–4 is valid under the stated independent-channel and monotone-geometry interface. No new kappa.** This review reads the mathematical argument, reconstructs its coefficient equations, and challenges its scope. It does not label a rerun of the author's finite checker independent verification.

## Independent reconstruction

For each target i, quotient all X coefficient rows by the span of original input lines contained in its actual facet F_i. A descending channel never leaves the downset once it enters. Under the exact update law P <- alpha P + local(C), every subsequent selected-X row in that quotient is a scalar multiple of its first-entry row. Histories may be copied, retained, and read repeatedly; they still generate one cyclic module in this quotient. Thus one decoder coefficient lambda_p,i per channel is sufficient simultaneously for every input column outside F_i. It is not a separately chosen coefficient for each nonorthogonal neighbor. This point is essential to the multiplication argument.

For a nonorthogonal edge i,j, neither input line belongs to F_i or F_j. Whichever facet downset the channel first enters, no later local term can inject either selected input column. The ordered pair of column coefficients therefore undergoes the same through multiplier t before the second first entry. If i is first, the pair changes from (d_p,i,a_p,ij) to (t d_p,i,t a_p,ij). Hence a_p,ij a_p,ji=d_p,i d_p,j in a commutative ring. Equal entries, zero resets, and zero divisors do not require separate inverses or cancellation. If a channel is born inside the first downset, its local-birth rule gives zero on both these columns, so the identity remains valid.

The own-target equation is lambda_1,i d_1,i + lambda_2,i d_2,i=1. Each foreign edge coefficient cancels between the two channels. Multiplying the cancellation equation at i by that at j, and commuting the four factors, identifies the product of channel-one own contributions with the product of channel-two own contributions. Writing u_i=lambda_1,i d_1,i therefore gives u_i u_j=(1-u_i)(1-u_j), hence u_i+u_j=1. On an odd cycle the weights alternate to u_i=1-u_i. Since 2=0 and 1 is nonzero, this is a contradiction. Every step is valid in a nonzero commutative ring, not merely a field.

The bipartite converse is an algebraic relaxation only: use one sum of selected inputs per color. Vertices in one color are pairwise real-orthogonal, so each receiver can remove other same-color inputs from its freely granted local information. This does not grant physically available local sums or a paid complete source. The author's distinction is correct.

## Scope checks and adversarial controls

1. Arbitrary independent original Y values do not invalidate a necessary selected-X coefficient argument. The proof never substitutes physical Y=0. It does exclude packet feedback carrying nonlocal X history through Y, because that violates the channel update law.
2. Every additional descending cache or packet entering a receiver downset is another communication source unless its selected-X projection is local or is already generated by that same channel. It cannot be hidden under a phrase such as stationary history.
3. Mixing two packets breaks the reciprocal identity even over F2. Let a first packet enter F_i with selected coefficients (x_i,x_j)=(1,0), meet a second packet carrying x_j inside F_i but outside F_j, and add it. At its later first entry into F_j its coefficients are (1,1). Then a_ij a_ji=0 but d_i d_j=1. The meeting can occur at F_i, followed by a descent to F_i intersection F_j. This is an admitted coupled operation and lies deliberately outside the theorem; it does not itself complete an odd cycle.
4. Arbitrary noncommuting block through maps also break the product identity. Take first-entry diagonal I, foreign coefficient A=[[0,1],[0,0]], and later through map T=[[0,0],[1,0]] over F2. Then the cross product is A T=diag(1,0), while the diagonal product is T A=diag(0,1). The independent scalar proof cannot be lifted to such blocks. If every block lies in one common commutative characteristic-two matrix algebra, the ring proof does apply.
5. The theorem grants all actual first-entry observations and all local inputs within each receiver facet, a larger information set than one ascending Y route can generally acquire. This direction of relaxation is sound for impossibility.

The next constructive escape must therefore use a genuinely coupled packet or Y history, or noncommuting block operations, and bind the resulting exchanges to a real cochart chronology. A failed finite circuit search would not strengthen this universal theorem.

## 1. General dynamic conditional-rank theorem

Let a finite linear execution over any field start at a specified handoff.
Let D be any downset of its geometric charts. Collect the coefficient rows
of EVERY live record currently inside D in a matrix O, using all independent
original input columns, including every arbitrary original Y. Let T contain
the coefficient rows of demanded final values whose final charts are in D.
If the actual future entrance messages into D have total dimension at most c,
then necessarily

    rank([O;T])-rank(O) <= c.                              (1)

Chronological induction expresses every future inside-D history as a linear
combination of O and the actual messages that have entered so far. Internal
operations, stationary zero births and zero records introduce no new input.
Departures and erasures can only remove available state. An entrant may depend
on earlier departures and feedback; opening its actual value preserves the
induction without assuming independent messages. The final target rows lie
in the span of O and all entrants, adding at most c dimensions. This proves(1).

If the only future entrants are h persistent whole-V descending packets,
each entering once, then c<=hV. An ascending record starting outside a downset
stays outside. Stationary or ascending zero-born caches add no entrance
channel. This is the narrow route model used below. If additional proper-born
descending copies are allowed, count their actual first-entry capacity too.
Equation(1) still holds, but h cannot then mean only Hub-born full descents.

A fixed scalar handoff tensored across V coordinates multiplies its deficit
by V. Arbitrary future noncommuting block gates do not evade the rank bound.
The application matrices below are binary and have the same ranks over every
characteristic-two extension field. Other-field reinterpretations of this
native XOR word are not asserted.

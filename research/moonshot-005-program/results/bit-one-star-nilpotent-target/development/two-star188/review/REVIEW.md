## 1. Native support and both rank separations

The independent checker authenticates the S013 native-root recovery and
reconstructs adjacency directly from integer dot products. Receivers 80 and
42 are adjacent, have degrees 62 and 61, and share neighbor 8. The canonical
binary matrix K has rank nine. Restricting K to columns outside the union of
their neighborhoods gives rank seven; restricting it outside the two receiver
roles gives rank nine.

As an additional independent check, the reviewer enumerates all 512 words of
the binary canonical row code. Exactly four are supported on the neighborhood
union: 0, K_80, K_42, and their sum. Only the zero word is supported at the two
receiver roles. These finite binary facts give the stated subspace dimensions
over every characteristic-two field by extension of scalars. Tensoring with
the internal identity preserves them.

The submitted row-separation argument is valid. A row of V that is canonical
must be K_80 tensor alpha + K_42 tensor beta. Both cross-star donor columns of
V are zero, so the two diagonal/contact entries force alpha = beta. At the
common neighbor this canonical row therefore vanishes. The common-neighbor
block of V has full row rank 188, so its row combination must be zero.

Column separation is a distinct requirement. The perturbation image is
supported at receivers 80 and 42, and the outside-pair rank-nine fact excludes
a nonzero canonical image vector with that support. Symmetry of the binary K
allows the same restriction test to establish this column statement; symmetry
of the perturbed block matrix is not assumed.

The rank-addition lemma is also correct. If the rows of V are independent
modulo row(M), then V restricted to ker(M) is onto. The image of M+UV therefore
contains im(U). Subtracting those image vectors then shows that it contains
im(M). Column separation makes the two images a direct sum, giving
rank(M+UV) = rank(M) + rank(UV). Both separation hypotheses are necessary;
the independent checker includes two-dimensional controls in which retaining
only one of them fails to add ranks.

These facts prove the exact full-width fitting and repair ranks without
pretending to have row-reduced a 639,360 by 639,360 matrix.

## 2. The submitted selectors and the nonflat conclusion

The reviewer reads the submitted full-width selector artifact and checks
every native block location and every selector coordinate. The two image
spaces have dimensions 93 and 95 and are disjoint. Every selected covector
annihilates its own image space, so every nilpotent part squares to zero.
The corresponding characteristic-two shear is its own inverse. Unmodified
foreign blocks are identities, proving the all-block invertibility claim.

For each star, the selectors collectively include every coordinate outside
its image. Thus their common kernel is exactly that image. The 188 selector
rows at neighbor 8 are distinct coordinate covectors and are independent.
Their square-zero parts have an additive commutator of exact rank 186, as
checked independently by sparse basis-column action at the full width.

The flatness proof permits arbitrary injective embeddings at each vertex.
The unmodified cross-star equations identify the embeddings at the two
centers. Every other reverse star edge is also unmodified, so the forward
shear equation places that center embedding in every selector kernel. Its
image must therefore lie in both disjoint star-image spaces. It is zero, and
injectivity excludes every positive subsystem dimension.

This implication genuinely uses both stars and their full selector coverage.
The independent negative controls remove one shared external coordinate from
all selectors and recover a one-dimensional flat system. Deleting the second
star likewise leaves the first star image as a flat line in the tested width.
A separate control allows a selector to read its own image coordinate and
produces a singular foreign block, detecting the square-zero premise.

The common-left statement has a different meaning from input flatness. All
covectors annihilating the sum of the two image spaces are fixed on the left
by every foreign block. The submitted images occupy the last 188 coordinates,
so the first 5,572 rows are literally common correct rows. No inference from
these left quotients to a flat input subsystem is valid here.

## 4. Scope and verdict

The target refutes a proposed inference from small positive fitting excess
to a large canonical input subsystem, even when all foreign blocks are
invertible. It is consistent with the near-minimum foreign-rank bound: that
bound controls individual block deficiencies and does not force flatness.

Neither return-bank bridge creates the target's proper blocks. Before this
target can yield an improved multiplication construction, its shared original
donor maps, genuine late line taps, early invertibility, middle/terminal
transport, invertible pure-Y map, ordinary operations, precision, storage and
complete recursive suppliers must still be supplied. The movement screen
is conditional on those physical obligations and is not a new complete kappa.

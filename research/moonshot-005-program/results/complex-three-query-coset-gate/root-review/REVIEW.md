## Why the intermediate is forced

Put L=[[a,I],[0,a]], with a rank-one group unit. Suppose three same-side
rank-one queries produce an invertible pure E3 map on two independent roles.
After removing the last query, W2 has no rank component below two, by the lower
Gaussian rank triangle, and none above three. Its E3 component lies in the
second query's affected output row. The other row is consequently pure E2 and
has full physical row rank d, because W2 is invertible.

The E2 component has rank at most d: after the final ordinary change it lies
in the final query's one affected row. Its full unaffected row forces equality.
Writing its ordinary image column as b,c, the square coefficient b must be
invertible: b times a d-by-2d row has rank d. Finite ordinary matrix algebras
are inverse closed by Cayley-Hamilton. Thus the remaining E2 row is C times
the full row for an ordinary C, and subtracting it isolates a pure E3 row T.
The same full-rank argument forces the ordinary coefficient of the first
query's affected E2 row invertible. No arbitrary individual ordinary block
has been declared invertible merely because its containing matrix is.

This gives the precise normal forms used below. Relative to a pure E2 row R
and the first query's untouched row S, the second operation must leave a
complementary coefficient B equal either to an invertible ordinary mu, or to
g mu with g a rank-one query unit. The companion T=A R+B S is pure E3.
If B=g mu, its other coefficient is A=g lambda-C, all displayed letters
except group units ordinary. Block triangularity of the actual invertible
ordinary change supplies mu's invertibility.

## First in-place query

Write the prequery ordinary basis as rows w,z. A normalized pure row has the
form R=(g1 w+h)L. The first coefficient is g1 w1 a+h1 a. The supports g1 H a
and H a are disjoint, so purity forces h1=0. In the second coefficient,
g1 H a is disjoint from both g1 H and H a. Its rank-one terms must therefore
cancel exactly: g1 w1+h2 a=0. It follows, without dropping any low terms,
that R=g1 w diag(a,a). The other row is S=zL.

Right multiplication of T by diag(a^-1,a^-1) and the inverse of the ordinary
matrix with rows w,z preserves a lower rank bound of two. Its second block is

    B+B z1 a^-1 (O^-1)22.

For B ordinary invertible, the first summand is a nonzero element of H's span
and the second lies in H a^-1 H, which is disjoint. For B=g mu, the respective
supports are gH and gH a^-1 H, also disjoint. The first summand has rank zero
or one and cannot disappear. This contradicts the lower rank bound. The
argument permits all ordinary zero divisors in z1 and the inverse block.

## First shear query

Write the first-query rows as zL+g1 wL and wL. After ordinary normalization,
R=(g1 w+h)L with rows h,w forming an invertible ordinary matrix. The same
support separation gives h1=0 and g1 w1=-h2 a. The matrix [[0,h2],[w1,w2]]
is invertible, forcing h2 and w1 invertible. Only these forced pivots are
inverted. Ordinary rescaling gives, with p=w1^-1w2 possibly singular,

    R=(a²,a p a),       S=(a,I+p a).

Let Y be the first block of T=A R+B S. Exact ordered multiplication gives
T2=Y a^-1 p a+B. Since both blocks of T are pure E3, the lower triangle and
ordinary bimodularity imply B a^-1 has no rank below two.

For a second shear, B is ordinary invertible and B a^-1 is nonzero pure E1,
a contradiction. For an in-place query B=g mu and A=g lambda-C. Then

    Y a^-1=g lambda a-Ca+g mu.

The support gH a is disjoint from H a and gH. The latter terms are rank one,
so absence of low components forces g mu=Ca. Consequently B a^-1=C is both
ordinary and supported in ranks at least two; it must vanish. This contradicts
invertibility of B. Matrix coefficients need not commute anywhere in this
derivation. Rescaling the ordinary row basis changes lambda,mu,C, not the
form of the actual group query g times an ordinary coefficient.

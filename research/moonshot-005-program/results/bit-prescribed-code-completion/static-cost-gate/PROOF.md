## Weighted cut

Give boundary1 weight1, boundaries2 through5 weight `c=(5^t-1)/4`, and all
other boundaries weight0. Every downward MOVE from dimension a to b pays at
least the sum of the weights it crosses. If it crosses boundary1, then b=0.
For `1<=a<=5`, concavity gives `a^t>=1+(a-1)c`; for `a>=5`, monotonicity gives
`a^t>=5^t`. If it misses boundary1 and crosses m<=4 positive-weight boundaries,
then `a-b>=m`, and

    (a-b)^t >= m^t >= m 5^(t-1) >= m(5^t-1)/4.

The last inequality follows from `5^t<=5`. Upward and unused operations only
increase cost. Five crossings of boundary1 and one of each boundary2 through5
therefore cost at least `5+4c=4+5^t`. The point-only case costs14, which is
larger because `4+5^t<9`. This proves the proposition.

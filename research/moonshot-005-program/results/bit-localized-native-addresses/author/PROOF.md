## 2. One localization through unbounded finite compilation

**Saturated-basis lemma.** Let S be a finite set of primes, R_S=Z[S⁻¹],
and P an n-dimensional idempotent over R_S of rational rank r. There is
an explicit integer matrix G, invertible over R_S, with
P=G diag(I_r,0) G⁻¹. It can be constructed solely by rational denominator
clearing and integer kernel bases.

Choose a positive product d of primes of S clearing P, so E=dP is
integral. With V=im_Q P, W=ker_Q P, take integer bases for the saturated
sublattices V∩Z^n and W∩Z^n. They are the integer kernels of dI−E and
E, respectively. A tracked unimodular column elimination or Smith
normal form computes these bases. Concatenating them gives the columns
of G. Their rational spans are complementary. For every x∈Z^n,

    dx = Ex + (dI−E)x ∈ (V∩Z^n) + (W∩Z^n).

Thus the finite cokernel of G is killed by d, all its prime divisors lie
in S, and |det G| divides d^n. This proves that G and its inverse
exist over R_S. The column grouping gives the displayed diagonalization.
This elementary lattice proof is sufficient without a choice of a
nonzero rational minor that may vanish modulo another prime.

For two equal-rank idempotents P,Q take S_PQ=G_P G_Q⁻¹ in GL_n(R_S).
It conjugates Q to P. Split a rank-r requested P by partitioning its
first r adapted-basis coordinates into the required positive consecutive
pieces. Conjugate their coordinate projectors by the same G_P.
They sum to P, are pairwise orthogonal as idempotents (both products
zero), and have exactly the prescribed ranks. They need not be symmetric.
Sequential execution of their D operators gives D(P) on every cell.
The notation for orthogonal idempotents here refers only to a zero
product, without an inner-product condition.

Tensor products, auxiliary identities, positive column repeats, permutation
embeddings and direct sums preserve these properties. In particular,
the embedding of a local GL symbol S on a projector sector E uses
S⊗E+I⊗(I−E), with inverse S⁻¹⊗E+I⊗(I−E). Changes of placement are
conjugations in the same ring. Starting from the audited charts and
unit-valued rational port adapters, induction through the chronological
product, splitting, zero specialization, homogeneous levels, root tiles
and peeling produces no address denominator or invertibility exclusions
outside {2,3}. The number of productive fields and normalized tariff
fractions do not enter the actual address matrices. Every compiler word
keeps integer role counts. Neither 1/B for a batch nor 1/v for a product
is an address instruction.

For every prime q≥5 and every w≥1 the ring map R→Z/q^wZ exists.
All matrices G,S and their inverses specialize as units. Every projector
is conjugate to diag(I_r,0) over the local ring, so its free rank stays r,
also after a column lift by I_f for every positive integer f. Right
route g↦gS permutes the entire GL_N(Z/q^wZ) fibre. No separate new prime
avoidance is needed for the **compiled address catalogue**. The generic
leaf-cover size bound, paid bad rows, physical right permutations, outer
analytic conditions and any independent non-address exclusions still
apply. For an integer lower bound L≥5 satisfying the remaining
size requirements, the first prime above L is already admissible for
these address projectors and matchers.

Two exclusions delimit the assertion. The integer idempotent
P=[[15,−10],[21,−14]] has a nonzero rational leading entry that vanishes
at q=5. Nevertheless G=[[5,2],[7,3]] has determinant one and directly
diagonalizes it at every prime. Conversely, splitting the identity by
the rational *orthogonal* projector onto (1,2) gives denominator 5.
This choice is unnecessary: a localized adapted-basis split works.
There is no claim that the arbitrary split just exhibited reduces over R.

    X_i,c ↦ X_i,c,     Y_i,c ↦ Y_i,c + X_i,c

for every i,c, with no cross-copy residual. There is no rank-only
replacement for this equality. If the circuit is initially written over

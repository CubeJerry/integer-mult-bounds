# Independent review of the 95-receiver nonlinear witness family

**Pass in the deterministic, finite-alphabet, fixed-schedule, zero-error model.** The source-specific lower bound is 9b future downset descent events and 8b disjoint private-region arrivals. The same reasoning proves a bound on missed witness regions at larger transcript sizes and conditional full entropy at equality. This does not identify a nonlinear transcript as a linear canonical code or price its routes.

I independently enumerated four pairwise nonorthogonal vertices in U and their common nonorthogonal complement after deleting those vertices. For ten-vertex remainders, I recovered the five independent four-stars. Every vertex belongs to exactly two stars, all ten resulting pair labels differ, and two vertices are adjacent precisely when their pair labels are disjoint. This explicitly identifies the graph with KG(5,2). The enumeration counts only witnesses obtained by the stated exact-complement rule. No exhaustiveness over alternative larger complements is needed. One Petersen-edge deletion breaks the identification.

For any certified W, fix all input variables outside W, including every Y, and choose the b whole messages of each role in W independently and uniformly. The replay from three pinned literal schedules checked every live field over all 222b binary input columns. For each receiver the available source U-scope in its facet is exactly its native orthogonal neighbors. Giving all of those neighbors to the decoder is therefore a valid relaxation. Without an additional owned descent into that facet, no information from outside the facet enters under local gates, public ascents and nested owned moves. Cutting chronological downward entries, the projected transcript Z_W of all events landing in the union of the W facets suffices for each receiver together with its own neighbors and the fixed outside inputs. Later cut payloads may depend on earlier ones. The schedule itself conveys no extra information.

The four isolated b-record messages X_I are recoverable from Z_W after outside inputs are fixed. Thus H(Z_W) = 4b·h + H(Z_W | X_I), where h is the entropy of one uniform record. For every fixed choice of X_I the ten remaining messages are an independent product and the same Z_W enables all Petersen decoders. I expanded the predecessor's 21 positive rational inequalities under all 120 permutations of the five symbol labels, using all 1,024 subset entropy variables with no symmetrization assumption on the code. They telescope exactly to −H(Z_W | X_I)/(b·h) ≤ −5. Removing a term invalidates the identity. Consequently H(Z_W) ≥ 9b·h for every outside assignment. Each of the included events has capacity at most h, so Z_W must comprise at least 9b events.

The count for each W is valid independently, with the same physical schedule. Thus if the total number of events into D_U is 9b+s, at most s land outside D_W, for every W. At exactly 9b, each Z_W includes every D_U event, and its entropy equals the total capacity 9b·h. By the chain rule every chronological conditional entropy attains h after fixing the outside inputs and earlier blocks. On finite alphabets this forces joint uniformity of all transcript blocks conditional on each outside assignment. It does not make an event a function only of W or preclude use of outside inputs as masks. Positive conditional entropy does force some dependence on W.

The private-region arrivals are a different count. Replaying each word recovers four rank-six pair charts, each contained in just its two target native facets, with precisely the 2b private public Ys inside the region of that chart and facets. Those Ys lack all 2b private own input records. Conditional counting forces at least 2b informative arrivals into each region. These destinations are outside D_U, so this adds 8b occurrences without repetition. Width expansion, free input-dependent schedules and randomized error are outside this review.

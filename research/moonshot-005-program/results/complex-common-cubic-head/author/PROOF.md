## 2. Literal canonical source and independent halves

Four arbitrary input fields X,Y,U,V begin at line a, ground, line a, ground, respectively. The literal source is:

1. MOVE V from ground to line a; ADD U-=V; MOVE V to facet b-perp.
2. MOVE Y from ground to line a; ADD Y+=X; MOVE Y to facet b-perp.
3. MOVE X and U from line a to the full nine-dimensional chart.

All ADDs see genuine common charts. The MOVE ranks are 1,7,1,7,8,8; there are no owners or zero inputs. Its physical map is
\[
 X'=F_sQ_bX,\quad Y'=F_sQ_aY+F_sX,\quad
 U'=F_sQ_bU-F_sQ_aQ_bV,\quad V'=F_sQ_aV. \tag{1}
\]
For example, the last U MOVE applies F_sQ_b to the current U-Q_aV. The checker replays all four input coefficients exactly in the finite head group algebra; the common F_s factor follows from the displayed chronology.

The XY and UV instructions have disjoint supports and no cross-plane reads. Each plane is therefore a standalone four-event, two-input source with three MOVEs of ranks 1,7,8 and one ADD. Its inverse is the literal reversed word with the ADD sign reversed. One half costs

Independent rebinding and sign conjugation do not assume an absent other-plane input.

## 3. A common coupling from an ordinary three-cycle

Let P be the ordinary binary coordinate permutation induced by the invertible linear map
\[
 P:a\mapsto b,\quad b\mapsto c,\quad c\mapsto a.
\]
It acts at every tensor column and fixes all body coordinates. Hence P^3=I and \(P Q_vP^{-1}=Q_{Pv}\).

Define
\[
 A=Q_b^{-1}P,\qquad A^{-1}=P^{-1}Q_b.
\]
Preapply P^-1 to Y and V in (1), and postapply P^-1 to X and U. These are exactly four ordinary array permutations. Since
\[
 Q_aP^{-1}=P^{-1}Q_b=A^{-1},
\]
the resulting native is exactly
\[
 \boxed{
 X'=F_sA^{-1}X,\quad
 Y'=F_s(A^{-1}Y+X),\quad
 U'=F_s(A^{-1}U-A^{-2}V),\quad
 V'=F_sA^{-1}V.} \tag{2}
\]
No Gaussian query or new MOVE was added. The full source still costs \(2h_t\).

Writing
\(L(A)=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\A&I\end{pmatrix}\) and
\(U(-A^{-1})=\begin{pmatrix}I&-A^{-1}\\0&I\end{pmatrix}\),
equation(2) is
\[
 F_s A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(L(A),U(-A^{-1})).
\]
A is the SAME operator at every fresh stage, and it commutes with every F_s. This is the useful change of interface.

The diagonal head A^-1 must remain in the invoice and endpoint algebra. Declaring F_sA^-1 to be a fresh rank-eight body and then counting that same head anew at every stage would be false. Repeated head factors reduce by the actual A-power identities below.

## 5. Explicit paid rank-one cyclic conjugacy

Put
\[
 U=Q_b^2 Q_c.
\]
U is one rank-one Gaussian followed by an ordinary translation; U^-1 has the same rank price. The cocycle calculation is
\[
 U P^2U^{-1}
 =Q_b^2Q_c\,Q_a^{-2}Q_b^{-1}P^2
 =Q_b^{-1}Q_c^{-1}P^2
 =A^2.
\]
The middle equality uses \(Q_a^2Q_b^2Q_c^2=I\), retaining the ordinary translation. Squaring and using(3) gives
\[
 \boxed{A^2=U P^2 U^{-1},\qquad A=U(ZP)U^{-1}.} \tag{4}
\]
Thus the order-three part of A is conjugate to an ordinary three-cycle through one explicitly priced rank-one Gaussian basis change. The checker rejects omitting Q_b^2 from U.

Equation(4) is a structural opportunity for a shared caller. It does NOT make U ordinary, authorize six free changes of original-input basis, or prove that the changes can be shared between independent fields.

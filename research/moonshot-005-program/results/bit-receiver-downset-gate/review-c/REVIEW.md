## Universal argument and load-bearing details

Let D be the union of downsets of all facets F_j with j outside c. It is a
downset of proper charts. For every C in D, LocalX(C) is contained in one
of those facet-local spaces, so W_c intersect LocalX(C)=0. The admitted
Y-feedback normal form says every actual history's X coefficient lies in
W_c tensor F^V + LocalX(C), provided row(G_x) is contained in W_c tensor F^V.
Its projection pi_C onto W_c is therefore unique.

At a common chart, every allowed linear operation takes linear combinations
of these projections, including arbitrary noncommuting block operations and
unknown-Y feedback. When a carrier MOVEs between nested charts both in D,
the projection agrees across the MOVE. The rigorous reason is uniqueness
at the LARGER chart: both local remainders lie in its local space. This is
essential for descending MOVEs, where local support shrinks; it avoids
assuming that an arbitrary local remainder may descend unchanged.

Original X inputs born inside D have zero projection, as do all original
Y inputs and zero-born caches. Ascending originals born outside cannot enter
a downset. A separately priced zero-born ascending copy likewise cannot
introduce a new projection except from actual in-D histories. Each
persistent descending Hub packet has at most one first entry into D, and
after entry it remains in D. Hence the span of all projections that ever
occur in D is generated by at most q whole-V first-entry histories. This
argument counts actual histories, not fictitious simultaneously available
records, and remains true with interleaving and stationary storage.

For every completed outside-class receiver j, the endpoint X coefficient
is e_j. Its unique projection at F_j is u_class(j), because the difference
is local. Thus the in-D projection span contains fourteen independent
whole-V class spaces. Its dimension is at least14V but at most qV, proving
q>=14.

Unknown original Y does not disappear physically: its X coefficient is
zero, while the full actual source must still obey all Y endpoint equations.
The theorem deliberately uses the already admitted X projection of the
Y-feedback normalization. It does not grant remote original-Y reads.

## Boundary of the result

The theorem can fail when the actual history code leaves W_c, when an
additional data-bearing descending lifetime enters D, when an outside
history is initialized inside for free, or when a nonmonotone/lateral
source route crosses the cut differently. Such changes require their own
counts and physical proof. The local-intersection-zero premise is necessary:
own-class facets have a real local direction K_j+u_c, so including those
facets in D would invalidate the projection argument.

The original sixteen-dimensional W_c candidates are therefore excluded
for q8 within the stated source interface, even with arbitrary cocharted
Y feedback and separately counted zero-born ascending copies. This does
not exclude arbitrary sixteen-dimensional historical spaces, general
noncanonical codes, or additional lower-chart masked exchanges. In
particular H_i=K_i+x_i leaves W0 and is not covered merely because its
incoming packet was K_i.

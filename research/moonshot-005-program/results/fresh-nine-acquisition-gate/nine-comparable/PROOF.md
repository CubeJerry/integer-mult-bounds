## A mixed group-capacity lemma for components of size at most two

Suppose all components have size one or two. Different components have incomparable births and disjoint source groups S_j. Give each group capacity p_j=1 or2, with sum p_j=9-z. Put E_j=span{g_i:i in S_j}, and E_0 for sources used by no nonzero packet. In the8V-dimensional output coordinate space, the image U of the z global decoder columns satisfies

    dim U<=zV;  E_0 tensor K^V subset U;
    dim((E_j tensor K^V+U)/U)<=p_j V.

Thus r_j=dim E_j<=z+p_j. If r_j=z+p_j, dimension forces dim U=zV and U subset E_j tensor K^V; call this a saturated group.

If a saturated E_j is fixed, every other group's outer projection in E/E_j has dimension at most p_k. When that dimension equals p_k, its scalar intersection F_k=E_j intersect E_k satisfies F_k tensor K^V subset U. Put F=sum F_k, f=dim F; then f<=z. After quotienting by F, that group's image has dimension at most p_k. Source projectors still span the whole symmetric square after any scalar quotient.

Two elementary projector bounds will be used. In dimension3, subspaces of total capacities4, each at most2, span at most5 symmetric-square dimensions: four lines give4; a plane and two lines give5; two planes intersect in a line and give at most3+3-1=5. Also source roots cannot lie in the union of two proper scalar subspaces: the product of nonzero annihilating linear forms would be a nonzero quadratic vanishing on all source roots, contradicting projector spanning.

**Mixed-capacity lemma: z>=6.** For z=0,1,2,3 the maximum source capacities from the M_r bounds are respectively13,25,45,78, all below96. For z=4 with no saturated group, the zero group has at most12 roots and the remaining capacity5 at most5*12, totaling72. If a singleton saturates, E_j has rank5; projection to dimension3 has remaining total capacities4, whose symmetric squares span at most5, contradicting dimension6. If a pair saturates, E_j has rank6 and remaining capacities are three singletons or a pair and a singleton; the rank6 argument below excludes both.

For z=5, first if E_0 has rank5 then U=E_0 tensor K^V. The dimension3 quotient with remaining total capacities4 gives the same5<6 contradiction. Hence E_0 has rank at most4 and at most12 roots. With no saturated group, every singleton has rank at most5 and a pair rank at most6; the remaining total capacity4 covers at most4*20 roots, totaling92. A saturated singleton has rank6 and is excluded below. A saturated pair has rank7. If the remaining capacity2 is another pair, all roots lie in two proper subspaces, impossible. Otherwise it is two singletons. Their outside intersections lie in U, giving a scalar F of rank f<=5; quotient projector dimensions would require

    binom(9-f,2)-binom(8-f,2)=8-f <=2,

whereas8-f>=3.

For completeness, the rank6 saturated argument used in both cases is as follows. The remaining capacities sum3. If they are three singletons, all their nonzero outer projections are lines; include their intersections in F. Then

    binom(9-f,2)-binom(7-f,2)=15-2f <=3,

but f<=5 gives15-2f>=5. If the remaining capacities are a pair and a singleton, and the pair outer projection has dimension at most1, projecting directly to E/E_j gives at most two lines, whose projectors cannot span Sym^2(K^2) of dimension3. Otherwise that pair outer projection has dimension2; both outer groups are saturated in their capacities, so their intersections lie in U. The same quotient difference15-2f>=5 would have to be at most3+1=4. All alternatives contradict projector spanning. This proves the mixed lemma for every V without presuming a tensor factorization of U.

## Shared centers must process every overlap

Let a higher center C have distinct eligible-line leaves c,d. Each pair consisting of its center and one leaf has at least V target rows by the general pair fact. Their supports lie in I(c), whose W shortening has exactly dimension V. Thus the pair contains the ENTIRE root-code direction W(c); likewise it contains W(d) with the other leaf.

Take a source in I(c) intersect I(d). If its coefficient in the center packet were zero, the two root-code directions would require nonzero coefficients in BOTH leaf packets. Its unchanged D would then have to visit the incomparable lines c and d, impossible. Therefore the center actually uses every source in that overlap. The overlap has at least15 sources, so the center birth-to-Hub rank r is at least5, since a(4)=8. Its chart contains both lines, so all its source coefficients lie in their intersection, of population at most28. Its source capacity is therefore at most min(a(r),28), with5<=r<=7.

This argument uses only support and the full-V target rows; singular or entangled packet operators do not weaken it.

## The priced finite case split

Assume packet moment J<8*9^t. Write r_e for each birth's codimension in the9-Hub. Its transport costs at least r_e^t; extra stages cannot improve this bound for0<t<=1. Since1<=r_e<=9,

    J >= 9^(t-1) sum_e r_e,

so the integer sum of birth codimensions is at most71. In particular every0 birth contributes9 and every line birth8. We now cover all components.

If all components have size at most two, the mixed lemma gives z>=6. For z>=8 the global returns alone cost at least8*9^t. For z=7 the sole pair contributes a line rank8 plus another rank at least1; total birth ranks are at least7*9+8+1=72, contradicting the assumed bound.

For z=6, there is one pair and one singleton. Write r for the other pair birth rank and s for the singleton rank; the pair also has a line rank8. Thus r+s<=9.

* If r,s<=7, their two packet supports contain at most37 sources by the exact a(r)+a(s) enumeration. This is below distance42, contradicting the pair dimension fact.
* If r=8, both pair births are at the same eligible line. Then s<=1. The pair group covers at most52 roots, the singleton at most1, and the zero-only root space has rank at most6 and covers at most36. At most89 of96 sources can be covered, impossible.
* If s=8 and the pair is strict, r<=1. The pair span contains all W(c), so every source in I(c) supplies a packet in that pair component. Its D cannot also visit the incomparable singleton birth; hence the singleton has no coefficient on I(c). The rank1 child packet uses at most one source j. A W row supported in this child plus singleton must vanish on I(c) minus {j}, which still spans all8 root coordinates by the two-disjoint-bases certificate. Such a W row is zero, contradicting the pair dimension fact again.

It remains to allow a component of size at least three. The center absorption bound z+h>=6 and2h<=9-z exhaustively restrict the possibilities. With z=3, h=3 and exactly six nonzero births, every component must be a pair, already covered. With z=4, h=2, five nonzero births permit only a three-node star plus one pair. With z=5, a shared component is a four-node star or a three-node star plus singleton. With z=6 it is a three-node star. Larger z has no shared center.

For z=6 and a three-node star, six global ranks and two line ranks already contribute70; the center would need rank at most1. Its overlap obligation gives rank at least5, impossible.

For z=5 and a four-node star, the five global and three line ranks contribute69; the center would need rank at most2, again impossible.

For z=5 and a three-node star plus singleton, let r be the shared-center rank and s the singleton rank. The five global and two line ranks force r+s<=10. Since r>=5, s<=5. The selected pair of center and singleton packets has support at most32, by the exact bounds min(a(r),28)+a(s). This contradicts distance42.

For z=4 and a three-node star plus pair, there are three line leaves and two centers. Write r for the shared-center rank and s for the other pair member's rank. The four global and three line ranks give r+s<=11. Here r>=5, so s=8 is impossible. Both centers have ranks at most7. Their joint support is at most40 by the exact bounds min(a(r),28)+a(s), again below distance42. Every shared-center case is excluded.

This contradiction proves J>=8*9^t for every comparable-birth nine-packet supplier, at every V and every characteristic-zero field. The proof never classifies an arbitrary rank6 quotient kernel.

## Exact 54-packet implication

Let S_ij indicate that receiver i is granted q_j. Allow field-volume V,
arbitrary linear coordinate mixing, and r broadcasts of at most V coordinates
each. Grant every receiver all broadcasts without distribution costs.
Universal recovery of all independently varying q_i implies r>=54.

The supplied 54 source roles have a checked topological ordering: every one
of their 99 induced side-information edges goes forward. A scalar fitting
matrix has rows e_i minus terms on receiver i's known coordinates, and factors
through the broadcast map. On these 54 vertices it is triangular with unit
diagonal, hence rank 54. For block codes the same principal submatrix is
block triangular with identity diagonal and rank 54V. The broadcast map's
rank is at most rV, proving the claim for every admitted V.

Nonlinear terminal decoding of the same linear observations does not evade
this argument: universal recovery implies kernel containment, hence a linear
recovery factorization over the coefficient field. This is not a claim about
unrestricted nonlinear encodings of several exact real values into one.
Reversing the certified edge 1->86 makes a two-cycle and rejects the DAG
certificate. Zero diagonal alone is insufficient: two receivers knowing
one another's inputs can decode from a single sum packet.

## Eight-packet indistinguishability certificates

For each of 104 receivers and each 33/47 variant, the supplied rational vector
z has z_i=1, zero entries on every coordinate that receiver knows, and zero
product with the entire eight-packet encoder. The independent checker verifies
all 208 vectors. Inputs q=0 and q=z therefore give exactly the same packets
and known coordinates while the desired q_i differs. This disproves universal
decoding for that interface, including nonlinear decoders of those records.
Changing the target coefficient by 1 introduces the nonzero encoder column
a(v_i), so the displayed kernel witness is mutation-sensitive.

The ordinary Gram decoder would need 48 other ports at each receiver. The
witness proof is stronger than merely finding missing terms in that formula:
it excludes every alternative decoder of the same eight packets and actual
coordinate side information. Conversely, granting the full ideal orthogonal
side information makes the exact Gram decoder work. These are distinct data
interfaces and must remain distinct in programme state.

## Critical scope boundary: common-response observations

This theorem concerns the specified graph for routing stored source values.
It does not follow merely from the original common word having these helper
occurrences. A q-dependent perturbation may spread through native ADDs and
then travel on an unlisted native MOVE. A receiver might exploit that dirty
common history after cancelling its nuisance inputs. Such extra observations
are outside the graph's granted coordinate set unless a separate source
response theorem shows that they carry no further information.

Accordingly the graph lower bound applies when the candidate interface uses
only the admitted timed arcs to convey source information, with initial
hoisted caches and no other usable q-bearing observation. It does not exclude
arbitrary endpoint-restored common-mode network codes, joint cancellation of
nonneutral helper pulses, changed native processing, new observer charts,
retimed occurrences, or reuse of consumed main routes. The latter two require
new graphs and independent verification. This boundary is material: the
programme's next escape must be analysed as a new interface, not labelled
impossible by the present 54 certificate.

Shared slot capacity, physical side-information acquisition, scalar costs,
cache/storage lifetimes, common restoration and other endpoint duties are
all generously ignored in this negative routing model. Their omission makes
the scoped bound stronger; it does not make a matching upper construction.

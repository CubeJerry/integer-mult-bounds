# A rectangular tensor does not rescue the retained eighteen-stage caller

## Result and scope

Let P104's displayed decoder have eight rank-one rows of binary pairing
price seven, on 104 odd labels spanning `F2^9`. Let another finite row
decoder over a characteristic-zero field have n odd labels spanning
`F2^g` with ordinary binary dot product. Repeated label values are allowed.
Tensor the displayed row decompositions, retaining each product row as its
own owned chain in each of the two native planes.

**Every integer g>=2 fails the retained eighteen-stage raw moment test at
saving1/200.** The bound grants cheapest single-jump public/private routes,
zero excess helper movement after credit, and zero source formation,
correction, scalar, ordinary, precision and cleanup costs.

This is a specified row model and caller. Shared ascents, changed callers,
other label geometries and other sources are outside its scope. No native
word or supplier is constructed. **No new kappa.**

## 1. An exact rank certificate over characteristic zero

Write `Q_ij=q_i dot q_j` as ordinary 0/1 scalars. A decoder M has diagonal1
and is zero on every off-diagonal odd cell, so `Q Hadamard M=I_n`. If
`M=sum w_k u_k^T`, its k-th contribution has characteristic-zero rank at
most `rank_Q Q[D_k,S_k]`. Suppose every support rectangle satisfies

    rank_Q Q[D,S] <= lambda_g rank_F2 Q[D,S].

Rank subadditivity then proves

    n <= lambda_g sum_k r_k,
    r_k=rank_F2 Q[D_k,S_k].

Rational ranks agree over every characteristic-zero field. The complete
certificates are:

| g | odd labels | lambda_g | decoder mass floor |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 1 | n |
| 3 | 4 | 4/3 | 3n/4 |
| 4 | 8 | 3/2 | 2n/3 |

The checker visits all rectangles up to transpose: 10,136 and32896. It
includes every subfamily and gives equality witnesses. Repeated labels only
duplicate rows/columns of a rectangle, preserving both ranks; therefore
arbitrarily many repeated labels are covered as well.

Every rank is exact. The rational engine uses prime65537, while all 0/1
minors of order at most8 have determinant absolute value at most
`8^(8/2)=4096<65537`. Hadamard's inequality thus proves that every nonzero
minor remains nonzero modulo this prime. Binary ranks use separate XOR
elimination. This is not a random or merely one-sided rank test.

## 2. Convert mass to the target moment and tensor

Put `t=199/200`, `c_g=1/lambda_g`. Since `0<=r_k<=g`,

    r_k^t >= g^(t-1) r_k,
    R_factor(t)/n >= c_g g^(-1/200).

The support pairing matrix of each product row is a Kronecker product, so
its price is `7r_k`. With eight base rows, moment per product port obeys

    rho >= (8*7^t/104)c_g g^(-1/200)
         = (8c_g/(104g))(7g)^t.

The native label dimension is h=9g. Ordinary Gram dimension and scalar row
count are not substitutes for this physical geometry.

## 4. The rejected four-label Gram and a legal control

Odd labels `e_1,e_2,e_3,111` have a three-leaf-star odd-cell relation.
A Gram factor consisting of the three coordinate unit vectors and a unit
vector u has the right diagonal. For u=(1,2,2)/3, however,

    M_(e_j,111)=u_j

is a nonzero forbidden odd entry. Each occurs in one rank-one row, so no
cancellation is possible. The checker rejects all six directed entries.
The bilinear complex vector(1,1,i) has the same polarity defect. The three
row supports have price1, but the assembled matrix is not a decoder.

A legal control puts all three leaf ports in one common direction and111
in an orthogonal direction. Its two rows have prices3 and1, total4. It does
not realize density3/4. No global optimality of this control is claimed.

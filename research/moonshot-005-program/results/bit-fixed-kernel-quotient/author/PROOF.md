# A fixed kernel grant does not freely enlarge the terminal seed budget

**Algebraic corollary of the admitted sharp terminal-seed quotient theorem; independent review and source admission pending. No new kappa.**

Let N,G⊂D be finite-dimensional subspaces of a vector space over any field, and let W,E be arbitrary subspaces. Let T range over subspaces with N⊂T, dim T≤q and W⊂D+T. We measure the enlarged observable space S=T+G, granting the fixed kernel rows G in addition to terminals without claiming physical availability. Put

    n=dim N, r=dim((D+W)/D),
    z=dim(E∩(D+W))−dim(E∩D),
    k_G=dim((N+G)∩E).

Feasibility holds precisely when q≥n+r. In that range,

    max_T dim((T+G)∩E) = min(dim E, k_G+z+q−n−r).

Proof: write g=dim G and a=dim(N∩G). Every S=T+G contains N+G, has dimension at most q+g−a and satisfies W⊂D+S. Conversely, if a subspace S satisfies those three conditions, choose a complement to G in S extending a complement to N∩G in N. Its span with N is a T with S=T+G and dim T=dim S−g+a≤q. Thus this is exactly the original quotient theorem with mandatory space N+G, dimension n+g−a, and budget q+g−a. Substitution cancels g−a and gives the formula. No block commutativity is used; tensoring fixed scalar spaces and allowing arbitrary operator-valued T is included.

The theorem does not assign a cost to G or guarantee a normalization whose kernel is G. It shows why counting a fixed acquired kernel as a new freely chosen seed direction is incorrect. Its benefit depends on the actual intersection (N+G)∩E, not merely dim G.

## 1. Exact reciprocal block

Let F be any field. Let a,b in F^p and c,d in F^m be four independently
variable banks. All statements hold coordinatewise for any positive field
volume. Let M be an invertible p-by-p matrix, let I be m distinct coordinate
indices, and let E select those coordinates, so A = E M.

Assume the actual chart schedule can do all of the following:

1. Bring each paired a_j,b_j carrier to one cochart C and apply M to a and
   M^{-T} to b using real cocharted elementary operations.
2. Bring both encoded carriers with coordinate I_i to receiver chart F_i,
   where c_i and the unchanged original d_i, or authentic delayed records
   with those values, are actually available.
3. Bring every encoded carrier to one hub H and execute M^{-1} on a and M^T
   on b, before any later operation requiring the restored a bank.

At F_i execute the two actual operations

    c_i += (M a)_{I_i},       (M^{-T}b)_{I_i} -= d_i.

The resulting complete bank map is exactly

    a_out = a,       d_out = d,
    c_out = c + A a, b_out = b - A^T d.

Indeed, the encoded b bank after all receiver injections is
M^{-T}b - E^T d. Hub decoding multiplies this by M^T, giving
b - M^T E^T d = b - A^T d. The a bank has not been changed at the receivers,
so its inverse restores a. The displayed map is invertible, with inverse
obtained by changing the signs of the two shears. It preserves the bilinear
quantity a^T b + c^T d. No forward donor original is inferred from a discarded
duplicate.

This proves the algebra under the stated physical schedule. It does not make
M a available at an arbitrary chart or grant a second bank when the source
has only one physical carrier. Neither the symmetry of the target matrix nor
the invertibility of M removes those requirements.

## 3. Exact shared-register criterion

Partition {1,...,p} as U disjoint H. Suppose the source has a single physical
carrier for a_u and b_u at every u in U, so b_U=a_U, while a_H and b_H are
independent. Require the proposed encoded interface to use the same physical
carrier for the corresponding two encoded outputs, with no extra local
reconstruction:

    (M a)_U = (M^{-T} b)_U for every a_U,a_H,b_H.

**Theorem.** This literal shared-register interface is valid if and only if,
in the U,H block order,

    M = diag(Q, R),    Q Q^T = I,

where Q and R are invertible. Empty blocks are allowed. Thus the coupled block
must be orthogonal, and it cannot mix with the independently represented block.

**Proof.** Comparing independent a_H coefficients gives M_UH=0. Write
M = [[Q,0],[C,R]]. Since M is invertible, Q and R are invertible. The U,H block
of its inverse transpose is -Q^{-T} C^T R^{-T}. Comparing independent b_H
coefficients forces this to vanish, hence C=0. Equality of the shared a_U
coefficients then says Q=Q^{-T}, equivalent to Q Q^T=I. Conversely those block
conditions make the two U output rows identical for all allowed inputs.
This proof is valid over every field, including characteristic two.

The theorem excludes only the literal alias-preserving compiler above.
Additional retained records, alternative encoded coordinates, changed
physical carriers, or a complete current-observation decoder may evade it,
but require fresh source and cost evidence. It is not a universal lower bound
on paired coding or on the number of paid returns.

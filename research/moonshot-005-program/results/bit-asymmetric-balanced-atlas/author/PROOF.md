# An asymmetric bank-480 atlas with explicit GF16 local words

## Results and scope

There is a literal causal native atlas with **480 field coordinates per role, ordinary quotient width 30 and two lanes per plane-middle flag**. It has the same normalized movement profile as the original bank-5760 balanced atlas, with only one twelfth as many field owners. The source allocation is changed: at a two-native plane, one donor has 15 early fields and the other has 30. This avoids fractional symmetric quotas.

The finite construction provides:

- A checked phase/orientation certificate, 106,560 distinct original-owner routes and exact native bank counts.
- One simultaneous rank-only source over GF(2^25), with all 6,470 foreign blocks invertible, all original X restored, and arbitrary original Y handled by its actual invertible preload.
- Explicit GF16 V9 and derivative local plane words. Every actual receiver projection has full donor rank. These fixed left-plane words can be retained in a simultaneous rank-only source over GF(2^28).
- Fully charged binary ordinary bounds, the unchanged movement invoice, and exact low-defect budget thresholds at the smaller bank.

No complete native identity map is asserted. Source synchronization, a numerical full coefficient table, recursive admission and the inherited foundations remain open. The changed phase allocation cannot inherit the old mixed-69 flow witnesses by uniform scaling. Fresh common-row work is kept in a separate package.

## 5. Explicit GF16 local modules

The independently checked nine-port partition supplies five disjoint classes of nine exits at each H. Every parent receiver sees exactly three exits in each class. This package authenticates that certificate and checks all 40,320 parent receiver-plane incidences. It uses GF16 with modulus `u^4+u+1`, and nine distinct nonzero nodes a=1,...,9 in that field.

Let `V[a,t]=a^t`, t=0,...,8. It is a 9-by-9 Vandermonde matrix. Any three rows restricted to any of the consecutive three-column blocks are invertible: each is a nonzero diagonal multiplier times a three-column Vandermonde matrix.

At a full H use two independent V9 stripes in each class, with one or both stripes early according to h_H. Each donor owns three coordinates per stripe, so its complete class quotient is six-dimensional and its selected three-port projection is invertible.

At a two-native H, call the 15-early donor small and the 30-early donor large. In each class the small donor has three early and three late coordinates; the large donor has six early coordinates. Order the nine early inputs as the three small coefficients and six large coefficients. Apply V9 to them. There are nine actual late Ys: three visit the small native line and receive its late parity additions; six begin directly from ground at H. Apply V9 to that late tuple, then add the derivative contribution of the large polynomial

`p(t)=t^3 Q(t), deg Q<6`.

The cross matrix is `C[a,t]=d(a^t)/da` for t=3,...,8 and zero for t<3. At any three selected nodes, the large donor is observed through values and derivatives of p. At each nonzero a,

`(p(a),p'(a)) = [[a^3,0],[a^2,a^3]] (Q(a),Q'(a))`.

The displayed matrix is invertible in characteristic two. A polynomial of degree below six with zero values and derivatives at three distinct nodes is divisible by their three squared linear factors, hence is zero. Thus the six large coordinates are recovered. The small donor sees separate invertible early and late three-column Vandermonde blocks.

At a singleton use V9 on its nine early coordinates per class and V9 on the nine actual late ground Ys. Add the full derivative matrix. Any three ports give six independent value/derivative observations of a degree-below-nine polynomial; the degree-below-six subspace already proves rank six. This matches the singleton's 45 source fields and thirty-field receiver cut.

All 84 triples are checked, including 252 full-plane donor minors, both two-native donors and singleton projections. The pure late map remains invertible on arbitrary original Y values. The code also replays the complete early/late binary word on all independent input basis vectors. Dropping the derivative loses rank for the large donor, and repeating nodes fails the Hermite condition.

## 6. Retaining the fixed GF16 left-plane library

The fixed local modules can coexist with simultaneous foreign invertibility. For one foreign pair, each eligible H's fifteen visible port values have full donor rank thirty, with different H using disjoint formal source inputs. The causal D/K/F matchings of Section 3 route **all** of these selected early and late port coordinates. Consequently their transfer from the donor's formal early/late source slots to the chosen receiver has row rank 480, even with the H modules held fixed.

An arbitrary source matrix E_j/L_j can therefore make that one foreign block invertible. Its particular E_j need not be invertible at that witness. Separately, choosing E_j=I and invertible downstream phase blocks witnesses the nonzero pure-wave determinants. The same polynomial-product argument synchronizes every nonzero determinant condition, including genuine early source invertibility. This is a rank-only conclusion, not a common-output equation.

The field must contain the fixed GF16 coefficients. GF16 does not embed into GF(2^25). Degree28 is sufficient: use GF16[t]/(t^7+t+1), with the binary irreducibility test for t^7+t+1 and coprime degrees seven and four guaranteeing irreducibility over GF16. The resulting field has 2^28 elements, above the same degree bound. In the explicit basis u^a t^b, every fixed GF16 local word is repeated seven times. Its costs are multiplied by seven; no four-bit local invoice is silently reused for twenty-eight-bit records.

The generic degree25 code and fixed-H degree28 code are distinct certified alternatives. No simultaneous common-row theorem is transferred between them without fresh witnesses.

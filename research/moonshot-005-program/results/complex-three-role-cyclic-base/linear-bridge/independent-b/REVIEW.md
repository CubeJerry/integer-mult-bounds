1. Starting with `KQKPK=D`, multiplication on both sides by `K⁻¹` gives
   `QKP=K⁻¹DK⁻¹`. Thus `R=P⁻¹` gives the stated linear condition, and its
   inverse substitution reconstructs the word in the correct noncommutative
   order. The finite-dimensional inverse-closure step is valid for a unital
   subalgebra: apply the characteristic polynomial in a faithful finite-
   dimensional representation. No commutativity of ordinary entries is used.

2. For the abelian refinement, the finite-group Fourier map is an algebra
   isomorphism under the stated characteristic and splitting-field premises.
   Applying it entrywise reduces matrix units to simultaneous invertibility
   of the scalar character blocks. The product of determinant polynomials
   is nonzero precisely when each is nonzero. An infinite field ensures an
   evaluation avoiding that product's zero set. The proof correctly does not
   infer singularity from a failed random search or a small physical width.

3. The diagonal implementation is conditional, as it must be. The finite
   residue tuple is sufficient for a fixed diagonal tensor-phase algebra;
   base-8 carry updates have total O(8^V) digit operations, by summing the
   geometric carry series. A finite scalar-matrix catalogue gives a finite
   pivot catalogue and bounded local condition constants. The actual paid
   source layout, scalar bit costs, compiler, and precision foundations are
   expressly retained. This does not authorize a free coordinate-dependent
   operation in a contract which lacks that service.

4. The treatment of endpoint gauges is correct. The literal-target no-go
   cannot be applied to `U D V` after arbitrary ordinary endpoint encodings
   are introduced; those encodings change the linear map itself.

The exact checker uses a separate C₂×C₂ Fourier example. It verifies an

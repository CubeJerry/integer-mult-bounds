# Clarification: the restoration frontier is time scoped

The fixed-frontier counting lemma applies to the information available to Hub when original X must be restored, before the later canonical/Y work. Its sufficient hypothesis is that every old observation available for that restoration is a function of the retained H signals and the other retained original-owner coordinates. The recovery transcript accounts for all additional X-dependent information that is available across that restoration boundary.

Stationary H snapshots or caches that are unavailable to Hub at that time do not distinguish inputs for this particular restoration task and do not invalidate its counting argument. A cache that becomes available for restoration would instead be additional side information and must be included in the retained observation or transcript; the lower bound must then be recomputed from the resulting fibres.

Such stationary H caches may nevertheless be available to the later proper B endpoint. Therefore the information theorem does not imply any canonical-return excess from H signal rank loss alone. It gives only the stated necessary extra-information count for the original-X restoration interface, and the direct gap6 tariff applies only to the declared export implementation. The later B target and canonical return require their own actual information and cost analysis.

This time-scoped version is the same rank/cardinality proof with the observation set taken at the restoration boundary. The frozen author proof, checker and source pins remain unchanged.

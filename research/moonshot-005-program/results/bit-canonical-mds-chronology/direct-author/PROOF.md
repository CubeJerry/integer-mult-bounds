## 1. General direct-query basis lemma

Let the current original-X coordinates form a basis with row space H direct-sum D, where H consists of the actually available Hub records and D has independent current held rows d1,...,dr at charts C1,...,Cr. Let C be the desired code, dimension c. Assume its coordinate projection onto D is surjective. Then C has a basis consisting of c−r rows in C∩H and r rows ci=hi+di, with hi∈H.

Indeed rank-nullity gives dim(C∩H)=c−r. Choose any lifts ci of the r coordinate basis vectors of D, then append a basis of C∩H. Their projections prove independence and their number is c. This is valid over every field; no source availability follows just from the abstract lemma.

For an actual source, form every chosen H component at Hub. A Hub-only code field descends to ground. The exceptional field for ci descends from Hub to the actual chart of di, adds that actual held record, then descends to ground. If the held record remains there until all queries finish, this is a valid chronological source. Its value is ci and its source record is unchanged. The direct-query cost is a real path, not an uncharged local operation: for ambient dimension8 and chart dimension d it is[8−d]+[d]. All seeding, reads, source retention and ordinary/owned operations must be included.

Surjectivity matters. If the projection is only a proper subspace of D, an arbitrary coordinatewise query basis need not exist. The earlier common-intersection patch remains a more general mechanism for such source interfaces. No universal optimality is claimed for the present choice.

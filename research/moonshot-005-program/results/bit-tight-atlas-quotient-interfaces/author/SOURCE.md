# Rigidity of separated donor encoders at the saturated native atlas

**Proved.** A separated deterministic encoder for one of the 3108 native source flags in the S022 atlas must be an additive quotient map followed by a relabelling of the output alphabet. The maps may initially be different for every donor and plane. In the native binary bank model every resulting quotient is binary linear. This imposes no global MDS condition on the resulting subspaces. Their existence as a jointly aligned atlas remains open.

Let G be a common finite abelian donor bank with |G|=m^16, m≥2. Every native j sends a value of an alphabet of size m on each of the 28 planes through it, via arbitrary fixed deterministic functions f_{j,H}:G→A_{j,H}. At a native receiver i the 16 visible functions of a fixed j are jointly injective. Indeed fix all other native donors in its facet. The receiver must distinguish the |G| different choices of X_j in their additive sum, and only these 16 tuples depend on X_j. Their total capacity equals |G|, so the map to their product is bijective. The target must be the local additive residual or admit neutralization of the rest of its source data. The companion gluing review proves that neutralization even with free access to the full binary canonical bank in the native input model.

For any visible plane P with at least two participating native donors j,k, fix those donors' other 15 coordinates. Their two source fibres U,V have size m and vary independently; all other visible bundle coordinates can be fixed. The shared P cut offers at most m outcomes. It must distinguish the |U+V| possible additive corrections. Thus |U+V|=m. For v0 in V, each U+v equals the same size-m set U+v0. Hence V−v0 lies in the stabilizer B of U. Since nonempty U is a union of cosets of B and V−v0 has m elements, B has exactly m elements, and U,V are both its cosets. Fixing one side's fibre and varying the other shows that B is independent of the fibre choices. Repeating with any further donor gives one group B_{i,P} for that plane and receiver. Consequently adding an element of B_{i,P} to X_j preserves every visible donor coordinate other than P. On a fibre fixing those other coordinates, different group elements give different P-coordinates by tuple injectivity.

Now fix any (j,H). The literal geometry gives a native i seeing H for which all other 15 visible planes P at j have at least two native donors. There are at least 13 such choices for every one of the 3108 flags. The 15 groups B_{i,P} then sum directly. If a sum of increments from them is zero, isolate the increment coming from one P. Its negative is a sum of increments each preserving the P-coordinate of all X_j. This P-increment therefore preserves the P-coordinate too. It preserves every other coordinate by its defining property, so it must be zero. Applying this to every P proves independence.

Each of the 15 groups preserves f_{j,H}. Their direct sum K_{j,H} has size m^15, exactly the size of each fibre of f_{j,H} by the 16-coordinate bijection. Each fibre is therefore one coset of the single subgroup K_{j,H}. Relabelling the quotient G/K_{j,H} gives exactly f_{j,H}. Over an elementary abelian two-group all subgroups are binary subspaces. This is a restriction on the source maps even when everything after their emission is nonlinear.

Once these subgroups are chosen, write L_{j,H}(i) for the intersection of the other 15 K_{j,P} visible to j at i. The 16 quotient maps together give a group isomorphism onto the product, so L_{j,H}(i) is complementary to K_{j,H}. Its quotient coordinate gives an isomorphism from L_{j,H}(i) to G/K_{j,H}. For every multi-donor H the subgroup agrees across its participating donors by the plane cut. At a single-donor H this assertion holds trivially. Write the result as L_{i,H}. In its chosen receiver frame the H contribution is consequently the sum of the unique lifts into L_{i,H} of the participating donor quotient values. This identifies the exact linear target for the shared 45-port test in MIDDLE.md.

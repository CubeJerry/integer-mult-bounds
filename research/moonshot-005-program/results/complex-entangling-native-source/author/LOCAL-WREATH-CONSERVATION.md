## 1. Actual finite family and the phase interface

Fix a head count d≥2 independent of tensor width. Let G be the actual finite unitary group generated by single-coordinate Gaussian C_i, single-coordinate quarter-phases D_i, single-coordinate Pauli translations/phases, and permutations of the d coordinate axes. These are tensor products of one-qubit Clifford matrices followed by a coordinate permutation. Let H be its monomial subgroup: local diagonal quarter-phases, local Paulis, and coordinate permutations. Finite complex linear combinations of H are permitted ordinary coefficients in arbitrary invertible role matrices; this is a larger algebra than any one fixed physical instruction catalogue.

Allow every admitted native orientation with diagonal A,A and cross either an ordinary scalar or a scalar times A², where A is a rank-one element of this wreath family (or its inverse). The actual order-nine source B=i C_b D_b P is one such A. Also allow paid one-bank or additive-shear Gaussian queries whose coefficients are scalar multiples of elements of G. Independent endpoint jobs are elements of G, up to invertible matrices over the ordinary algebra. Every role is arbitrary and retained. No entangling CNOT, CZ, or multi-coordinate Gaussian direction is added to G by this definition.

An all-width exact identity can be tested at a sufficiently large width divisible by the finite central-phase order. At such a width the projective group elements are linearly independent by the retained finite-group tensor Gram theorem: distinct projective unitary matrices have normalized trace inner product strictly below one in modulus, so sufficiently high tensor powers have strictly diagonally dominant Gram matrix. The central phase has first been made trivial by the divisible-width choice; it is not discarded in an arbitrary physical representation. This converts the actual all-width identity to the group algebra of the projective group at that width. Then its ordinary Pauli normal subgroup may legitimately be quotiented as a group algebra. The resulting group is

    (S3)^d ⋊ S_d,

acting by permuting the three Pauli axes X,Y,Z within each coordinate and permuting coordinates. Actual ordinary H maps into the subgroup preserving the d distinguished Z axes as a set. Ordinary units remain units under the quotient: a represented ordinary unit has an inverse in the finite ordinary algebra by Cayley–Hamilton, and faithfulness transfers the inverse identity before taking the quotient.

This reduction is an obstruction argument. It supplies no physical Pauli quotient, no free ordinary inverse implementation, and no small-width independence assertion. Width-dependent scalar coefficients are allowed: choose any sufficiently large permitted width at which the stated exact caller and its ordinary units exist.

## 2. A signed permutation representation on 3d local Pauli axes

Let Ω={(i,p):1≤i≤d, p∈{X,Y,Z}}. Let ρ be the permutation representation of the quotient group on C^Ω. Define the diagonal involution J by

    J e_(i,Z)=+e_(i,Z),
    J e_(i,X)=−e_(i,X),   J e_(i,Y)=−e_(i,Y).

Set ρ0(g)=ρ(g) and ρ1(g)=Jρ(g)J. The two representations agree on every ordinary H element because H preserves the distinguished Z set. Consequently they agree on its entire group algebra and on every ordinary role encoding matrix.

For a monomial permutation matrix R, the rank of R−JRJ equals the number of basis vectors for which the source and image signs differ. For a local Clifford wreath element g, every coordinate whose local Clifford moves Z to X or Y contributes exactly two such sign crossings; a local Clifford preserving Z contributes zero. Coordinate permutations merely reorder these contributions. A one-coordinate Clifford is ordinary exactly when it preserves the Z axis; otherwise its computational matrix has Gaussian rank one. Gaussian ranks add under tensor product. Therefore

    rank(ρ0(g)−ρ1(g))=2 r(g).                         (1)

This includes arbitrary local Clifford products and coordinate permutations, not just powers of B. At d=3 it gives the order-nine power pattern (0,1,2,3,3,3,3,2,1) automatically.

## 3. Every native pays four difference ranks

Put X=ρ0(A), Y=ρ1(A), E=X−Y. Since r(A)=1, rank E=2. An ordinary-cross native has block difference diag(E,E), rank four. A square-cross native has difference

    [[E,c(X²−Y²)],[0,E]],

for a fixed nonzero field scalar c. The identity X²−Y²=XE+EY and invertible block triangular row/column operations reduce this to diag(E,E), again rank four. For an inverse native, N0^-1−N1^-1=−N0^-1(N0−N1)N1^-1, so its difference rank is also four. Unaffected retained roles add zero blocks.

A one-bank query or distinct-bank additive shear with rank-r coefficient has difference rank 2r by (1). For any product of invertible source/ordinary/query matrices, telescoping the difference and subadditivity bound the final rank by the sum of these individual difference ranks. Ordinary stages contribute zero. Independent Gaussian endpoint jobs of ranks h1,…,hR have final difference rank 2Σh_j, and ordinary outside encodings preserve that rank. Thus every such caller obeys the exact universal inequality

    H:=Σ h_j ≤ 2m + Σ_(queries q) r_q.                 (2)

All orientations, signs, gate orders, arbitrary finite inventories, independent dirty roles, and ordinary algebra coefficients in the specified family are covered. This is a mathematical proof, not an inference from a finite synthesis search.

## 4. Complete accounting implications and exact scope

For native-only common-body callers, m sources introduce seven fresh body axes each. On R retained roles the full Gaussian invoice is

    P=m[1]+m(R−1)[7]+m[8],       [r]=r^t, 0<t<1.

All m(R−2) rank-seven idles are included. Write δ=[8]−[7], so 0<δ<1. Concavity and subadditivity give

    Σ_j[7m+h_j] ≤ Rm[7]+δH.

Indeed [7m]≤m[7], and each integer head increment at starting point7m has size at mostδ. Combining H≤2m with P=Rm[7]+m(1+δ) gives

    P−target ≥ m(1−δ)>0.                              (3)

Thus every native-only inventory in this family fails for every 0<t<1, at every fixed d. Ordinary/layout/compiler/precision/recursive expenses only increase its cost. Spectator volume multipliers remain common and positive.

With paid queries, (2) still holds for every fixed d. For the actual three-head source every query rank is at most three. Its full price includes Σr_q^t, and the same argument gives

    P−target ≥ m(1−δ)+Σ_q(r_q^t−δ r_q)>0,

because δ<t7^(t−1)<r^(t−1) for r∈{1,2,3}. No corresponding all-d query-price conclusion is asserted from this inequality alone when arbitrarily larger query ranks are allowed.

This theorem does **not** cover full ordinary Clifford transport. A coordinate CNOT maps a local Pauli axis such as X_i to a two-coordinate Pauli product, and a CZ does likewise. They do not act on Ω by permutation, so the two representations above do not supply an agreeing ordinary interface for them. Such entangling monomial maps are legitimate ordinary operations in the wider programme and are the next constructive direction. A new caller must charge their real layout/phase cost and keep every prior generic rank/query-count obstruction visible.

# One ordinary coefficient can lose its chart equality

In the actual #7830 scalar word, deleting any one of the **220,217** ordinary nonzero-read cochart equalities still leaves a native moment of at least **9589.81296** at exponent 199/200, with the same 5,707 paid slots and public endpoint frames. The caller admits less than 9587.42447841. Coefficients can vary arbitrarily and any extra ordinary equalities may be imposed. The theorem requires the remaining equalities. It also requires fixed paid connections and their charges, helper credits and outer caller. These premises exclude a cascading dead-operation removal.

Make a graph with a separate token for each form and for each arrival. An ordinary nonzero operand requires its two tokens to share a Clifford frame. An equality outside the 3,623 graph bridges cannot affect the component labels. Among the bridges, 212 leave public pins on both sides with the same prescribed label. A further 2,029 detach an unpinned component incident to exactly one paid slot. Giving that component its old neighbor's label changes at most one paid price by at most 9, so its cost is at least 9598.81296 − 9. This argument also allows independent changes at all other components.

For each of the remaining 1,382 bridges, split its two equality components, retain all original endpoint pins and all 5,707 paid slot identities, and separately solve the nine weighted flow problems on the new graph. Divide each old aggregate capacity by its multiplicity and round down, then check sorted capacity sums by exact integer powers for every individual paid slot. Pinned vertices are contracted into two terminal sides for each flag, and all flow values are integers. The least of these 1,382 certificates is **9592.85023**, attained at deletion of the 91018 operand from form 98212.

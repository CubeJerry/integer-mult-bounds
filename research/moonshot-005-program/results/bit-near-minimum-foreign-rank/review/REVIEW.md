## 1. The rectangular factor argument has the claimed scope

Let A be any fitting with identity diagonal blocks, proper native support and rank `9b+h`. Eight mutually noncontact point roles give an identity principal block of rank 8b. In a full rank factorization `A=C R`, their row and column factors form mutually dual coordinate projections and inclusions. The factor space splits into eight b-dimensional point blocks and a complementary space of dimension b+h. This normalization is valid without symmetry, commutativity, or invertibility of an individual foreign block.

An edge role having exactly two contacts with the frame has factors supported on those two point blocks and the complement. Two edge roles sharing a point have a prescribed zero mutual block. Thus, on the point-plus-complement space, the product of the stacked edge rows and concatenated edge columns is block diagonal with blocks gamma_e(a). Write G_a for the sum of their ranks.

For completeness, if the stacked row/column ranks are l and p, and the individual row/column ranks at e are lambda and mu, the individual product-kernel inclusions give

`lambda-g_e(a) <= l-G_a`, and `mu-g_e(a) <= p-G_a`.

Sylvester's inequality through a space of dimension `2b+h` gives `l+p <= 2b+h+G_a`. The edge diagonal equation gives `alpha_e(a)=I-gamma_e(b)`, so each of lambda and mu is at least `b-g_e(b)`. Combining these inequalities yields

`G_a <= h+2(g_e(a)+g_e(b))`.

No square or invertible Schur factor has been assumed. This is exactly the inherited LR inequality, here applied to a rectangular complementary space. A selected K6 or K7 of edge roles remains inside the eight-point factorization. Selecting fewer edges does not replace b+h by an incorrectly smaller or uncharged complement.

Both point-edge contact blocks have rank at least `rank alpha_e(a)`, hence their rank deficiencies are bounded by **g_e(b), at the opposite endpoint**. The reviewer independently checks this index in all eight residual duals. Using g_e(a) would not be justified by the displayed diagonal equation.

The all-width argument is sound for the stated model: integer XOR expansion modulo4 gives exactly the quadratic phase module with arbitrary constant/linear coefficients and even pair coefficients. Restriction of genuine characters is onto the16 affine functions because the four relevant binary directions are independent. Conversely2xy=x+y-(x xor y), so every module element is realized by finitely many actual columns. Thus a finite family exhausts every width; a width cutoff is not used to infer the theorem.

The two-qubit proof supplies more than the finite census. The phase difference between halves is constant plus an even linear form. Squaring the8-dimensional block reduces to two conjugate copies of a4-dimensional Clifford operator T. The binary Pauli action of T has order1,2 or3 from the exact eight-case table. A unitary acting trivially on Pauli labels is phase times Pauli: matching conjugation signs removes a Pauli, and the remaining matrix commutes with all translations and coordinate signs, hence is scalar. A Gaussian dyadic scalar of modulus1 is a Gaussian unit by the reduced numerator sum-of-squares argument. Therefore T^24=I and A^48=I. The denominator lemma also passes. Combining this with Gaussian dyadic coefficients bounds reduced denominators by2. This is a statement about Fourier-character matrices, not a constant-cost implementation of their dependence on all input frequencies.

For the old normalizer, conjugating Lambda(QR)^-1 by Lambda gives R J. Two involutions R,J have involutive product exactly when they commute.

## Proof without a common-demix assumption

Separate the receiver state into (i) propagation of its initial vector
y+Kx and (ii) increments from the pure-X caches. The final homogeneous
receiver map on arbitrary y is I, so part(i) finishes as y+Kx. Part(ii)
must therefore supply (K+I)x. This uses linearity and the same propagation
of the initial y and Kx parts. Merely claiming an arbitrary distributed
packet schedule has rank9 is insufficient; it must meet this interface.

Follow each physical public receiver record to its final facet, treating
whole-(chart,value) SWAP as a relabelling of that trajectory. Distinct
physical trajectories have distinct final receiver facets. At any point
on a trajectory at chart C, all contributions from earlier local X-cache
reads that have reached it are supported on source lines contained in C:
the cache read is local and every subsequent information path is upward.
This applies to cache contributions passed through other public receivers
as well. The initial nonlocal Kx part was separated and is not incorrectly
subjected to this support invariant.

Take an output i with a last incoming operation mixing another public
record into it, at chart C. Let h!=i be the final facet assigned to one of
the other physical records in that operation. Both trajectories pass C,
so C is contained in F_i intersect F_h. Every local cache contribution
acquired before or at that operation vanishes at the ports S_i minus S_h.
After that operation, the output has no further incoming public-record
mixing. Its later local corrections come from a single nested cache chain
inside F_i; self operations and outgoing reads do not enlarge the set of
available input ports. That chain can expose at most18 contact ports,
fewer than the at-least21 private ports it must supply. At a missing port
the desired correction block is I_V, while every available block is zero.
This is a contradiction for every V and every k.

If an output has no incoming public-record mixing, all its correction
reads lie on one chain and the preceding single-chain obstruction applies.
Thus arbitrary sequences of public-only coding, rather than just one
common demix, are excluded. The argument does not require scalar packet
rows or commuting operator coefficients. It concerns support zeros.

## Sharp price for arbitrary linear terminal postprocessing

All 400 terminal register contents y can vary independently because L is invertible. Fix a terminal chart a and set all initial terminal registers outside a to zero. The new outputs required outside a have dimension `rank C[outside a,a]`. In any interactive linear repair, all dependence of the exterior on the fresh a-registers passes through the scalars transmitted out of a. Local auxiliary operations can preserve earlier information and relay messages, but each charged scalar transmission out of a can increase its exported rank by at most one. Sum this cut over the terminal charts. Each transmission has only one source and every positive transfer costs at least one unit of the moment. The exact ranks are 96 at the hub, one each at 96 peripheral charts and zero at eight. Hence the tariff of any such terminal postprocessor is at least 192, with equality in the emitted word. The sum of incoming ranks is 178 and gives a strictly weaker certificate. The statement allows arbitrary intermediate relays and ordinary local linear basis changes while holding the original rail and its terminal stocks fixed.

Even granting arbitrary invertible *birth-chart* mixing before L leaves a lower bound of 170 subsequent received packets. At a peripheral final chart b, collect birth charts from which every demanded E row at b vanishes. This entire foreign-coordinate block remains foreign under an independent invertible change at each birth chart. Its rank inside the old b stock is one at 96 peripheral charts. Those charts each require one incoming direction, because the desired local space has its full terminal dimension and intersects the old local space in codimension at least one. The hub’s desired rows have rank 104 when restricted to the 200 zero-chart inputs, while its actual L rows have rank 30 there. Local birth mixing preserves rank 30 on that coordinate block. Each received scalar increases the available projected rank by at most one. This gives 74 additional incoming packets at the hub, hence 170 in total.

# Independent review of the identical-scalar-stripe port bound

**Accepted in the stated construction class.** The bound applies to
donor-specific scalar quotient rows identically repeated across all bundle
coordinates, on the pinned balanced routes. It permits arbitrary
deterministic downstream functions. It does not exclude general array maps,
varying scalar stripes or full-bank donor basis changes outside that class.

## Why the common-function argument is valid

Fix a joint-tight receiver and one ordinary plane. Fix the other fifteen
quotients of each donor on that plane. The balanced quotient theorem makes
the remaining source coordinates independently variable. Their only varying
path to this receiver crosses its fifteen actual plane-to-D4 ports, whose
total alphabet has exactly the size of the required plane contribution.
Correct recovery therefore forces the complete port tuple to be a bijective
relabelling of that contribution. Each port has its full positive alphabet
and is a function of the contribution alone. The plane sees no unlisted
source input on the specified canonical-zero slice.

This conclusion concerns the whole domain of the plane's quotient inputs:
each quotient ranges freely, and the plane encoders are functions of those
quotients, not of the other donor coordinates which were fixed during the
argument. Arbitrary nonlinear relabellings do not invalidate it.

For two receivers, one shared port is invariant under the kernel of each
target map, hence under their sum. The corresponding common row space is
the intersection. Two distinct scalar rows repeated on the same p-coordinate
bundle have zero intersection; a common function is then constant. This
contradicts the port's full positive range. Thus the two target spaces agree.
Connectivity propagates that equality, and all covered ports factor through
one p-dimensional aggregate. Uncovered ports are granted their entire raw
capacity, so the final upper bound is conservative.

The argument counts the actual early and late records once. It neither
duplicates a port's earlier state for free nor turns a rank bound into an
unjustified movement charge. Same-chart caches remain functions of the
counted incoming data.

## Native geometry and the total cut

The independent checker computes native orthogonality by integer dot
products, source capacities directly from the balancing factor, and port
visibility by inclusion of root sets. It uses union-find over port
neighborhoods, rather than the author's receiver-edge tree construction.
All 1,108 ordinary shared-port graphs are connected. Every published
spanning-tree edge is separately checked against its claimed D4 witness.
The uncovered-port histogram is 834 planes with zero, 260 with five,
four with nine, and ten with ten: 1,436 ports total.

Consequently the complete plane-output alphabet is at most
`|F|^(1108*360r + 1436*24r + 11*540r) = |F|^(439284r)`.
The eleven singleton planes are allowed their entire source alphabets;
the empty plane contributes no varying source input.

The canonical map has rank nine. On its kernel, with all Y inputs zero,
the desired native output equals X because K=I+O. It must therefore
distinguish `|F|^(102*5760r) = |F|^(587520r)` inputs. All other varying
information reaching receivers crosses the counted plane boundary.
The smaller output alphabet gives the strict deficit `148236r`, at every
positive r and over every finite characteristic-two field.

## Controls and remaining scope

The independent checker examines all 196 ordered pairs of one- and
two-dimensional subspaces of F2³, constructs their common-function
equivalence classes, and compares the class count to the row-space
intersection. It retains an intermediate array intersection and a
disconnected private-port example as controls against overextending the
collapse argument. The written cardinality proof, not the finite controls,
establishes its arbitrary-width and nonlinear scope.

An actual array code can have intermediate target-space intersections and
is not excluded. The native four- and seven-donor fragments pay different
private ports and are not counterexamples to the theorem. No complete
supplier, multiplication exponent, or foundational discharge follows.

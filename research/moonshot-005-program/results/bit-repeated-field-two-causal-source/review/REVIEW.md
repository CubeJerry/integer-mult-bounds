## 1. Local saturation and the distribution used for entropy

The independently reviewed binary predecessor supplies the full-range shared
port argument. The fifteen visible ports at a jointly tight receiver have
exactly the target alphabet size. Their complete tuple is therefore a
bijective relabelling of the target and ranges over the full physical product
alphabet. Shared subtuples have their full product range. This is stronger
than full marginal range and is the fact needed to force nonzero pairwise
base intersections.

The canonical-zero construction and the entropy calculation serve distinct
purposes. Restriction of the rank-nine canonical code outside a receiver's
foreign set is surjective, so arbitrary foreign inputs can be extended to
make the canonical values zero. This establishes the local functional
identities for **every** local quotient tuple. After those identities have
been established, their entropy can be evaluated under independent uniform
local quotient inputs. Those are also the marginal distribution induced by
independent uniform original donor banks. There is no assumption that the
entire collection of original donors remains independent when globally
conditioned on the canonical kernel.

Under the stated identical repetition, the target at a receiver is a graph
plane tensored with the common repetition space. Pairwise intersection of
graph planes holds over every coefficient field. The elementary star-or-top
argument is field independent: a pairwise-intersecting family either shares
one line or lies in a three-dimensional span. Every donor projection of a
true top has rank two, because the top contains a graph plane.

## 2. Early preservation and the absence of a bypass

The original balanced programme has a square genuine early source tuple.
It restores all original X at Hub before any original Y leaves ground. Its
allowed early source map is invertible; in particular uniformly distributed
original X makes the full early tuple uniformly distributed over its product
alphabet. Early tuples at different planes are independent.

At an ordinary H, each of its m native donors contributes
`a=xr/4` early values. Its forty-five actual H-to-D exits have
`e=mxr/180` early values each, so their total capacity is exactly ma.
All informative exits from this early H step go through those ports.
Short public closures occur after that boundary, and a stationary snapshot
at H has no path to Hub before restoration unless an additional informative
route is introduced. Later Y operations cannot restore an early original
whose information was already discarded. Hence the H output tuple must
retain ma field information dimensions.

This is the retained source and chronology contract, not a claim about a
source permitted an additional direct H-to-Hub escape, a source permitted to
reconstruct originals from a new ground bank, or a source with a changed
restoration time. Such changes require another argument.

## 3. The common-function information inequality is valid

For a hypothetical top, let T be its 3r-dimensional linear target and let
Z_j be independent uniform donor quotient values, each of dimension 2r.
The actual early donor tuple E_j is a function of Z_j with entropy at most a.
The aggregate covered early output Y is both a function of T and a function
of E. Therefore

`H(Y) <= I(T;E)`.

For any donor j, the chain rule and deterministic data processing give

`I(T;E) <= I(T;E_-j)+H(E_j)
         <= I(T;Z_-j)+a
         = r+a`.

The equality uses `H(T)=3r` and `H(T|Z_-j)=2r`; each top projection to the
chosen donor has rank 2r. No linearity of E_j or Y is used. Granting all u
uncovered early ports their full ue capacity yields the necessary inequality

`ma <= r+a+ue`, or `45(m-1)x-u*m*x <= 180`.

The independent checker reconstructs every ordinary H from the integer root
table and matches its complete visible-port incidence to the admitted color
certificate. All 1,108 strict reverse inequalities hold. The minimum margin
`45(m-1)x-u*m*x-180` is 30, attained at twenty-three planes. Thus every
ordinary target family is a star.

For a separate finite control, the review uses the binary three-space

`T=(x0+x2, x1+x4, x3+x5)`

on three independent two-bit donors. It enumerates all 4,096 triples of
one-bit Boolean early encoders. Exact integer products verify both the
mutual-information upper bound and the bound on the largest deterministic
common function. The latter is computed with connected components of the
bipartite support graph, without importing the author's implementation.
This particular control is not sharp; its lack of equality cases is not
used to strengthen the theorem. A future-input control fails the required
early factorization.

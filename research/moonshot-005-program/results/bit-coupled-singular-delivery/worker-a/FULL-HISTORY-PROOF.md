## 2. A cyclic acquisition lemma

Let \(F_0,\ldots,F_{k-1}\) be independent queried facets, in a chosen cyclic
order. An input belongs to the pattern \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) when
its native line lies precisely in the queried facets indexed by \(J\).
Different nonempty patterns are disjoint source cohorts. Put
\(C_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}F_j\).

At \(C_J\), one genuine original of the cohort can receive additions from all
other originals in that cohort. It then carries the pure cohort aggregate
\(z_J\); the other originals proceed to Hub. If \(J\) is one consecutive
cyclic run \((i,\ldots,j)\), that aggregate carrier can follow

\[
 C_J\subset C_{J\setminus\{i\}}\subset\cdots\subset F_j\subset\mathrm{Hub}.
\]

It deposits \(z_J\) in an actual zero-born cache at every visited suffix chart.
This reaches the flag of each requested packet exactly once: packet \(i\)
descends through the successive prefix intersections starting at \(F_i\).
At Hub the carrier is restored by adding the other, already pure cohort
originals again. This is a sequence of elementary invertible ADDs, not an
assumed pure-sum overwrite.

If \(J\) has two cyclic runs, one extra original donor can receive \(z_J\)
at \(C_J\), retain its own mask \(x_d\), and serve the second run while the
cohort carrier serves the first. The donor carries \(z_J+x_d\), not a falsely
asserted pure aggregate. A genuine copy of \(x_d\), captured before recoding
at a chart visited later by the appropriate descending packet, removes that
mask. At Hub, first restore the cohort carrier, then add all pure cohort
originals to the donor to restore \(x_d\). Every addition and cache is listed
in the word.

For three facets every nonempty pattern is one cyclic run. For four facets
the only two-run patterns are \(\{0,2\}\) and \(\{1,3\}\). The following
construction uses exactly two donors for these two exceptional patterns.

## A general coupling lemma

Suppose a literal linear source word has a retained owned row g at a proper chart C, with that value stable throughout the uses below. A second packet P visits C and later reaches a nested chart D. Between C and its first capture/reset at D, P only receives ADDs from fields independent of P and unchanged by the modification. At D it copies its value a into a zero-born owner K and resets by adding K back into P. Between this capture and retirement, K is not modified and is read only for that reset and by a specified set of receivers. Suppose each of those receivers reads K exactly once and also visits C while g remains available; all other dependencies and source lifetimes are unchanged.

Add g into P at C, and add g into each such receiver during its existing C visit. The value of P at D becomes a+g. The capture puts a+g into K, and the following reset gives zero exactly as before. All later packet values therefore agree with the original word. Every affected receiver gets the same extra g once from K and once from the corrective read at C, so its terminal value is unchanged. Other receivers and all originals are unchanged.

Thus, for any actual retained g satisfying those custody and chronology conditions, coupling costs exactly one packet ADD plus one corrective ADD per affected receiver. It introduces no movement, new field or retirement. The statement does not grant an arbitrary g for free: its actual source acquisition, storage and original lifetime remain necessary premises.

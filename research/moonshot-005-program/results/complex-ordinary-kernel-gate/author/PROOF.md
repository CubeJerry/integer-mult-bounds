# An ordinary-kernel obstruction for collective common-head callers

**Proved within the fixed G192/H24 interface.** If a caller uses m at least one native halves, z odd Z-type rank-two queries and x odd rank-one queries, and produces R independent rank-two Gaussian heads of which k are Z-type, then

\[
 R\le 2m+z+x,\qquad
 R\le 2m+z+x-2+k. \tag{1}
\]

No new Gaussian supplier or multiplication exponent is established.

## 2. Parity and the rank components

Modulo the four translations, G is the signed-permutation group
\((\mathbb F_2)^3\rtimes S_3\), with Qa,Qb,Qc corresponding to the three sign generators. Define its parity character
\[
 \eta(g)=(-1)^{\operatorname{wt}(x(g))}.
\]
Then \(\eta\) is one on H. It is negative on every physical rank-one Gaussian and on the central-Z rank-two double coset. It is positive on every nonalternating rank-two Gaussian.

Extend
\[
 \theta(g)=\eta(g)g
\]
complex linearly and entrywise to role matrices. This is an involutive algebra automorphism, and it fixes every ordinary matrix.

Let \(E_r\) be the linear span of Gaussian group elements of physical rank r, for r=0,1,2. The admitted rank-component lemma gives
\[
 \mathcal A=E_0\oplus E_1\oplus E_2,
 \qquad E_0=\mathcal B,
\]
and each \(E_r\) is a left and right \(\mathcal B\)-module. The sum is a literal direct sum of group-basis spans; faithfulness makes it a direct sum of actual operators too.

If a full caller M has an endpoint of R independent rank-two Gaussian jobs up to invertible ordinary input/output encodings, then
\[
 M\in\operatorname{Mat}_R(E_2),\qquad M\text{ is invertible}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, ordinary multiplication and its inverse preserve E2. A mathematical inverse belongs to the finite-dimensional ordinary algebra whenever its faithful image is invertible. Its physical implementation remains a separate cost requirement.

If exactly k endpoint jobs are Z-type, then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M-\theta(M))=kD. \tag{4}
\]
The equality follows by applying the ordinary outer encodings: the difference becomes block diagonal, with k invertible blocks twice the corresponding Gaussian and R-k zero blocks.

## 3. Gate kernels are ordinary coordinate constraints

For each nonordinary gate F_j let C_j select certain input roles:

- For a native half, C_j selects its two active roles.
- For an in-place odd query, C_j selects that role.
- For a distinct-role odd shear, C_j selects its source role.

In every case,
\[
 \ker(F_j-\theta(F_j))=\ker C_j, \tag{5}
\]
and both F_j and \(\theta(F_j)\) act as identity on this kernel.

For a native, \(\eta(A)=-1\), while \(\sigma+\epsilon\) is even. Consequently its difference is
\[
 N_{\sigma,\epsilon,c}-\theta(N_{\sigma,\epsilon,c})
 =\begin{pmatrix}2A^\sigma&0\\0&2A^\sigma\end{pmatrix}
\]
on the active pair, and zero on the remaining roles. It has rank2D and precisely the claimed coordinate kernel. The original native and its theta image both fix all inactive-role vectors.

For an in-place odd query, the difference is one invertible block2g, of rankD. For an odd shear, the difference is the single block2cg in its source-to-destination position, again of rankD. Both gates fix every vector whose selected source coordinate is zero. These statements do not assume that a shear is free merely because its determinant is one.

The identity-on-kernel conclusion is essential. A theta-even nonalternating rank-two query generally has zero difference but is not identity on its entire kernel. Such a gate is expressly excluded from this theorem's query inventory.

## 4. Telescoping with actual prefixes and theta suffixes

Write the chronological circuit as
\[
 M=O_LF_LO_{L-1}F_{L-1}\cdots O_1F_1O_0,
 \tag{6}
\]
where all O_j are invertible ordinary matrices, and the L=m+x+z gates F_j are the permitted nonordinary gates. Ordinary source adapters are absorbed into these O_j.

Let
\[
 P_{j-1}=O_{j-1}F_{j-1}\cdots O_1F_1O_0
\]
be the **actual** prefix before F_j, with P_0=O_0. Define
\[
 T_j=
 \theta(O_LF_L\cdots O_{j+1}F_{j+1})\,
 O_j(F_j-\theta(F_j))P_{j-1}. \tag{7}
\]
For j=L the theta suffix is identity. The exact telescoping identity is
\[
 M-\theta(M)=\sum_{j=1}^L T_j. \tag{8}
\]
All factors on either side of the gate difference in (7) are invertible. Thus T_j has rank2D for a native and rankD for an odd query.

Put
\[
 K=\bigcap_{j=1}^L\ker T_j. \tag{9}
\]
The next fact is stronger than a rank count: K is cut out by ordinary operators alone.

Define the ordinary prefix
\[
 U_{j-1}=O_{j-1}O_{j-2}\cdots O_0.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{K=\bigcap_{j=1}^L\ker(C_jU_{j-1}).} \tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** At j=1, all left factors in T_1 are invertible, so the first constraint is precisely C_1O_0v=0. On that kernel F_1 acts as identity. Therefore the actual vector arriving at the next ordinary operation is exactly O_0v.

Inductively, suppose all constraints through j-1 hold. Every earlier nonordinary gate has fixed the vector it received. Hence the actual prefix P_{j-1}v equals U_{j-1}v. By (5), the jth kernel constraint becomes C_jU_{j-1}v=0, and F_j fixes that vector too. This proves equivalence of the two intersections at every step. QED.

The same induction shows that
\[
 Mv=O_LO_{L-1}\cdots O_0v\quad\text{for every }v\in K. \tag{11}
\]
No commuting assumption on the ordinary interleavings or the Gaussian gates is used.

## 5. A pure rank-two endpoint has no such kernel

Stack the ordinary matrices C_jU_{j-1} into a rectangular block matrix B. Then K is its kernel. Because H is a finite unitary group, \(\operatorname{Mat}_R(\mathcal B)\) is a finite-dimensional unital star algebra. In particular, \(B^*B\) lies in that algebra.

Let \(\Pi\) be the orthogonal projection onto K. It lies in the same ordinary algebra. One explicit proof takes the finite set of eigenvalues of the positive semidefinite matrix \(B^*B\) and chooses a polynomial p with p(0)=1 and p(lambda)=0 at every positive eigenvalue. Then
\[
 \Pi=p(B^*B)\in\operatorname{Mat}_R(\mathcal B).
\]
If zero is not an eigenvalue, the projection is zero; if B is zero, it is identity. These cases are included.

Equation(11) gives
\[
 M\Pi=(O_LO_{L-1}\cdots O_0)\Pi
 \in\operatorname{Mat}_R(E_0). \tag{12}
\]
Equation(3) and the right ordinary-module property give
\[
 M\Pi\in\operatorname{Mat}_R(E_2). \tag{13}
\]
The intersection of these two subspaces is zero. Hence M Pi=0. Since M is invertible, Pi=0 and
\[
 \boxed{K=0.} \tag{14}
\]

This projection is a mathematical witness used for a necessary condition. The proof does not add a free physical projector, a destructive operation, or an unpriced basis change to the caller.

## 6. Rank budget and the deleted-native improvement

From K=0 and (9),
\[
 RD=\operatorname{codim}K
 \le\sum_j\operatorname{rank}T_j
 =(2m+x+z)D.
\]
This proves the first inequality in (1).

Because m is at least one, choose a native index j0. Let Delta=M-theta(M). The sum identity(8) implies
\[
 K=\ker\Delta\;\cap\!
 \bigcap_{j\ne j_0}\ker T_j. \tag{15}
\]
For the reverse inclusion, every term except T_j0 vanishes on the vector, and Delta also vanishes, forcing T_j0 to vanish. No independence or generic-position assumption is needed.

Using (4), the rank of the deleted native, and subadditivity of codimension yields
\[
 RD\le kD+(2m+x+z-2)D.
\]
Dividing by positive D proves the second inequality.

The result is universal over arbitrary finite ordinary interleavings in the stated algebra and every allowed query placement. It is not the outcome of a finite synthesis search.

## 7. A concrete excluded changed-query inventory

The signed-permutation difference-rank gate leaves one native half, one odd Z-type query and one odd rank-one query numerically possible for three nonalternating rank-two heads. Here
\[
 m=1,\quad z=x=1,\quad R=3,\quad k=0.
\]
The new inequality requires
\[
 3\le 2+1+1-2=2,
\]
a contradiction. Thus that changed-query caller is also excluded inside this fixed algebra, despite its positive optimistic raw Gaussian price.

This does not retroactively change the earlier rank gate's valid statement that the inventory survived that particular invariant. The new theorem supplies the missing obstruction.

The theorem does not by itself close inventories containing theta-even rank-two queries, larger ordinary coordinate-phase algebras, altered source heads, or non-Gaussian endpoints. Those are separate possible research directions and must retain complete physical and recursive accounting.

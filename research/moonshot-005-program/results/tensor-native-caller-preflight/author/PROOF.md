# Tensor decoder density does not pay the retained caller

## Status and purpose

**Proved within the stated caller and route interfaces:** at `t=199/200`,
the pinned eighteen-stage caller cannot use a native geometry of ambient
dimension `h>=38`, even if the decoder, excess helper movement, local
correction, cleanup and ordinary work are granted zero net cost. This is a
failure of this caller's raw moment condition, not a lower bound for all
multiplication algorithms or all possible callers.

**Proved conditional arithmetic obstruction:** in the explicit same-pattern
even-stage invoice family below, the direct P104 tensor-square row decoder
fails at every positive even stage count. At `s>=4`, even a free decoder
fails. At `s=2`, a different decoder would have to reduce its moment by more
than 34.0073504654% before paying any omitted expense. Neither a legal
two-stage caller nor such a shared-ascent decoder is asserted.

A direct tensor-square BIT fitting with 81 global Hub packets also fails
this invoice family. Its fixed-eighteen-stage rejection does not need that
81-packet assumption. Both strict suppliers and all five foundational
assumptions remain open. **No new kappa.**

## 1. Three dimensions that must remain distinct

The base P104 has 104 odd binary labels spanning `F2^9`, and an ordinary
real Gram decoder of rank eight. Each of its eight rank-one rows has
pairing price seven. Write

    M=sum(k=1..8) w_k u_k^T,
    M_ii=1, M_ij=0 if i!=j and q_i dot q_j=1.

For the square, use `q_(i,j)=q_i tensor q_j`. These are 10816 distinct odd
labels spanning `F2^81`. The ordinary matrix `M tensor M` is a decoder: if
both binary pairings are one and the product ports differ, at least one base
off-diagonal factor is zero. Its 64 displayed rank-one rows each have
pairing rank `7*7=49`, by the rank formula for Kronecker products. Its row
rank mass is 3136, or `(7/13)^2` per port.

This proves an algebraic identity, not a physical source. The ordinary real
Gram dimension is 64, but the binary label dimension is 81. The retained
complex interface uses the latter: X travels from its label line to the
full label space and Y travels from zero to the orthogonal label hyperplane.
Each has rank difference `b=h-1=80`. Substituting real dimension 64 for native
label dimension 81 would change this interface.

For the separate BIT lead, tensor the verified 111 real source lines in
dimension 8. There are `111^2=12321` product lines spanning dimension 64. If K
is the retained binary fitting, with diagonal 1 and off-diagonal support on
real-orthogonal pairs, `K tensor K` is a fitting for the product lines:
every nonzero off-diagonal product coefficient contains a differing
orthogonal factor. Its rank is 81 over every characteristic-two field. A
direct Hub-to-ground realization with one independent V-packet per rank
direction uses 81 rank-64 descents per plane. This is one interface; it is
not claimed necessary for every product-line fitting.

The BIT observation inherits the programme's verified native rank/contact
facts. It does not treat division by four in the complex matrix as a legal
characteristic-two operation. Both suppliers need separate source proofs.

## 2. Favorable route bounds

For `0<t<1`, a register taking nested elementary moves of positive ranks
`d_1,...,d_l` and having endpoint dimension difference b costs at least b^t:

    sum d_j^t >= (sum d_j)^t >= b^t.

Turns are allowed. This is subadditivity and total rank variation. We grant
one cheapest jump even where intermediate contacts would prohibit it.

Use the retained helper grammar: an independent helper's positive productive
boundary rank r is its nested endpoint dimension difference. Its own paid
MOVE moment is at least r^t. Thus paid helper movement minus productive
helper credit is nonnegative; we grant zero. Causal splitting into fresh
independent helper segments obeys the same statement segment by segment.
This does not extend the grammar to a new helper boundary with unproved
rank pricing.

For private decoder movement we consider two favorable lower bounds:

- **Free decoder:** zero, regardless of source realization.
- **Direct row decoder:** each row is a distinct owned chain. If its price
  is c_k, that chain costs at least c_k^t. Set `R_t=sum c_k^t` and
  `rho=R_t/n`. Two planes cost at least `2R_t` per stage. Shared ascents or
  another source may escape this row premise, but cannot improve on zero.

We grant zero further correction, acquisition, copies, cleanup, scalar work,
restoration, erasure, precision, layout and ordinary cost. These remain real
construction obligations. This is a necessary-condition test, not a complete
invoice for an existing higher-label source.

## 3. Exact eighteen-stage caller and type B

The pinned `source/caller.json` has, per port of either type:

- two productive ranks `1+18b`;
- four productive ranks `1+jb` for each j=1,...,9;
- four paid rank-one QUERY jobs;
- four paid whole idles of rank jb for each j=1,...,8.

A type-A native has four main fields with rank difference b at each stage.
A type-B native has two, and its caller has **36 extra rank-b whole idles**.
Therefore the combined favorable native-public and idle bill agrees:

    18*(4 b^t) = 18*(2 b^t)+36 b^t.

The checker recounts every target mask and QUERY/IDLE occurrence. Merely
counting fewer native main fields would give a false type-B saving.

The negative theorem is conditional on a higher-h native satisfying this
unchanged caller interface. The source formal identity has a head of order
four and independent stage symbols; replacing body width 8 by b changes
job ranks. No higher-h native is thereby constructed.

Its working ambient is `h+18b`, while its largest productive rank is
`1+18b`: for the complex square these are 1521 and 1441, and for the BIT
square 1198 and 1135. A later compiler must keep these quantities distinct.

## 4. Explicit even-stage invoice family

For an integer a>=1, put s=2a. Define the same-pattern family by

    T/n = 2(1+2ab)^t +4 sum(j=1..a)(1+jb)^t,
    P/n >= 4 +4 sum(j=1..a-1)(jb)^t +8a b^t +4a rho.

Let `g_a(b,rho)=T/n-P_lower/n`. For a=9, this is source-bound to the pinned
caller, including type-B cancellation. For other a it is a **hypothetical
invoice family**; no actual caller matrix at that stage count is certified.
An exclusion of this family is not an exclusion of all callers.

The target needs g_a>0 after every omitted cost is restored. Equality is
insufficient for the strict uniform supplier requirements.

## 5. Infinite-stage proof and geometry monotonicity

Set `delta(x)=(x+1)^t-x^t`, positive and strictly decreasing. Rearrange:

    g_a = 2(1+2ab)^t +4(ab)^t
          +4 sum(j=1..a) delta(jb) -4 -8a b^t -4a rho.

Extend this formula by `g_0=-2`. Successive differences g_(a+1)-g_a strictly
decrease with a: both power increments decrease by concavity, the new delta
decreases, and linear terms have constant differences. Hence:

- g_1<0 and g_2-g_1<0 imply g_a<0 for all a>=1;
- g_1<-2=g_0 implies the same without a separate second-value check.

This proves the unbounded-stage assertions; no finite search extrapolation
is used.

Put `f_a(b)=g_a(b,0)`. For real b>0, differentiation and delta'<0 give

    f_a'(b)
    < t b^(t-1)[(2^(t+1)+4)a^t -8a] <0.

The first inequality replaces `(1+2ab)^(t-1)` by the larger
`(2ab)^(t-1)`. The second uses `2^(t+1)+4<8` and a^t<=a. Thus the free
bound strictly decreases in geometry dimension, for every a>=1.

### Stronger saving cannot fix the sign

For 0<t<=1, normalize the free bound by b^t:

    f_a/b^t=2(2a+1/b)^t+4(a+1/b)^t
      +4 sum(j=1..a-1)[(j+1/b)^t-j^t]-4b^(-t)-8a.

This strictly increases with t because `x^t log x` increases in x>=1.
The complex-square additional term is
`-4a*(64/n)*(49/b)^t`, also increasing in t as 49<b. This applies to every
higher complex tensor power as well. A negative value at 199/200 remains
negative for every kappa>=1/200 with 0<t<=199/200.

For direct BIT packets normalize instead by h^t. The owned term becomes
constant; the negative public terms increase with t. Paired target/idle
differences have arguments at least `(h-1)/h>=1/2`; on this range
`d/dx[x^t log x]=x^(t-1)(1+t log x)>0`, since 1-log2>0. Thus the normalized
BIT gap increases in t too. No stronger saving repairs these failures.

## 7. Complete supplier and compiler obligations remain

A real source still needs arbitrary-input endpoints, actual acquisition,
legal charts, junk correction, independent helper return, all owned
lifetimes and erasure, all-width phases, precision and the full ordinary
invoice. None is constructed by favorable row arithmetic.

The complex supplier needs U>.005. The BIT supplier separately needs a
covered C and completed ordinary B>.005, including

    B < min(rho_atom,(1-rho_atom)C+rho_atom*b_old,1-rho_atom).

From b_old=.00385248, a one-stage bridge needs
`C>3112961/621875000=.005005766432160804...`. Finite multistage bridges can
have a smaller strict C>.005, but retain their atom, stock, high-digit and
completed-interface obligations. The failures above occur before these
strict losses.

A fixed larger geometry can change constants without itself prohibiting an
asymptotic improvement. If an actual unit had strict contraction, suitable
fixed compiler parameters could be investigated. Here chronological products
multiply target and paid moments. A raw ratio P_t/T_t>=1 does not become
<1 by taking powers; the source's strictification expressly requires
alpha=P_t/T_t<1. This is not a theorem against every hypothetical compiler.
No compiler/cover/precision allowance is counted as an earned improvement.

## Physical interface and source tightness

The theorem retains a particular source interface. At each ordinary donor,
28 plane messages are functions of that donor's original bank and fixed
initial baselines. No message may acquire an additional independent source
input before emission. Each message has at most Q possible values, where
the bank has Q^16 possible inputs. Early and late coordinates count together
as one plane message. Caches may implement this function, but cannot add
another informative observation or route.

The independent route replay verifies 106,560 distinct base owner routes.
Each ordinary source plane has 30 base field slots across the two phases,
or 240 field coordinates after the actual eight copies. Each ordinary
donor has one complete bank of 480 early base fields. Every proper receiver
sees exactly 16 source planes. H-to-D containment, D/K perpendicular
containment and the native middle complements are checked from the actual
real root geometry, so unseen source planes cannot contribute to that
receiver along the retained monotone proper routes.

Every H has 45 genuine outgoing ports. For each native perpendicular
receiver, exactly 15 ports are visible, each carrying two actual fields
across the chronological phases. Their eight-copy combined capacity is
therefore 240 field coordinates, with alphabet size Q. A stationary copy
or an arbitrary later nonlinear decoder does not enlarge that transcript.
The same ratios hold under positive legal proportional repetition.

The author adds the necessary tightness lemma for arbitrary leaf sources.
Fix every original input except that of an ordinary donor k other than 10.
At a proper receiver i other than 80, the required target contribution is
the identity on that donor's whole original bank. Thus its vector of 16
visible plane messages must be injective: equal message vectors would
force equal later outputs, whereas distinct original inputs require
distinct outputs. The bank has Q^16 elements, and the product of the
16 message alphabets has at most Q^16 elements. Injectivity forces equality
of these cardinalities and a bijection onto the entire product.

This is stronger than a global assumption that some early source change
of basis is reversible. It establishes exactly the local view needed in
the proof. In particular, fixing 15 of the visible messages leaves Q
possible original inputs, and fixing one leaves Q^15. These facts hold
for arbitrary nonlinear separated encoders. They do not assume the two
leaves retain RS coefficients, linear maps or channelwise encodings.

## Why a shared H aligns whole fibre partitions

For two ordinary donors sharing H, choose a nonexceptional receiver
perpendicular to H. Fix the other 15 visible source messages of each
donor and all other original inputs. Let U and V be the remaining
original-input fibres. Source tightness gives |U|=|V|=Q. Their variations
can reach the receiver only through the single combined H cut of
alphabet size Q. The exact target must distinguish every original-coordinate
sum in U+V, hence |U+V| is at most Q and is exactly Q.

The finite-group equality lemma used by the author is correct. For any
v0 in V, the sets U+v all equal U+v0. Consequently V-v0 lies in the
translation stabilizer L of U. Its size is at least Q; a coset u0+L lies
inside U, so its size is at most Q. Both U and V are cosets of this same
subgroup of order Q. No vector-space assumption is needed at this step.

The proof also supplies the essential consistency over all fibres.
Independent source banks make every pair of fibres attainable. Holding
one fibre at the first donor fixed while varying the other forces every
second-donor fibre to have the same stabilizer. Fixing one of those and
varying the first donor does the same there. Thus their **entire** off-H
partitions coincide, as unlabelled coset partitions of the common original
additive bank. The statement is not restricted to one favourable input
or one selected fibre.

The original-coordinate identity blocks are essential to comparing these
partitions directly. The actual target assignment has 123 modified block
locations, all in receiver row 80 or donor column 10. The pinned S028
definition assigns identity to every other foreign block. Every use of
alignment in the certificate avoids the exceptional row and column.

## Propagation to an arbitrary neighbouring quotient

Let j be the only assumed RS anchor, with arbitrary full-bank bijection
psi before its 28 evaluations. For one neighbouring leaf k and their
shared plane H, choose an off-H leaf quotient Q0. Each certified receiver
sees H and Q0. It also sees the claimed anchor plane R, as is checked
directly from geometry; the exact root reflection supplies the same map.

The anchor's off-H fibre at such a receiver is the pullback under psi of
a coset partition with direction span(phi) tensor F^s. The scalar phi is
the actual monic degree-15 polynomial vanishing at the other 15 visible
nodes. The review constructs it by dividing the full 16-node annihilator
by the H factor, then verifies its entire zero set. It uses the pinned
nodes 1 through 28 in actual H order.

All 15 certified directions vanish at R, and their independent rank is
15. They therefore span the full kernel of evaluation at R. The join of
linear coset partitions has cosets of the sum of their direction spaces
as its classes. A bijection preserves such a join, because it preserves
the equivalence relation generated by chains of partition steps. The
join of these pulled-back anchor partitions is consequently exactly the
anchor R-message partition, with every class of size Q^15.

Shared-H alignment identifies each of these off-H partitions with the
corresponding leaf partition. Since the Q0 message is among the fixed
off-H coordinates, it is constant on every such fibre and therefore on
their join. Its own fibres have size Q^15 by the tight-view lemma.
Containment of one Q^15-class in another of the same finite size forces
equality. Thus the leaf Q0 partition is exactly the anchor R partition.

This step is sound even when the anchor bijection mixes every channel,
field component, binary coordinate and repeated copy, and when the leaf
source is nonlinear. It transports an information partition; it does not
claim the leaf's displayed numerical values equal an RS evaluation.

## The final contradiction

Each final receiver sees the plane H3 shared by the two leaves, but is
not perpendicular to the anchor. It therefore sees neither of the two
anchor-edge centres. Its 15 off-H3 quotients at each leaf are all among
the propagated ones; the checker verifies exact coverage, not only a
subset count.

Their partitions are those of 15 distinct anchor evaluations. Intersecting
them gives the pullback under the same psi of a one-dimensional scalar
RS kernel, repeated across all channels. On the two arms, these kernels
are spanned by monic degree-15 polynomials with different 15-node root
sets. They are distinct scalar lines, and the independently computed
rank of the two coefficient rows is two. Their coset partitions differ.
Taking pullbacks under one bijection cannot make different partitions
equal.

The final shared-H3 alignment lemma requires these exact two off-H3
partitions to coincide, producing the contradiction. The argument uses
both joins and intersections of partitions, each of which is preserved
by a bijection. It does not introduce a linearity assumption on psi at
the final step.

The scalar ranks remain unchanged under finite field extension. The
direction dimensions and class cardinalities scale with the positive
number of channels, so the discrepancy survives all stated legal body
repetitions. This is a finite-field argument; no inference from equal
infinite cardinalities is made.

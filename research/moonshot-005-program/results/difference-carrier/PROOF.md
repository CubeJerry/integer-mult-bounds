## 1. Two ADDs preserve exactly the useful difference

At a real common cut let public helper H have actual value h, and let a raw main carrier Q have value q at the same chart A. Execute:

\[
Q\mathrel{-}=H,\qquad H\mathrel{+}=Q.
\]

The resulting pair is

\[
(H,Q)=(q,\delta),\qquad\delta=q-h.
\]

The map is invertible: h=H−Q and q=H. It uses two actual cochart ADDs, no initialized field, no overwrite, no erasure and no transport. The common state has changed by exactly δ in the helper coordinate; the raw carrier retains that same perturbation rather than the negative old helper value.

Suppose the helper's original MOVE can carry q to a location supporting a genuine receiver module. The module may use a single immediate raw-receiver read, a no-write helper segment with its exact nonzero SCALE factors, or a separately proved owned continuation. Its complete instructions and every reservation it consumes must be listed. The existence of a geometric path is not itself a receiver module.

## 2. Exact terminal restoration

Let W be the original common public endpoint map. Let z be the endpoint response of the native suffix to a unit perturbation of H at the loan cut, holding every other live cut coordinate fixed. After running the complete common suffix the actual public outputs are

\[
Y=Wu+z\delta.
\]

Assume:

1. every nonzero z_i has final chart T;
2. the raw donor follows its remaining original funded itinerary to T carrying δ;
3. the unperturbed helper-cut row h=a u is recoverable at T: a=λW for a row λ supported only on public outputs physically at T;
4. the perturbed suffix satisfies every operation precondition, including the zero-destination requirement of COPY.

For each nonzero z_i execute `Y_i -= z_i Q` at T. Every common output is now exactly Wu. Then execute `Q += λ_i Y_i` for each nonzero λ_i, using the **restored** common outputs. The raw donor becomes

\[
Q=\delta+\lambda Wu=q-h+h=q.
\]

No nonzero γ, matrix inverse, extra field or SCALE is needed. In particular z=0 is allowed: q remains recoverable because δ was retained in Q. The order matters: reconstruct h only after the endpoint corrections unless the certificate separately compensates for the perturbation in those reads.

If the delivery module is one direct addition into a raw receiver, the extra instruction count is exactly

\[
2+1+|\operatorname{supp}z|+|\operatorname{supp}\lambda|
\]

ADDs. There are no additional MOVEs, INITs, CLEARs or SCALEs. The complete one-loan word includes all 71,261 common instructions, all 1,710 raw-main itinerary MOVEs, all 400 initial difference ADDs and all 400 final recoupling ADDs, plus those stated additions. A selected recipient is fully correct only when its entire required difference endpoint is the implemented shear. This does not complete the other raw endpoints.

## 3. Same-cut compensators

After the two-ADD loan, δ is physically present in Q at A. For any other live common field i at A, a further instruction `common[i] += α_i Q` injects α_iδ. The loaned helper already has coefficient α_H=1. Hence the cut perturbation is αδ, supported on the actual cochart live common fields. One ADD per nonzero extra component is sufficient.

Let S be the full suffix response map on these cut coordinates. The new endpoint response is z=Sα. A certificate may therefore seek

\[
\alpha_H=1,\qquad (S\alpha)_{\text{off }T}=0,
\]

together with the suffix COPY zero-destination constraint. The same row a and endpoint recovery λ are used. There is no additional constraint λSα≠0. Each source COPY constraint is a separate linear equation in α at the actual pre-COPY cut.

The original helper still equals q immediately after the controls because no compensator changes it further. A direct read immediately after its MOVE is therefore valid. A later receiver module must additionally show that the controls preserve its claimed observation.

## 4. Itinerant controls with stationary physical snapshots

Same-cut controls are only one possible control family. After Q becomes δ, pause the common native. Advance Q along any suffix of its own remaining funded main itinerary, using each original reservation once. At each chart where future controls will be needed, INIT a fresh owned field D_C=0 and execute `D_C += Q`. Leave that field stationary at C. Continue Q to the terminal chart T. The source common state has not advanced during this preparation, so no common event has been skipped or reordered internally.

Resume the common word. At any chosen later event where a common target i is actually at C, execute `common[i] += c D_C`. This introduces cδ into that common field. The snapshot remains unchanged. After its last use, CLEAR D_C at C. Every snapshot is a real owned V-field with a named lifetime; no remote reread or instantaneous field copy occurs.

Thus controls at any future source event in the donor's remaining itinerary charts can be realized without additional Gaussian MOVEs. The raw route is used once to seed the snapshots. It is not reused to revisit charts. Each snapshot costs exactly one INIT, one ADD and one CLEAR; every nonzero control costs one ADD. Storage and erasure are charged. If s distinct chart snapshots are used, declare s additional owned labels unless nonoverlapping reuse has actually been proved. There are s additional lifetimes and the extra owned peak is at most s beyond the unpaused common peak. A refined concrete schedule may have a lower peak. Source-owned labels may be reused only when their source lifetimes do not overlap these caches.

One need not snapshot a chart where the raw Q itself remains available at every needed control. For example controls at T may directly read Q. Snapshots at repeated equal chart visits may be shared only when their physical chart and lifetime really agree. A proof may instead impose a direct chronological donor route schedule and use no snapshots; that is a separately checkable optimization.

Let z_0 be the original helper perturbation response, and let z_k be the public endpoint response to a unit addition into the kth chosen control target at its source cut. By linearity the total response is

\[
z=z_0+\sum_k c_k z_k.
\]

The endpoint condition is the finite exact linear system

\[
(z_0)_{\mathrm{off}\,T}+\sum_k c_k(z_k)_{\mathrm{off}\,T}=0.
\]

Append the analogous equations for every COPY precondition, and any equations needed by the receiver observation. A solved system gives an actual source-safe compensated suffix after the snapshots and controls are lowered as above. An inconsistent system proves impossibility only for the explicitly granted control events, charts and receiver conditions. No finite linear failure is a lower bound on unrestricted multiplication.

## 5. Several loans compose with triangular restoration

There is a useful joint theorem that avoids matrix-invertibility assumptions. Consider m chronological loans using distinct raw donors. Before its loan each donor still holds its original q_i; it has not been changed by another raw receiver operation. After its two-ADD loan it stores δ_i until the terminal correction stage. The chosen receiver and control instructions may depend linearly on these actual carriers, but their complete chronology is fixed.

At the ith loan, the actual helper is

\[
h_i=a_i u+\sum_{j<i} b_{ij}\delta_j.
\]

Here a_i is the unperturbed native row at that cut, and b_ij is the exact response of all previous loan/control operations at that cut. There is no assumption that earlier common perturbations have already been restored. The new difference is δ_i=q_i−h_i.

Suppose the final common outputs are

\[
Y=Wu+\sum_{j=1}^m z_j\delta_j,
\]

each z_j is supported at T, all raw donors finish at T, and a_i=λ_iW with λ_i supported at T. First restore all common outputs by subtracting every z_jQ_j. All raw Q_j still contain their δ_j at this stage.

Then process loans in **reverse chronological order**, i=m,m−1,…,1, and add to Q_i:

\[
\lambda_iY+\sum_{j<i}b_{ij}Q_j.
\]

The common Y has already become Wu. For every j<i the raw donor has not yet been restored, so Q_j=δ_j. Consequently the addition is precisely h_i and Q_i becomes q_i. This proves complete donor and common restoration by downward induction. No Γ matrix needs to be invertible, and no temporary field is necessary.

Exact ordinary work is the sum of the two entry ADDs per loan, all receiver and control instructions, every nonzero coefficient in the z_j endpoint corrections, every nonzero λ_i term and every nonzero b_ij restoration term. Stationary snapshots are additional explicitly charged lifetimes. This theorem is a sufficient composition rule; it does not assert source feasibility of even one new useful loan, nor affordability of a large collection.

## 7. Two-loan worked case and a wrong-order counterexample

Take two common inputs h₁,h₂ and two independent raw donors q₁,q₂. Between the two loans, the native performs `H₂ += 2H₁`. Its endpoint operator is

\[
W=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Loan1 gives δ₁=q₁−h₁. At loan2 the actual common helper is h₂+2q₁=(h₂+2h₁)+2δ₁, so

\[
\delta_2=q_2-h_2-2q_1,
\quad b_{21}=2,
\quad z_1=(1,2)^T,\quad z_2=(0,1)^T.
\]

After both loans the common outputs are (q₁,q₂). The three corrections `H₁−=Q₁`, `H₂−=2Q₁`, `H₂−=Q₂` restore (h₁,h₂+2h₁). Then restore Q₂ by adding H₂+2Q₁ and only afterwards restore Q₁ by adding H₁. This gives precisely q₂ and q₁.

The tempting forward order is false. If Q₁ is restored first, the subsequent formula for Q₂ uses q₁ where it requires δ₁, and its result is q₂+2h₁. Setting h₁=1 gives error2. `CERTIFICATE.json` checks all six independent input columns when two raw recipients are included; it does not rely on that one numerical example.

## 8. A legitimate γ=0 case

The old negative-helper escrow would have Q=−h and try to infer δ from `(λY+Q)/(λz)`. The following typed example has λz=0 and admits the new method.

Before the loan, the native has created an owned snapshot P=h. After the loan, the native signed-swaps the public H with P using three ADDs, then clears P. Originally this suffix maps (h,h) to (h,−h) before retirement. With the loan it maps (q,h) to (h,−q). Hence the public endpoint perturbation is z=0. The complete native preserves arbitrary h and retires an owned value under the explicit erasure contract.

The difference carrier still holds δ=q−h. Adding the restored common H=h recovers q. The old numerator is h−h=0 and its γ is zero, so its recovery formula is undefined. This example establishes that the new interface genuinely has weaker sufficient premises. ## 9. Later controls are strictly stronger than same-cut controls

These two illustrative typed networks are distinct from the frozen 71,261-event native. They prove that the control interface is nonempty and that the proposed broadening changes mathematical capability; they are not source-specific crossing witnesses or multiplication bounds.

### 9.1 A stationary snapshot at an intermediate chart

Use nested charts A⊂B⊂T. The common helper H initially contains h at A; a common main X takes A→B→T; a common main Y initially contains y at B. The source moves H from A to B, adds H into Y, then moves H to T; X follows its two original routes. Its endpoint map is H=h, X=x, Y=y+h.

At A the loan changes H to q and Q to δ. Pause common and move Q along the two reserved raw-X routes. At B leave one actual owned δ snapshot; finish Q's route to T. Resume common. Once H reaches B, add it to a raw recipient, obtaining r+q. The source's Y+=H temporarily gives y+q. The extra control `Y−=D_B` restores y+h. Clear D_B. At T execute H−=Q and Q+=H, restoring h and q.

The literal word has 16 instructions: eight ADDs, six original common/raw MOVEs, one INIT and one CLEAR. Relative to its five-instruction common source plus two raw MOVE reservations, the extra nine instructions are seven ADDs and the one added lifecycle. Peak additional owned storage is one V-field. No MOVE is added. Without the B control, the required off-terminal common output has errorδ. At the loan cut the cochart common H and X have endpoint responses (H=1,Y=1) and (X=1), so no same-cut combination with coefficient1 on H can cancel the Y error. The later control can.

### 9.2 A future original owned field needs no extra snapshot

Again use A⊂B⊂T. The source first INITs P at A, copies h from H into P, then moves P to T. The loan occurs now, while H is at A and P is already at T. The remaining source moves H to B, performs Y+=H, moves H to T, moves owned P from T down to B, performs Y−=P and clears P. A separate common main X has an original A→T route, providing the corresponding single raw-donor reservation. The unchanged native ends with H=h, Y=y and X=x.

Apply the two-ADD loan at A. Pause common, move Q=δ along its reserved A→T route, and add Q into the actually cochart P: now P=q. Resume the source. H and P each carry q when used in the two Y updates, so those updates cancel exactly. A raw recipient reads H=q at B. Once H reaches T, the two terminal ADDs restore H=h and Q=q.

This complete illustrative word has 17 instructions. The baseline is the ten-instruction common source plus one raw MOVE. Exactly six ADDs are added: two entry operations, one future P control, one receiver read and two terminal restorations. There is no extra MOVE or owned lifetime. The original owned descent remains real, charged source geometry. Deleting the P control gives off-terminal common errorδ. At the initial loan cut only H and X are cochart at A; P is at T, so same-cut controls cannot cancel that error. Later raw-donor access to P is essential.

## Maximal endpoint and same-side placement

An ordinary input column isolates the maximal-rank endpoint because the
endpoint input encoding is invertible. A shear's inverse fixes its affected
output coordinate. If the outer operation were a shear, removing it would
leave that isolated high-rank column in a preword whose filtration excludes
it. Thus the outer operation must be in-place. No inverse is granted as a
free physical operation.

For three queries after L, removing the last in-place query gives the isolated
column O3^-1 e_i X, where X=g^-1 u G4. The lower Gaussian triangle puts X
in ranks at least three; the preword's F3 bound makes it pure E3. X is
invertible because the endpoint and the forced square ordinary block u are
invertible. Since the preword's E3 part occupies only the second query's
affected row, the other entry of O3^-1 e_i must vanish. Its remaining block
is invertible. This forces triangular output separation; the partner row
comes from the second query's untouched row and is in F2. Rank three is
impossible there.

For a rank-two partner, its full row rank forces the coefficient multiplying
the first query's E2 row invertible. Consequently that E2 row factors through
the partner's ordinary input row V2. Every subsequent maximal component
inherits the same right factor by ordinary bimodularity. A rank-four
endpoint cannot factor through V2 while retaining an independent input row
V4: the two rows form an invertible ordinary input encoding. This excludes
(4,2). The argument never assumes rank projection preserves physical rank;
the needed full rank comes from the isolated invertible endpoint X.

## Mixed placement and the Gaussian Schur argument

With two queries after and one before, the rank-four component lies in the
outer-query corner. Full rank forces the high endpoint's ordinary column
and row to that corner, with invertible remaining blocks. The complementary
ordinary diagonal blocks are invertible by two-role triangularity. Both
outer queries are in-place by the preceding maximal-endpoint argument.

Removing those two queries leaves N in F2 with rank(pi2 N)<=d: its degree-two
component comes only from the middle query's affected row, followed by
invertible ordinary matrices. A rank-three complementary endpoint already
contradicts F2. For rank two, scaling only the complementary row and column
normalizes their corner to a single Gaussian group unit H. These ordinary
scalings commute with the corresponding outer queries and preserve the
filtration and rank bounds. The exact endpoint form is

    M=[[G+P H Q,P H],[H Q,H]].

The coefficients P,Q may be zero divisors. Since g,H,k are homogeneous group
units, multiplication maps each group basis element bijectively to another
with its nonzero cocycle. The absence of rank-three cross-entry coefficients
therefore partitions P=P1+P2 and Q=Q1+Q2 by ranks one/two. This would be
invalid for an arbitrary nonhomogeneous H; normalization to the actual
Gaussian endpoint is essential. In particular the projection formulas do
not assert a false multiplicative law for pi2.

The invertible lower-right block H in pi2(N) already has rank d. The rank-d
bound forces its Schur complement to vanish, giving exactly

    pi2(N00)=g^-1 P2 H Q2 k^-1.

Putting Z=N00-pi2(N00) in F1 then gives

    G=g Z k-P H Q+P2 H Q2 in F3,

contradicting nonzero pure E4. Every multiplication order is preserved, and
neither P nor Q is inverted. The transposed arguments cover the remaining
placements. The companion reviewed coset theorem excludes (3,3); one-row
or one-corner maximal support excludes (4,4). These are all pairs with total
at least six, since no endpoint exceeds four.

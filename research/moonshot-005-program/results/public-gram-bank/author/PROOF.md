## 1. Universal public-pivot lemma

Let R be a commutative ring containing1/2, H a finite input index set, and
G a symmetric matrix over R. Suppose I contained in H has size r and
G_II=I_r, G_HH=G_HI G_IH. Write S=H\I. For every R-module V and arbitrary
d in V^H, perform actual public operations at a common chart:

\[
q_I=d_I+G_{IS}d_S,\qquad r_S=-d_S+G_{SI}q_I. \tag{1}
\]

Compute q first, with D_S unchanged, then negate each D_S and add its q
combination. Thus q_I=G_IH d and r_S=(G_SH-I_SH)d. The public matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\G_{SI}&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&-I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&G_{IS}\\0&I\end{pmatrix},
\]

with determinant(-1)^|S| and explicit inverse

\[
d_S=G_{SI}q_I-r_S,\qquad d_I=q_I-G_{IS}d_S. \tag{2}
\]

If T contained in S is restored from r_T to original d_T while q_I remains,
use those d_T directly, apply the first equation(2) only on S\T, then its
second equation. This remains a triangular invertible change over R; a
known carrier permutation changes only determinant sign. In the native
instances R=Z[1/2]. The inverse is an algebraic certificate, not permission
to read differently located final outputs together without acquisition.

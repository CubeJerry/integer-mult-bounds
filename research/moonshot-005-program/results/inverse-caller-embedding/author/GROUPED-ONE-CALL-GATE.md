# The grouped four-bank outer normalizer needs four Q calls

**Proved in the explicit restricted ordinary algebra below.** This excludes one proposed grouped one-call/three-query repair. It does not exclude a different interleaving around the native, arbitrary ordinary route operators, nonuniform Gaussian services or the two-native extraction investigated by Worker A/Worker C.

Worker A derives the actual one-call identity

`Q_post F Q_pre=C(R') Δ(K)`,

where Q_pre applies Q on X,U, Q_post applies Q on Y,V, K=QD, and C(R') is the ordinary shear `Y+=R'X, U−=R'V`. Because K commutes with Q,

\[
F=E\Delta(K),\qquad E=Q_{\rm post}^{-1}C(R')Q_{\rm pre}^{-1}.
\]

Direct inversion gives the exact grouped post-native normalizer

\[
E^{-1}(x,y,u,v)=
(Qx,\ Qy-R'x,\ Qu+QR'Qv,\ Qv). \tag{1}
\]

There is no commutation of R' and Q in this calculation. Four real Q bank applications suffice by `E^-1=Q_pre C(R')^-1 Q_post`.

Consider implementations of(1) in which every intervening ordinary factor has the same value under the two algebra evaluations `Q=+I` and `Q=−I`, with R' assigned the same fixed matrix in both. This includes the algebra generated by fixed R', scalar ordinary role mixing and functions of Q² such as the retained lambda operations, provided all named inverses remain defined. A counted Q invocation either applies Q or Q^-1 to one bank, or adds Q or Q^-1 times one bank to another. More generally it may be any primitive whose two evaluated matrices differ by rank at most one bank dimension k. No initially clean or hidden Q-dependent auxiliary record is supplied.

**Lemma.** An algorithm in this interface needs at least four counted Q invocations to implement(1) on four independent k-dimensional banks.

**Proof.** The two endpoint evaluations of(1) differ by `2I_(4k)`, since the cross terms−R'x and QR'Qv are identical at the two signs. The endpoint difference therefore has rank4k. Telescope the difference between the two products of algorithm factors. Ordinary factors contribute zero; each Q-dependent factor contributes a product whose rank is at most k. Rank subadditivity bounds the full endpoint difference by qk for q counted invocations, regardless of cancellations or the conditioning of intervening invertible factors. Thus q>=4. The same proof allows fixed zero scratch when both evaluations have identical initial scratch and the desired four output rows are selected. ∎

The point of this lemma is its exact ordinary-algebra premise. Some actual route operators or column-dependent transformations may not have identical restrictions under the two evaluations. A native invocation inserted between the putative normalizer's Q calls is also not an ordinary factor in this proof. Grouping such a circuit into E^-1 can require nonordinary conjugations, so the lemma is not a blanket one-native lower bound.

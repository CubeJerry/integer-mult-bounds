## Coupled mask lemma

Suppose the current legal word has a descending owned packet P and an ascending
original X_h meeting at a facet F. A later retention at a proper chart M stores
the packet value r in a field Z. The old receiver equation has coefficient one
on both this retained contribution and the held-original contribution. The
packet resets with `ADD Z P` before all its lower work. The held original is
eventually restored to raw X_h at Hub.

Let u be actually constructible from the co-located original values at Hub
before P's first Hub load. Assume u is also constructible at the actual time
when the old restoration of X_h has completed, using donors other than X_h.
Initialize A at Hub, construct u there, and move A to F. At the actual P/X_h
meeting, add A to P and to X_h. Retain the modified packet as before. Its value
at the retention is r+u. The old receiver gains u twice and is unchanged. The
existing packet reset adds r+u to itself and leaves exactly the old lower-packet
state. All subsequent old lower operations therefore run unchanged. The old
restoration of X_h ends with X_h+u at Hub; the second actual Hub recipe for u
restores X_h. Clear A explicitly.

These assertions require that the indicated packet and held-original masks
remain unaltered until their matching uses, and that any other read of a changed
state be accounted for. The literal replay checks that requirement here. The
lemma does not permit inserting an arbitrary unavailable Hub row, treating a
historical row as a current source, or treating the restoration as free.

When F is a rank-seven facet in the rank-eight Hub, the added movement is exactly
one rank-one move. Ordinary costs are one INIT, one CLEAR, two coupling ADDs,
the actual Hub construction ADDs, and the actual Hub restoration ADDs. A nonzero
mask gives a dirty CLEAR. This binary linear identity lifts unchanged to any
characteristic-two field and to whole-record widths by applying the same word
componentwise.

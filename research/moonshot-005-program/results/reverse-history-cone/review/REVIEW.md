## Why final hub values retain the entire hub history span

The source has exactly 1,022 hub arrivals and 1,022 final hub fields. The hub starts empty, no field departs it, each arrival introduces a distinct public role, and no owned field visits it. Every internal hub operation is an ADD between public fields or a nonzero SCALE. These facts are checked directly against the current physical locations.

Viewed as a map from the vector of arrival values to the final hub vector, introducing a new arrival appends a new coordinate. Each subsequent ADD or nonzero SCALE is invertible on the currently present hub coordinates. The full local map is consequently invertible. Its intermediate values are also linear combinations of the arrival coordinates. Final hub values and the complete hub arrival history have exactly the same linear span.

No admitted control acts at the hub: the hub contains every receiver port. Thus restoring the final common hub vector forces every hub-arrival response to vanish. This uses restoration achieved by the admitted controls; an extra unmodelled correction or record at a forbidden chart would change the interface. The equivalence does not grant transport between nonhub charts.

## Completeness without assuming a full native inverse

Write I_i for receiver i's initial observation matrix, A_i for its m_i nonhub arrival rows, and H for the final hub observation matrix. The relevant arrival relations are the coefficient vectors l for which l A_i belongs to the row span of I_i and H.

The review constructs an explicit common-input matrix V with 458 columns. A complete exact forward replay authenticates that its final offhub responses are the 458 coordinate unit vectors and its hub responses are zero. Every hub arrival also has zero response on V. In particular H V=0. These are directly checked witness properties; a claimed formula for the full native inverse is not assumed.

For each receiver, exact rational elimination computes

\[
r_i=\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix}I_iV\\A_iV\end{bmatrix}
-\operatorname{rank}(I_iV).
\]

Right multiplication by V sends the true quotient by row(I_i,H) into the test quotient by row(I_i V), because H V=0. Therefore the true quotient arrival rank is at least r_i.

The certificate supplies exactly m_i−r_i arrival coefficient rows. The review checks that those rows are independent and that each has an exact completion by initial observations and final hub fields on all 1,480 original common inputs. They give m_i−r_i independent elements of the true arrival-relation kernel, so the true quotient arrival rank is at most r_i. The two bounds agree. The supplied rows are thus a complete basis of the arrival projections of all full history null relations.

Across the 96 receivers, the ranks sum to 1,538 and the complete relation counts sum to 1,859, accounting for all 3,397 arrival rows. Any additional null relation involving only initial and final hub observations has zero private response: the initial observations are captured before controls, and the final common endpoints are restored. Consequently these 1,859 completed arrival relations suffice for the pure-gain test even if such additional initial/hub dependencies exist.

## From the identities to zero own gain

With a fixed common-input observation matrix O, the granted history has the form

\[
h=O u+Bq.
\]

The allowed additions depend linearly on independent raw differences and do not change O. Each completed source identity has zero response to every allowed control, by its exact support localization. Initial observations have zero private response, and final hub observations have zero private response under endpoint restoration. Completeness then implies that every left-null relation of O has zero private response.

A linear decoder that outputs a pure own qY2 term must annihilate O. Its coefficient row therefore lies in that complete null space, where its response on qY2 is zero. This proves the claimed zero clean own-reverse gain for each of the 96 interfaces.

The source identities themselves remain valid against arbitrary realized increments at the admitted sites, by linear superposition. That fact alone does not extend the no-information conclusion to common-dependent replacement. Such an operation can change the common observation matrix from O to O', and O' can have new null relations not covered by this certificate. Dirty-helper swaps, mixed common/raw records, a changed native rule, carrier remapping, extra observation charts or an independent raw-return record require a new interface analysis.

The proof also does not attach one paid rank-one call to each failed own duty. Joint coding and different record interfaces may change transport costs and observability. The result supplies no numeric whole-system lower bound and does not conflict with the separately verified savings from raw-route fusion. It certifies the failure of this specific additive source-history interface, with its stated endpoint and observation assumptions.

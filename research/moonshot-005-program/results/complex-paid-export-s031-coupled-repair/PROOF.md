## A distinct new bank cannot help a two-message repair

The two messages must both leave the rank-six cut for every extension of
`A` to any newly introduced banks. Suppose one endpoint is a distinct new
bank `w`. This bank has no old message incidence and no original input or
output role; equal-span aliases of old banks have already been identified.

If only one new message is incident to `w`, put `w` on the same side as its
other endpoint. That message is not outgoing, so at most one new message
leaves the cut. If both messages are incident to `w`, choose the side of `w`
that minimizes outgoing incidence. Two incoming messages or two outgoing
messages can both be made non-outgoing; a chain through `w` has at most one
outgoing message for either side choice. If the other endpoint is also new,
choose the side of the new-bank component in the same way. With two total
messages these cases exhaust the possible incidences.

There is consequently an extension of `A` with at most five total outgoing
messages and the same six-field identity demand, a contradiction. Any feasible
two-message repair uses only the 1102 old physical banks as endpoints. This
makes exhaustive endpoint screening finite, without making a surviving
endpoint pair a construction.

## Limitations

This result allows arbitrary linear recoding of surviving old forms. Its
one-message exclusion is broader than a test that freezes their original
payloads. It applies only to the specified seven old deletions, the already
paid genuine observer, the original target roles, and the displayed complete
caller tariff. It neither excludes all low-grade repairs nor admits a new
complex supplier or a new multiplication exponent.

# What the new fine-prime Gaussian caller demands at 0.005

The [fine-prime product in #7834](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/7834) restores original convolution coefficients before carry and uses an absolute rounding error bound. Its published axis count uses d ≍ p^0.997 and precision p ≍ log n. At its common primitive saving 0.001737 the largest log exponent is 0.998268211. This addresses a weaker complete rate and depends on the PR's named predecessor results.

Set d ≍ p^a, with 0<a<1 and local address length ≍ p^(1−a). Suppose both the ordinary address interchange and full grouped complex butterflies meet their stated interfaces at exponent 1−δ. With the original Gaussian block and window exponents 9/8 and 1/2, the dense per-axis term has exponent a. Both global permutation and butterfly bills have exponent 1−aδ. Their maximum is minimized at a=1/(1+δ), giving the saving δ/(1+δ). To absorb the fixed polylogarithms and certify 1/200 from these invoices one needs **δ>1/199**. This is a condition for this proof's sufficient time bound.

The remaining displayed powers also fit near this balance. The local quadratic chirp work has power 2(1−a). The sparse arithmetic powers are a+1−9/8 and a+9/8−2+1/2. Sparse movement has powers 3/8 and 1/8. The setup stays subexponential at fixed 0<a<1, and the rounding proof budgets O(p^a log p)=o(p) cumulative shifts. For example, δ=0.00503 and a=0.99499 put each displayed power strictly below 0.995. The full source, template, tape, numerical and original-coordinate proofs are still required for the chosen interfaces.

A separate native BIT price tests compatibility. At m=870 the raw productive moment at 1/199 is positive for integer excess h≤49 above the nine-bank floor and negative at h=50. Over arbitrarily large even m the limit admits h≤56 and excludes h≥57 under the same paid and productive inventories. At h=56 the first passing even m is 11,442. A hypothetical exact-floor source first passes at m=110. Thus the earlier h=128 target cannot supply even this stronger raw screen. The actual native source, its ordinary compiler and a comparably strong complex word remain outstanding.

## A specific double loss already excludes the target

The published [compact ordinary wrapper](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/4f0c67d5bb41d4af5501dcd40b3b388b35e0ff02/research/fine-prime-gaussian-product/ORDINARY.md#complete-exponent-and-finite-thresholds) takes a compact saving c to c(1−c). Even granting a compatible compact supplier at our best possible fixed-tariff raw saving, c<0.00504325518581 by the native ideal ceiling. Monotonicity on [0,1/2] gives c(1−c)<0.00501782076295<1/199. The balanced Gaussian proof would then give less than 0.00499276794827 from these invoices, even with arbitrarily strong butterflies. To cross the common-primitive threshold this compact reduction requires c>(1−sqrt(1−4/199))/2≈0.00505063453737. This excludes this particular composition of invoices for the target. The separate rebalanced self-hosted BIT ordinary supplier would need its own proof of compatibility with the Gaussian caller's disjoint CNOT permutation and arbitrary physical spectators.

## 1. Priced occurrence lemma

Start at an audited cut with no field on a rank-seven native facet. Let n
public X originals be below Hub at ranks r_j<7, with all other X already at

Initial nonglobal X coefficients must be native-local at their actual charts,
as required by the retained normalized fitting/occurrence theorem. All arbitrary
original-Y coefficients, restoration, endpoint and ownership equations remain.

Let Q0(t) be the executed prefix plus the direct minimum remaining public
ascents, and q all future positive owned MOVE occurrences. Let a_j indicate
whether X_j makes a future upward facet arrival. Each monotone X visits at most
one facet; each Y visits only its own final facet, supplying its usual anchor.
Owned upward facet arrivals and downward facet departures inject into q. The
retained 36/52 proper-cover occurrence proof therefore gives

    chi(A) <= sum_j a_j + q.

For 0<t<1, a facet-visiting X must pay at least (7-r_j)^t+1 for its remaining
route. Its direct ascent, already in Q0, costs (8-r_j)^t. Hence that visit has
compulsory extra price

    delta_j=(7-r_j)^t+1-(8-r_j)^t,  0<delta_j<1.

Subadditivity proves the route lower bound. Strict concavity proves positivity;
strict increase proves the upper bound one. Every positive owned MOVE costs at
least one. Thus

    Q >= Q0+q+sum_j a_j delta_j
      >= Q0+chi(A)-sum_j a_j(1-delta_j)
      >= Q0+chi(A)-n+sum_j delta_j.                 (1)

The last step grants every possible original facet visit when minimizing the
lower bound. It does not assert that all visits actually occur. An intermediate
chart costs something even when its original eventually must reach Hub. This
lemma applies to every block fitting covered by the retained occurrence proof;
it does not require scalar codes or immutable packets.

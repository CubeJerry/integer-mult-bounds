## The two-ADD cleanup, including arbitrary baselines

Write B_i=y_i+A_i and B_k=y_k+A_k for the complete known-axis baselines at
ground. Before the parent's parity inverse networks, apply

\[
Y_i\to Y_k,\qquad Y_k\to Y_i.
\]

These are the inverse of the future two-ADD exchange. Immediately after
this preconditioning, the two baseline values are B_k and B_i+B_k. The
parent parity network has identity action on all initial Y/axis baselines
and contributes D_i only to the target. After it, and after the courier
reads R, the two actual values are

\[
Y_i=B_k+D_i,\qquad Y_k=B_i+B_k+R.
\]

At J, execute Y_k to Y_i and then Y_i to Y_k. The results are

\[
Y_i=B_i+D_i+R,\qquad Y_k=B_k+D_i.
\]

The target gains the complete half sum. The courier's R terms cancel,
including its nonnative terms, and are replaced by D_i, which is a valid
native correction for that courier. The baselines return exactly to their
original rows. The seven exchanges use disjoint target/courier pairs, and
their inverse networks are all performed while every Y is still at ground.

This cleanup does not assume the target's whole current value is pure X,
nor that a courier can forget unwanted information. The second forward ADD
and the earlier baseline inverse are actual operations. Removing either
fails the all-column replay.

## 3. Why the quadratic test excludes every real two-destination cover

Choose a nonzero coordinate of r_j and delete that coordinate from vectors in
L_j^perp. The remaining seven coordinates give a linear isomorphism to R7.
There are28 homogeneous quadratic monomials z_a z_b with a<=b.

Suppose evaluation on selected forced normals has rank27 and its nonzero
kernel form q has symmetric matrix rank greater than two. If two proper
subspaces covered these normals, enlarge them to hyperplanes with nonzero
linear equations l and m. The nonzero polynomial lm would vanish on all the
normals. The kernel is one-dimensional, so lm would be proportional to q.
But the symmetric matrix of lm is(l m^T+m l^T)/2, of rank at most two. This
contradicts the certified rank of q. At least three departures are required.

The argument covers arbitrary real destination charts, including irrational
ones and higher-rank first destinations. It is not a finite catalogue search.
For sources without this quadratic certificate, seven independent forced
normals still imply at least two departures; no three-departure claim follows
merely from linear rank seven.

The verifier establishes ranks by exact integer cross-multiplication, reducing
each row by its integer gcd. These operations preserve rational row spans.
It performs no floating point, modular rank test or symbolic-library rank
call. Candidate forms are checked nonzero and evaluated on every newly
derived forced normal. Rank27 plus one explicit nonzero vanisher proves the
one-dimensional kernel exactly. The symmetric twice-form matrix has rank6
in every asserted three-exit case. A changed polynomial is rejected, and
seven coordinate normals covered by z0*z1=0 supply the linear-rank-only
negative control.

Binary duals tensor with I_m over every characteristic-two field. Granted
block rows vanish while the target identity survives. Thus the path count
holds for arbitrary block coefficients and whole widths. The quadratic ranks
refer to the real chart geometry, independently of that coefficient field.

## 2. Why the cyclic stationary-cache decoder works

This is A's constructive mechanism. Let a_0,...,a_(m-1) be pairwise real
orthogonal native roots, and let C be a c-dimensional chart orthogonal to
all of them, so c+m<=8. Assume the actual common sum z=sum_j x_(a_j) can
be supplied at C after the input-cache phase. Its acquisition and descent
remain a separate paid obligation.

For each j, route original X_(a_j) upward through the forward cyclic
interval charts

    S_(j,k)=C+span(a_j,...,a_(j+k-1)),  1<=k<=m-1,

then to Hub. At each actual stop create a stationary owned raw copy of
x_(a_j). These charts are nested on that original's one physical route.
The copies never ascend; they stay at their real acquisition charts.

After the common sum exists, original Y_(a_i) rises from ground to C and
adds z. It then follows the reverse cyclic interval charts

    T_(i,k)=C+span(a_(i-k),...,a_(i-1)),  1<=k<=m-1.

At T_(i,k)=S_(i-k,k), the actual stationary cache of x_(a_(i-k)) is already
present. The receiver reads it. The T charts are nested, exclude a_i and
lie in F_(a_i), so the entire Y route is a legal ascent. After all local
reads its value is

    y_(a_i) + sum_j x_(a_j) + sum_(j!=i) x_(a_j)
    = y_(a_i)+x_(a_i).

Every X stays raw throughout. Every arbitrary initial y column is retained
literally. The same binary word works at every positive record width over
every characteristic-two field. This proof, rather than a finite numerical
check, supplies that uniform value identity.

There are m(m-1) actual stationary cache owners, each with INIT, raw capture
and CLEAR. Put d=8-c-m. Each source-original route costs

    [c]+(m-2)[1]+[d+1],

and each receiver route costs

    [c]+(m-1)[1]+[d],

where [0]=0 and zero-distance operations are omitted. A supplied owned
sum at an actual h-dimensional superchart pays [h-c] to descend to C.
This formula does not price the sum's earlier acquisition for free.

Here m=c=4, so each original X and Y has one rank4 jump and three unit

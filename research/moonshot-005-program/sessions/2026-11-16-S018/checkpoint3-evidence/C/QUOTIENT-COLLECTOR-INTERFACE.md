# Compile collector searches modulo actual known ground inputs

This is a constructive corollary of the actual-ground output normalization
and the physical Hub restoration criterion. It states exactly when a
many-receiver search may treat some original carriers as zero on the
unknown coordinates. It does not supply the remaining network or assert
that a finite unsuccessful search excludes other constructions.

## Actual source and planned word

Choose S among the111 original input coordinates. Before routing the
originals, make an independent actual raw copy of x_s at its native line
and descend that owned copy to ground, for every s in S. Keep all original
Ys at ground until the preparation below is complete. This source costs
exactly |S| rank-one owned descents, |S| INIT, |S| capture ADD and |S| CLEAR.
The copies do not replace any required original-X native-to-Hub transit.

Plan a fixed legal native word W, including every actual source acquisition,
arithmetic co-location, public route, owned route and retirement. It starts
with raw original X and arbitrary original Y. Zero-born additional records
must acquire their contents physically. It ends with every X at Hub and
every Y at its own native facet. Assume initially that an original Y is
read only into another original Y. In particular, no arbitrary y enters an
X or owned record in this simplest compiler grammar.

Write its final maps as

    X_out=A x,
    Y_out=F x+M y.

M is automatically invertible: it is the product of W's original-Y-to-Y
ADDs. A must either be certified invertible, or be covered by a separately
proved actual Hub restoration mechanism. If the only X writes are
original-X-to-original-X ADDs, A is automatically invertible and the
reverse of that X ADD subsequence, now at actual common Hub, implements
its inverse. If X reads a nonlocal packet, invertibility of A is an extra
condition: the quotient experiment alone does not establish it.

Let U be the complement of S, and let D be the desired X-to-Y increment
matrix, usually the full identity. The following is then sufficient and,
for correction only by the stated ground known source, necessary:

    F[:,U] = D[:,U].

After the candidate meets this condition, compute its omitted S columns
on actual inputs. At ground, before W begins, preload the Ys with

    (D-F)x,

using the actual raw x_S copies, then apply M^{-1} to the original Ys.
The latter can be implemented by reversing the future Y-to-Y ADD
subsequence while every Y is still at ground. Clear all raw source copies
after these real reads. Execute W, then apply the actual Hub inverse of A.
The resulting complete endpoint map is X=x, Y=y+D x.

The proof is direct substitution. The ground error depends only on S by
the displayed U-column equality. The future Y transfer maps
M^{-1}(y+(D-F)x) to y+(D-F)x, which cancels the entire known-column error.
The X endpoint is unaffected by Y preparation in this grammar. The final
Hub inverse changes no Y. The source copies can be retired before W, so
their lifetimes need not overlap W's owners.

## What this permits in an actual collector search

On U columns, an original X_s for s in S initially has value zero. It can
therefore be used as a symbolic zero accumulator when searching for the
unknown-input transfer. Its **physical** record still contains x_s, is
still at its native line, and must obey that original's upward route.
Every known-column contribution it later sends to a receiver is removed
by the single actual ground preparation. No separate late raw x_s donor
is required at each receiving facet.

This cancellation can involve many collectors, many receivers, coupled
known offsets and later original-Y forwarding. It is not limited to one
collector carrying x_s+u and one receiver preloading x_s. A fixed candidate
determines the complete known offset matrix, which is corrected together.
There is no requirement that the known-coordinate part of each individual
collector remain unchanged along its path.

Likewise a search may set initial y=0 when optimizing the X-to-Y transfer
within this grammar, because M^{-1} gives a constructive physical lift of
every arbitrary independent y afterward. This is a statement about which
columns the search may defer. It does not mean the physical Y records are
zero, or permit a field to be read at a chart it has not reached. Their
real arbitrary-y contents remain throughout the final all-column word.

Every j in U remains a genuine independent unknown. An original X_j used
as a carrier retains its own unknown contribution unless a real operation
changes it. Any resulting nonzero error in F[:,U] must be corrected by the
actual network. It cannot be absorbed into the ground preparation.

## Wider Y feedback and the restoration gate

If Y may enter an X or owned record, use the general normalization theorem
instead of automatically reversing only the Y-to-Y subsequence. Reconstruct
the actual final homogeneous blocks. The final X-from-Y block must vanish,
the final Y-from-Y block must be invertible, and the required pure-X error
rows must lie in the actual ground-source span. Temporary Y content in X
is allowed when those conditions hold; the separate positive physical
control in `normalization-feedback-control` demonstrates this case.

An unknown-column search must not infer original restoration from its
restricted matrix. A may be injective on U columns and still lose an S
direction. Actual ground x_S copies are not Hub side records. If A is
singular, use the full physical restoration criterion with actual Hub
observations, keeping every arbitrary-y column in any mixed side field.
The reviewed reset singular fixture and x+y nuisance counterexample
demonstrate these separate failure modes.

## Costs, predecessors and limits

After the candidate is found, known-column preload ADDs number at most
111|S| in the direct raw basis. The inverse Y word costs the length of the
future Y-to-Y subsequence if that simple construction is used. The X
inverse likewise costs its reverse subsequence in the pure-original
grammar, or the actual verified elimination word otherwise. These finite
ordinary counts, storage/lifetime effects and their uniform compiler
implementation remain charged; zero new movement does not mean free work.

This corollary packages the protected-carrier nuisance idea, root's
known-source reset, C's ground-Y normalization and the already admitted
Hub restoration theorem into a useful search interface. It does not evade
the historical Y-feedback normalization, fitting rank or geometric arrival
bounds. It supplies neither a scalable shared code nor an all111 decoder.

native_all_columns, owned_erasure, compiler, precision_tape and

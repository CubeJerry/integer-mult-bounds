## Algebra and full-rate identities

I read the entire construction and checked the following dependencies.
The balanced convolution builds a formula, counting duplicated children,
with syntactic degree D and size exp(O(log²m)). Coefficients c_a are
integers generated by exact binomial arithmetic; no execution-ring factorial
inversion is introduced. Constant children are handled without increasing
factor length.

For product composition, the two tensor families commute and every cyclic
rotation preserves each family's cyclic order. Regrouping proves fg I on
every grade. For sums, the grading operator and degree-minus-one factor map
q-commute. The mixed Gaussian-binomial coefficients vanish as integral
cyclotomic identities, so no complex-only or unramified-specialization
assumption is used. Extracting equal-rank total grades gives length d and
rank d times the two old ranks. All independent coordinates are retained.

The common order lcm(1,...,D) supplies exactly the intermediate roots needed.
Repeated power-of-two rounding is avoided. A single final identity padding
and cyclic block construction gives L^b=P I. Regular multiplication on the
finite free dyadic power basis sends P to P I, yielding a fully dyadic
pencil before reduction modulo an odd prime. This handles primes dividing
an internal root order. Query substitutions commute with row matrices.
Shifted predictors use sign substitution and a sign on one factor; all
cyclic products receive the sign exactly once.

## Actual generation and heights

The matrix rank recurrences give log K<=F log max(2,D). Every intermediate
rank is bounded by the final construction bound. Entry operations are copies,
root shifts, additions or scalar scaling; tensor concatenation does not hide
products of arbitrary affine entries. Redundant root arrays therefore have
polynomially bounded bit lengths in F and m. Cyclotomic coefficients have
O(M) bits, and dense polynomial division and companion-matrix powers have
polynomial-in-M bit costs and temporary heights. Literal dense output,
sequential scans and schoolbook arithmetic fit E2(C log²(m+2)) without a
matrix-search oracle or a free compressed expansion.

The separate fixed-depth rational-address argument controls numerator and
denominator *values* by exp(poly(m)); this expense is below the new scalar
budget. Taking a compatible prime radix above the complete width and bad-value
product, then paying for trial division, padding and scaling pieces, stays
within the same height-two budget. These are analytical upper bounds, not
numerically evaluated constants or performance measurements.

## New profile and inversion

With Z_j=(j+2)^3 log2(j+3), the complete profile is S_j<=E2(CZ_j²).
Thus log S_j<=2^(CZ_j²); fixed powers of this quantity and the diagonal index
O((j+log S_j)²) give d_j,b_j<=2^(C'Z_j²). The absolute source cutoff is
absorbed by enlarging a constant; the machine does not need it as advice.

For z=log2(x+16), choose j=floor(c[z/log2²(z+2)]^(1/6)). It is eventually
positive and at least half the unrounded value. Since log(j+3)=O(log(z+2)),
sufficiently small fixed c makes C'Z_j²<=z/2. Both d_j and b_j are then
at most sqrt(x+16)<=2sqrt x. This proves eligibility, not just an unconstrained
cost optimization. Adding x/(j+2) gives the claimed explicit residual.
The weaker z^(-1/8) form follows because log2(z+2)^(1/3)=O(z^(1/24)).

The construction does not meet the stronger exp(m^C) MR bound merely by this
estimate: exp(exp(O(log²m))) is exp(m^(O(log m))). The stronger conditional
profile and the preflight endpoint hypotheses remain explicitly open.
The window-depth obstruction is compatible with reducing row dimension,
and S002's formal flat sum-of-products barrier does not cover the nested
composition. No global runtime lower bound is inferred.

# Scoped external research leads

These entries record actual reading scope and relevance. They are not a
comprehensive novelty audit and do not supply unreviewed tape primitives.

## Ordering-based biclique decompositions

Cardinal, McCarty and Yuditsky, *Biclique decompositions from Welzl orders*,
[arXiv:2606.09785v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09785), 14 October 2026.
Read the introduction and complete Section 2, not all applications. Its
interval-to-partition construction and breakpoint viewpoint motivated
the separate crown-product run and laminar calculation. E003's exact
proof does not assume a small-contiguity bound for that graph family.

Pinto, *Biclique Covers and Partitions*, EJC21(1)P1.19, DOI
[10.37236/3595](https://doi.org/10.37236/3595). Abstract/introduction scope
only. Cover and partition local loads are different objects; the paper's
separation does not settle S004's specific JR hypothesis. The SEA2025
paper on fractional binary ranks was inspected only through its primary
search excerpt. No result about total partition count was treated as a
local-load tensorization theorem.

## A fresh tape-sorting result

Yann Tal, *Sorting in o(N log N) Time on a Multitape Turing Machine*,
[ECCC TR26-331, revision 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/331/revision/1/download/),
25 November 2026. Read the abstract, introduction and Sections 2.1--2.3;
the rest is unaudited. E002 had separately recorded the abstract before
this E003 read. The revision derives sorting and transposition consequences
using the new OpenAI tape result. Its general sorting bound does not by
itself improve E003's structured complete-field interchange. Importing a
sorting routine that depends on the multiplier being constructed would
also require a noncircular dependency check. No such import is made.
Its wide-item and local-piece mechanisms remain possible future leads,
with their width regimes, movement and space still to be audited.

## Symmetric-group transforms and the exposed star sum

Iglesias and Natale, *A Fast Fourier Transform for the Johnson graph*,
[arXiv:1912.09243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09243). Read the primary
abstract only. It gives a sparse-factor basis transform on fixed-size
subsets using exact arithmetic and Jucys--Murphy operators. This suggests
looking beyond monomial routing for E003's sum of hub swaps. It does not
provide a ready executor for arbitrary full register arrays with weighted
seed-dependent coefficients. The domain, coefficients, inverse transform,
bit heights and physical layout all differ and remain unproved here.
No quantum-circuit, RAM-arithmetic or representation-theoretic operation
has been counted as a free tape operation.

The search used both available engines and primary papers where a result
was inspected. Search recall, dates and partial reading do not certify
literature priority or optimality.

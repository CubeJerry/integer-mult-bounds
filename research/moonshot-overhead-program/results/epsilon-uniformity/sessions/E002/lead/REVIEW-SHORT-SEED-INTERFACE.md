The Newton count pencil has seed-diagonal coefficients
chi_j(t)=(-1)^(a_j dot t) on all W=2^r independent seed rows. For the
unnormalized Walsh matrix, H inverse=H/W. The (u,v) entry of
H inverse diag(chi_j) H is W inverse times the character sum over t
with frequency u+v+a_j. It is exactly one when v=u+a_j and zero otherwise.
The spatial star swap acts on a different tensor factor. Thus the complete
seed change conjugates the weighted star sum to
sum_j X_(a_j) tensor P_j, with no initialization, projection or volume
expansion. Applying the inverse restores all independently occupied rows.

The r one-bit Walsh steps can each split the payload stably by one seed
bit into a fixed number of streams, add/subtract aligned partner records,
and merge them. These are ordinary full-volume tape sweeps, with reused
marked regions and charged finite counters; no long f-field interchange
has been hidden. Hence the interface change costs O(V log(m+2)) ordinary
work under the retained fixed-precision row-scan contract. Its logarithmic
norm growth is O(log m). This argument verifies the interface cost, not a
cost for executing the resulting sum of long-field coupled permutations.

The same character pattern persists across Newton stages, but retaining
the seed basis requires charging every other conjugated seed-row map.
The hub swaps still do not commute. The comparison correctly leaves their

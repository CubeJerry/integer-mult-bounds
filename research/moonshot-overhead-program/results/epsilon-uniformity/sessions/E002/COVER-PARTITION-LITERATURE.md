# Focused prior-art check: covers, exact partitions and algebraic rank

This is a narrow follow-up search during E002, not an exhaustive novelty
review. Primary publisher sources were checked with two search engines.
The distinction used by the new source-specific theorem is established
background in biclique theory.

Trevor Pinto, *Biclique Covers and Partitions*, Electronic Journal of
Combinatorics 21(1), P1.19 (2014),
[DOI 10.37236/3595](https://doi.org/10.37236/3595), studies both total and
per-vertex incidence measures. Its principal separation shows that small
local covering number does not in general bound local partition number.
The introduction and retrieved Section 3 rank comparison/Section 4 theorem
passages were read; a full independent proof audit was not performed.
The ordinary rank-subadditivity method is existing methodology. E002's
claim is the explicit construction and failure calculation for its retained
characteristic-two matrix, not discovery of a general cover/partition gap.

Angikar Ghosal and Andreas Karrenbauer, *Engineering Insights into Biclique
Partitions and Fractional Binary Ranks of Matrices*, SEA 2025,
[DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.SEA.2025.18](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SEA.2025.18),
was inspected through its abstract, introduction and definitions/tensor
product discussion. Its binary rank uses binary factors under ordinary
matrix multiplication; it is not rank over F2. Its Boolean-cover and exact
partition distinctions and fractional formulations are relevant background.
Their results about fractional tensor ranks are not imported as bounds on
E002's local load or on physical tape cost. No unproved multiplicativity
property is assumed in E002's argument.

The E002 cover theorem is self-contained given the retained label/rank
matrix and the separately reviewed opposite-pin construction. It constructs
an actual finite cover by a deterministic averaging/greedy argument and
then shows its too-small matrix span cannot reproduce P*−I. Its limitations
are therefore source-specific and explicit. No literature priority or
optimality among all exact weighted rectangle decompositions is claimed.

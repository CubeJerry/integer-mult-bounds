## 1. P2: finite construction and margins

The enlarged constant in the characteristic-two predictor is paid. With
`D_pred<=24 sqrt(m)`, the completed call invoice is bounded by
`2^17 sqrt(m) D_I(1+lambda)`. Taking logarithms adds 17, not an
unaccounted exponent. The retained estimate

\[
\log_2[D_I(1+\lambda)]\le 3x/d+10d^2\log_2(4x)
\]

and `100J<=d<200J` imply the half margin once
`x>=2^42 J^3 log_2 J`; the predecessor decreasing `log(x)/x`
comparison has more than enough slack. The rational Walsh construction
retains its quarter margin at the same enlarged threshold. Passing to
the next power-of-two trial exponent changes x by only an absolute factor,
so `log m=O(J^3 log J)` remains valid. The new scalar-field and shift
choices do not change the geometry's rational matrices or label guards.

The complete address-unit list has polynomial count and bit height. Its
product has polynomial bit length; the elementary compatible-prime bound
therefore supplies `b=poly(m)`. Multidigit leading batching needs
`ell=ceil(log_b W_I)=poly(m)`, although `W_I` itself is exponential.
Its direct remainder bill and binary exterior factor `<b^2` belong in
the polynomial proportional coefficient. Expanded optional validation can
still cost `2^{m^C}` and is charged as preparation. Only the polynomial
compact plans are interpreted during payload processing.

## 3. P4--P6: physical binary routing and the separate Walsh recurrence

The guarded-XOR proof in `permutations.tex` has fixed-degree control,
rank and offset arithmetic. Its exception fraction `80k 2^-G` pays both
destination computation and the stable short-list sort. The theorem's
explicit choice of G absorbs polynomial-m coefficients and adds only
polynomial-m factors and one power of `log k` to the pass count. Narrow
slots use direct bit moves. For the admitted large-input regime,
`k>=m^K` also leaves the required constant fraction of free bits.
Any remaining small-k cases have `k=poly(m)` and can be included in
the polynomial-m direct bound or handled by the guarded fallback.

The outer CRT tables use at most seven axes, each of width at most
`20 ceil(log_2 k)`, so their explicit table dimensions and arithmetic
degrees are absolute. The genuine short-axis primes are kept, with their
absolute tested cutoff. They are different from the address radix.

P6 includes the essential width-dependent Walsh toll:

\[
T_F(a)\le m^C(1+a^{\rho_J})(1+\log(2k))
          +(\sigma_F/W_F)T_F(a/A_F).
\]

Interchange is proved first, then supplies the slot-exchange/packed-XOR
work used in this toll. Division by `a^rho_J` leaves a polynomial-m
local coefficient and recursive ratio at most one half. Thus no circular
recurrence or missing `a^rho_J` factor is used. Route B's amended S3/S2
now states this same sequential two-tier argument.

Each internal child has one contiguous complete active slot with all
other fields as spectators. It does not repeat the top-level arbitrary
selected-position permutation over all k address bits. Base-A piece
assembly has at most polynomial-m overhead. Since `W_F<=m^C` and
`A_F>=m`, the leading divisibility modulus `W_F^j` is a fixed power
of the selected width. Padding against at least `2^(k/2)` spectator
rows therefore adds at most a constant factor above the stated guard.

## 4. Numerical precision and scalar-domain separation

The rational Walsh side uses exact rational coefficients and W1's charged
short-times-long multiplication/division. Its coefficient heights,
small-row dimensions and local logarithmic norm gains are polynomial m.
W2 emits the selected small spectral block on demand; the number `2^m`
of possible blocks does not multiply its operator norm or per-record
lookup work. The paid spectral converter words remain.

W3's propagated-error gain has logarithm
`O(m^K log(k+2))`. Its event count is polynomial in m and k because
branch counts are polynomial m while arity is at least m. At
`k>=m^{K'}`, both gain and event logarithms fit an absolute multiple of
k, yielding an absolute `O(k)` numerical format. The bootstrap bounds
all intermediates and hence prevents integer overflow, not just final
rounding failure. General long-times-long products retain the source's
fixed `O(k log k)` multiplier.

The finite characteristic-two processor implements exact permutations of
encoded symbols. It does not substitute finite-field scalars for the
numerical rational Walsh coefficients. At the outer layer, the source's
unitary-boundary schedules and fixed norm/error margins give exact
convolution coefficients by rounding and then the full integer product
by carries. The absolute prime-shape fallback remains independent of J.

## 6. P9--P11: one machine and one visited-space bound

Let x=`log_2(1+log_2 n)`. Choosing the largest dyadic `J=2^h` with
`J^4 h<=x+O(1)` gives

\[
J=\Theta((x/\log(x+2))^{1/4}),\quad
Z=J^3\log(J+2)=\Theta(x^{3/4}(\log(x+2))^{1/4})=o(x).
\]

The parameter profile gives `log m=O(Z)`, and the normalization
`r` as the least power of two at least `8m^K` gives `log r=O(Z)`.
For `u=ceil(n/r)`, `log u=log n-O(Z)`. The logarithm of all permitted
setup, even repeated `r^2` times, is at most
`2^{O(Z)}(1+x)=2^{o(x)}`, whereas `log n=Theta(2^x)`.
It is therefore `n^{o(1)}` actual work and space and eventually fits
the construction cap. The threshold `m^{K'}=2^{O(Z)}` is also
`o(log u)`, and the fixed unknown `U_abs` is eventually passed.

The chunk workspace is `m^K u<=n/8+m^K<=n/4` eventually. Reused
marked intervals hold all additive regions and each successive chunk.
Inputs, outputs and the diagonal positional wrapper add a fixed multiple
of n. A candidate that tries to leave the fixed allowance is stopped
before that head move. Exact clocks and cleanup have charged work.
Consequently this is a bound on the union of visited cells, uniformly
over all input lengths and all selected J.

On successful lengths, `r^2u<=2rn` and `L(u)<=L(n)` give

\[
T(n)\le n\sqrt{L(n)}\,
       2^{O(\log m)+O(x/J)}
 =n\sqrt{L(n)}\,
       2^{O(x^{3/4}(\log(x+2))^{1/4})}.
\]

This is `n^{1+o(1)}`, so the outer quadratic clock eventually does
not abort. At every other length, the capped attempt and exact fallback
terminate within the same uniform linear visited-space allowance. There
are only finitely many exceptional lengths, so the eventual bound extends
to a conventional absolute big-O statement by changing its constant.

## 1. Character basis and multiplicity

For `p` not dividing `q`, a finite field extension contains a primitive
`q`th root because `q` divides `p^f-1` for some `f`. Lifting its defining
irreducible polynomial gives a finite free local ring over `Z/p^2 Z`
with residue field of characteristic `p` and maximal ideal generated by
`p`. One Newton correction lifts the root; the derivative is a unit.
Every nontrivial character difference remains a unit. Therefore the
character matrix is invertible, including when `q` is composite.

The inner distance-`p` adjacency eigenvalue on weight `t` is the
coefficient of `z^p` in `(1-z)^t(1+(q-1)z)^(h-t)`, with multiplicity
`binom(h,t)(q-1)^t`. The source label matrix is exactly
`I+e_p(A_1,...,A_h)`: selecting `p` outer coordinates enforces far
inner words there, and identity factors enforce equality elsewhere.
No extra outer pairs are included in this tensor description.

Writing `c=binom(2p-1,p)` and `beta=binom(2p-2,p-1)`, one has
`c=1 mod p^2`, `beta=pc/(2p-1)`, and `v_p(beta)=1`. These hold also
for the smallest prime `p=3`; no stronger Wolstenholme congruence is
being used. The degree argument for `I+A` gives
`1+lambda_(p-1)=q^p mod p`: all `p-1` nontrivial coordinates must
occur, hence each occurs once. The sign from subtracting the distance
polynomial cancels Wilson's `-1`.

With one weight-`p-1` inner character and all other inner characters of
weight `h`, the outer eigenvalue is

\[
 1-\binom{h-1}{p}+a\binom{h-1}{p-1}
 =1-c+(a+1)\beta\pmod {p^2}.
\]

Its quotient by `p`, reduced modulo `p`, is `-q^p`, a unit. The
multiplicity counts disjoint character choices and is exactly
`h binom(h,p-1)(q-1)^(h^2-p)`. There is no duplication from the
choice of the distinguished outer coordinate, since weights `p-1`
and `h` differ.

## 2. Factor rank over the non-field ring

The proof correctly avoids reducing ordinary matrix rank modulo `p`,
which would lose the whole exhibited block. In the finite free local
extension, the annihilator of `p` is precisely the ideal `(p)`.
Unit pivots reduce an `M` by `r` factor `U` to

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}I_a&0\\0&pC\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad a=\operatorname{rank}(U\bmod p),
\]

using invertible row and column operations. Since its product with the
other factor is zero modulo `p`, the first `a` rows of that factor
are divisible by `p`. Dividing the product by `p` and reducing gives
rank at most `a+(r-a)=r`. On the other hand, the transformed
`p`-times-unit diagonal block gives an invertible `M` by `M`
matrix after this division and reduction. Thus `r>=M`.

Restricting rows and columns to the exhibited eigenspace cannot increase
factor rank, and extension of scalars preserves any proposed smaller
factorization. This proves the claimed lower bound over `Z/p^2 Z`.

## 3. Reserve and fresh-query consequence

Restricting the rational Gram matrix to labels varying in one inner
word gives the two blocks `J` and `J-(2/p)A`, up to nonzero factors.
The all-nontrivial character space has dimension `(q-1)^h`, is
orthogonal to the constant vector, and has `A` eigenvalue `-c`.
It therefore contributes that dimension to the second block, while
the first contributes one. Hence `D>=1+(q-1)^h`.

For `q>=2h^2`, the two Bernoulli estimates used in the note are valid.
The factor-rank fraction is at least `q^-p`. The reserve condition
`m^alpha>2(D-2)` then implies `q^h<2m^alpha`. Combining them yields

\[
 mr/N>2^{-p/h}m^{1-\alpha p/h}
 \ge\tfrac12m^{(p-1)/(2p-1)}
 \ge\tfrac12m^{2/5}.
\]

The use of `alpha<=1` and `m>1` is justified by the retained contraction
setting and reserve inequality. The lower bound is for this invoice's
fresh-query factor; it is not a lower bound on every implementation's
running time.

## 4. Polynomial lemma and scope

For each nonzero residue `a`, zero values at `a` and `a+p` give
`p f'(a)=0 mod p^2`. The reduction therefore has a double root at
each nonzero residue and is nonzero at zero. This proves the degree
bound `2p-2`. The displayed square `(1-X^(p-1))^2` attains it at
all `2p` specified arguments. This argument depends on ordinary
polynomials over the ring; operations involving division by nonunits
are outside it.

The note states the essential restriction clearly: lifting a small
characteristic-`p` factorization can preserve its factor width while
changing the transfer entries by multiples of `p`. The theorem leaves
open a new inexpensive correction for those transfers. It also leaves
open different labels, divided powers, extra query information and
nonlinear operations. Those exclusions are appropriate.

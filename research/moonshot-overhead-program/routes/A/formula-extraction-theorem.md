## 1. Statement and dependencies

**Theorem F.** The integral full-rate formula construction of
`../B/formula-full-rate-linearization.md`, combined with the accepted
source profiles and physical compiler, gives one fixed deterministic
finite-alphabet multitape integer multiplier with

\[
 T(n)=O\!\left(n\sqrt{L(n)}\,
 2^{\,O\left(s(n)\,
       {\bigl(\log\log s(n)\bigr)^{1/3}
        \over\bigl(\log s(n)\bigr)^{1/6}}\right)}\right),
 \qquad \text{visited cells}=O(n).                    \tag{F1}
\]

This display is for sufficiently large n; the fixed exact fallback handles
all smaller lengths. The constants are absolute. The source's absolute
prime-shape cutoff is retained as a named fixed cutoff, and no numerical
value for it is asserted.

The required dependencies are the integral variant FL4--FL7, the accepted
parameter bounds S1--S13 in `sharper-parameter-growth.md`, the bounded
local-template contract in `physical-compiler-ledger.md`, and the actual
common-cap/restart construction in `scheduler-and-chunking.md`. The proof
below checks that the new representation satisfies that same contract and
that the selected candidate is eligible at its chosen input size.

No fixed-characteristic-only predictor is used in this proof. The integral
formula factorization replaces the original binary evaluator in both the
interchanger and the dyadic Walsh construction. Its identities hold over
the dyadic base and under every odd-characteristic reduction, including
reductions dividing an intermediate factor length.

## 2. The modified finite constructor

For j>=1 set

    J=j+2,   theta_j=1/2+1/J,   rho_j=1/2+1/(2J).

Keep the source label parameter search: prime p in [100J,200J], even
L in [100J,202J], and trials m=2^u+1 with u a power of two. Here the
parameter L is distinct from the input-length function L(n). The complete
interchange coefficient is tested against m^rho_j/2, and the pre-padding
Walsh coefficient against A^rho_j/4. The source uses at most a factor-two
leading-row padding, so these tests leave a half contraction margin.

Let d_pred be the original binary rare-tail predictor degree. At a trial m,

    d_pred=O(sqrt(m log(m+2))).

The balanced coefficient formula is an actual tree with copied
subexpressions. Its syntactic degree is at most d_pred and its size is at
most

    S_m <= [8(d_pred+1)]^{ceil(log2 m)+3}
        = 2^{O((log(m+2))^2)}.                           (F2)

The integral cyclic formula lemma gives d_pred affine pencil stages on
at most d_pred^{S_m} independent rows. This is a whole-identity product;
every row carries its own arbitrary input. Direct-summing the 2^m shifted
predictors gives a common full-rate batch R with

    R <= 2^m d_pred^{S_m}
      <= 2^{2^{C0(log(m+2))^2}}.                         (F3)

Formula architecture and degree budgets are fixed independently of the
shift, including zero subexpressions. The new stage count d_pred is no
greater than the source's old padded stage count b. Four predictor
applications, rare-tail checks, label conversions, reserves and recursive
child volumes therefore satisfy the same complete S1--S8 call invoices.
Only the number of independent scalar rows and finite scalar data change.

The common algebra is

    E=Z[1/2,Z]/Phi_M(Z),
    M=M0*2^a,   M0=lcm(1,...,d_pred),
    2^a the least power of two at least R.

It supplies every intermediate cyclotomic root and a distinct power-of-two
check-node subgroup generated by Z^{M0}. The check differences are units
by explicit source inverse products with denominator two. Since
log2 M0<=d_pred log2(d_pred+1)=o(m), eventually M<=2R^2. The bounded
remaining trial range can use the known source constructor. All basis
coordinates, scalar-multiplication matrices, repair tables and their
generation costs are polynomial in R, by the explicit list-and-arithmetic
proof in FL7. In particular this conclusion is not inferred from the final
output size of an arbitrary machine.

The completed template has dyadic base coefficients. Basis expansion of E
multiplies independent row width and calls by the same rank, preserving the
normalized call ratio. That rank and the resulting batch W need not be
powers of two. The source binary/Walsh caller explicitly permits arbitrary
W through its leading-row padding; the quarter pre-padding margin above
charges its possible factor of two. No replication of an odd-dimensional
module to an exactly dyadic width is assumed.

Consequently all acceptance guards and margins pass by the same bound as
S13:

    log2 m_j <= C1 J^3 log2 J.                           (F4)

Failed trials are bounded by the final accepted trial's explicit envelope,
and each trial constructor terminates. The bounded initial range uses the
known total source construction or the fixed correct fallback.

## 3. The full finite envelope and physical profile

The complete scalar list-and-arithmetic construction is polynomial in R.
The rational label matrices and all their nonzero pivots, factor inverses
and unit scalings have polynomial-m bit heights. Their numeric address
magnitudes and the compatible address prime are bounded by 2^{m^c}, for a
fixed c, exactly as in `rational-address-and-generator-bounds.md`. The
quantity 2^{C(log(m+2))^2} eventually dominates m^c and every fixed power
of log R. Thus one common envelope satisfies

    P_m <= 2^{2^{C2(log(m+2))^2}}.                        (F5)

It bounds finite instruction/list sizes, numeric address data and radix,
numerical scalar coefficient bit heights, basis-expanded alphabets,
retained descriptions, actual generator time and actual generator space.
All finite verification loops, including all 2^m frequency tuples, also
fit this envelope after an absolute enlargement of C2.

The accepted physical compiler is polynomial in precisely these finite
quantities. It therefore gives absolute K and U_abs such that the modified
candidate has generator time g_j, retained/work space d_j, and interpreted
time and proportional-space coefficients a_j,c_j satisfying

    g_j,d_j,a_j,c_j <= P_j^K,
    tau_j(v) <= a_j v L(v)^theta_j,
    space_j(v) <= c_j v+d_j,             for v>=U_j,
    U_j <= max{U_abs,2^{P_j^K}},
    P_j=P_(m_j).                                         (F6)

As in the accepted compiler, the visible log^4 L factor is absorbed using
theta_j-rho_j=1/(2J); its polynomial reciprocal-slack coefficient fits P_j.
The finite processor is interpreted on the same fixed number of tapes and
fixed alphabet, with all scalar/base conversions charged. Packed records,
guard counters and sorted exceptional events, ordinary setup arithmetic,
numerical precision and amplification, CRT and carry assembly retain the
same costs. The larger finite algebra does not introduce an uncharged
unit-cost operation.

The source's prime-shape cutoff U_abs is independent of j. Exact shape
checks and a correct fallback make every candidate total and correct at
all lengths, without the need to know a numerical value for U_abs.

Combining (F4)--(F6), and increasing one absolute constant C, yields

    log2 P_j,
    log2(g_j+d_j+a_j+c_j+2),
    log2 L(U_j) <= 2^{C J^6 (log2 J)^2} =: E_J.           (F7)

Indeed (log2(m_j+2))^2=O(J^6(log2 J)^2), while
log2 L(2^{P_j^K})=O(1+log2 P_j). Fixed powers and the one fixed absolute
cutoff are absorbed by C. This removes one logarithmic level from the
old literal-monomial envelope; it does not assert a numerical start point.

## 4. Common physical cap and restart costs

Keep exactly the previously proved common-cap and restart mechanism. Use
sparse J=2^h, h>=2, so j=J-2. For this candidate take r_j to be the least
power of two at least 4c_j, and set

    N_j=ceil(max{8c_j,4(c_j+d_j),r_j U_j}).                (F8)

For n>=N_j the chunk length v=ceil(n/r_j) satisfies v>=U_j and
c_j v+d_j<=n/2. The separately charged diagonal product assembly, input,
output, schedule and cleanup use absolute O(n) visited cells. Its time is

    t_j(n)<=g_j+C3(a_j+1)c_j n L(n)^theta_j.              (F9)

This includes r_j^2 chunk products and O(nr_j) physical assembly, with
amortized carries and exact 2n-bit output. Generation remains inside each
timed attempt and its retained workspace is included in d_j.

Diagonal enumeration of (h,k), where r=2^k, gives attempt index
i<=(h+k+1)^2. The proved prefix-weight restart schedule costs
C4[n+i(1+log2 i)^2(t+1)] for a successful t-step attempt. Since
k_j=O(1+log2 c_j), this schedule factor is polynomial in log P_j and h.
The full generator, chunk, schedule and cleanup costs consequently give

    T(n)<=C5 n sqrt(L(n)) [1+D_j L(n)^{1/J}],
                                         for n>=N_j,
    log2 D_j, log2 L(N_j) <= E_J.                        (F10)

Here C in E_J has been increased once more. The estimate for N_j follows
directly from (F8) and (F6), including the additive generator workspace and
the absolute cutoff. The separate schoolbook attempt ensures the same
fixed machine terminates for every input length. Failed/aborted attempts
never publish an answer and never cross the common marked physical cap.

## 5. Selecting an actually eligible sparse candidate

Put s=log2 L(n), z=log2 s. For sufficiently large s define

    Y=[z/(2C(log2 z)^2)]^{1/6},
    h=floor(log2 Y),   J=2^h,   j=J-2.                  (F11)

Then h>=2, Y/2<J<=Y and log2 J<=log2 z. Therefore

    C J^6(log2 J)^2 <= z/2,
    E_J <= 2^{z/2}=sqrt(s).                             (F12)

The threshold estimate in (F10) gives
log2 L(N_j)<=sqrt(s)<s=log2 L(n); hence n>=N_j. This proves eligibility
of the parameter selected for the final bound.

Also sqrt(s)=o(s/Y), because Y grows at most as a fixed power of log s.
Equation (F10), with exponent excess exactly 1/J, now gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log_2 H(n)
 &\le O(1)+\log_2 D_j+s/J\\
 &\le O(1)+\sqrt{s}+2s/Y\\
 &=O\!\left(s\,{(\log\log s)^{1/3}
                         \over(\log s)^{1/6}}\right).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{F13}
\]

This proves (F1), with the finite-constructor and composition gates accepted.
The selected J is used in the analysis; the single fixed machine discovers
successful attempts through its already specified schedule.

## 6. Meaning and remaining separation

The new logarithmic-overhead scale is asymptotically smaller than the
earlier accepted extraction scale

    s [log log log s / log log s]^{1/3}.

This improvement comes from replacing the source's flat monomial tensor
evaluator by a graded tensor factorization of an explicit balanced formula.
The tensor operation itself is established mathematics; its attribution is
recorded in B's note. The programme contribution is its full-rate use for
these predictors and the complete quantitative constructor/compiler chain.

This is an explicit improvement over the programme's previous upper
envelope. It is not a comparison with an unspecified published o(1). The
displayed upper-bound scale for log H grows faster than s^beta for every
fixed beta<1; this statement does not lower-bound the actual overhead. It does
not establish the polynomial-s benchmark, a positive benchmark exponent,
or constant overhead. The stronger compact-integral-predictor hypothesis
remains open: the present formula construction has quasi-polynomial
formula size and does not meet its singly-exponential-in-polynomial-m
finite-description promise.

The formula lemma also lies outside the scope of the Chow compression
barrier: a nested sum/product formula is converted through graded matrix
factorizations, not compressed into a short flat sum of products. Thus the
new positive result and that restricted obstruction are compatible.

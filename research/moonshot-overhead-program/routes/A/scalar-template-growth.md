## 1. Clifford dimension

Take binary queries (ell=2) and sufficiently large m so the source prediction
degree D satisfies D<=m. A bounded initial range can be handled separately.
Each query contributes `1+I_j T` to the count generating function, where
`I_j=(1-(-1)^beta_j q_j)/2`. The predictor is therefore multilinear in the
m variables q_j. After collecting terms it has at most

    h <= 2^m

monomials, with dyadic numerator/denominator bit lengths O(m^2). This follows
directly by expanding the source degree-D Newton expression: there are at
most polynomial-m many outer summands, binomial coefficients have O(m) bits
when |u|,D<=m, and each product of at most m affine indicators has denominator
at most 2^m. Padding zero terms gives a common monomial count for all shifts.

Let b be the least power of two at least max(2,D), so b<=2m. The source
matrix-root construction has dimension

    d0=b^h,  log2 d0<=2^m log2(2m)<=2^{2m}.                (G1)

Using every shift gives R=2^m d0 independent scalar rows. Thus

    R <= 2^{2^{2m+1}}.                                   (G2)

R is a power of two. The source cyclotomic extension can take a primitive
R-th root eta, with degree d=R/2 over Z[1/2]. It contains the required b-th
root. Descent to the dyadic base multiplies rows/calls by d, so the expanded
row count is at most R^2. The same scalar ring can be reduced modulo every
odd data prime p for interchange.

## 2. Heights of cyclotomic inverses

Represent ring elements as dyadic polynomials of degree <d in eta, using
eta^d=-1. For a nontrivial 2-power root rho of order 2^a<=R,

    product_(v odd mod 2^a) (1-rho^v)=2.

Therefore `(1-rho)^(-1)` is one half the product with the factor v=1
omitted. It has at most d-1 factors. Before or after reducing by eta^d=-1,
its numerator coefficient l1 norm is at most 2^d and its denominator is 2.
An inverse difference of two distinct node powers is the same expression
times a unit monomial. No factoring of a finite-field polynomial or
unproved generic ring inversion is required.

For a j-column exceptional Vandermonde matrix, j<=R, the Lagrange numerator
uses j-1 factors with unit-monomial roots. The inverse node differences use
j-1 of the products above. Every inverse entry therefore has a dyadic
representation whose numerator bit lengths are O(jd+j) and whose denominator
is a power of two with exponent O(j). Products and sums used in binary
Hermite repair keep numerator/denominator bit lengths O(R^2+m^2).

The actual predictor multiplier at a sign pattern is an integer of
O(m^2) bits. The binary Hermite factor `D_z(p)=-(p+2z)/2` adds no new
large denominator. This verifies that the source's full finite coefficient
list can be produced with polynomial-in-R coefficient bit lengths.

## 3. Constructing the entire finite scalar list

All matrices can be generated by explicit loops:

1. Enumerate 2^m shifts and expand/collect the multilinear predictor.
2. Construct the cyclic/diagonal tensor factors in the matrix-root lemma,
   and explicitly list their affine-pencil coefficients.
3. Enumerate the 2^m binary frequency patterns, determine their exceptional
   row sets, and construct Lagrange inverses using the products in Section 2.
4. Build the Hermite tables, then expand each cyclotomic coefficient in the
   eta basis and, for the normalized Walsh variant, descend to dyadic row
   matrices as in the source binary proposition.

Each finite loop count, matrix dimension and polynomial degree is bounded by
a fixed power of R. Ring polynomial multiplication and exact integer
arithmetic on the O(R^2+m^2)-bit coefficients can use schoolbook algorithms.
Consequently the output description, generation time and generation workspace
of **this scalar part** are bounded by R^C for an absolute fixed C.
This includes listing all frequency tables; they are not an oracle.

For reduction modulo odd p, reduce dyadic coefficients using the inverse of
2 modulo p. The finite algebra F_p[Y]/(Y^d+1) has d coordinates; multiplication
by a fixed element is an explicitly given linear map on those coordinates.
Using fixed-length binary blocks and ordinary modular arithmetic costs
poly(R,log p), instead of treating the p^d possible elements as a flat
truth-table alphabet. The source permits this fixed binary encoding because
the number of coordinates is fixed for a chosen template.

## 4. Limits and next integration

The bound R^C is extremely large, but explicit and elementary. The degree-D
linearization is not being called compact. To use it globally, also bound
the routing labels, rational address bases and scalings, compatible prime
radix, one-level tape scan list, and recurrence margins. Those are separate
pieces. The final primitive processor coefficient must depend polynomially
on this expanded data and on the *numeric magnitudes* of rational address
scalings; their bit lengths alone do not pay for all monotone pieces.

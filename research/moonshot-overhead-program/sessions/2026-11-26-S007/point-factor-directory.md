# Joining the two actual affine trace planes to the individual FS1 factors

Let $b=3c$, $q=2^b$, $a=2^c$, $K=\mathbf F_{2^b}$ and $L=\mathbf F_{2^c}$. For every stored representative $\alpha$ of $L/(\mathbf F_2,\mathrm{Frob})$ take both planes of the relative trace $z\mapsto T(z)$ with values $\alpha$ and $\alpha+1$. Each is an affine copy of $L^2$. Give each point its serial index in the prescribed plane order and its actual $b$-bit value $y=f(z)$. There are $J=2Ra^2=O(q/b)$ records by the stored-orbit count. The following adapter returns the compact binary residues $f\bmod u$ in the literal FS1 order by degree and Lyndon word. Its inverse accepts all $q$ independent residue bits and returns every original geometric occurrence in serial order. On ordinary fixed tapes it takes, for any fixed rational $0<\eta<1/2$,

$$O_\eta\!\left(\frac{qb}{(1+\log\log(b+3))^\eta}\right)\text{ steps and }O_\eta(q)\text{ cells ever visited}.$$

The local algebra takes $O(qb^{7/10}+q\sqrt b\,\operatorname{polylog}(b+2)+\operatorname{poly}(b)+2^c\operatorname{poly}(b))$ steps before sorting, allowing an additional $O(q\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ for integer control. This proof assumes the actual roots' point values have been produced. It leaves the cost of that evaluation and higher-precision lifting with their respective routines.

## The representative points cover every binary factor

Relative trace commutes with Frobenius. Given any $x\in K$, the class of $T(x)$ modulo complementation has a chosen representative $\alpha$. Hence a Frobenius power of $x$ occurs on one of these two planes, so every irreducible factor of $X^q-X$ occurs. On any Frobenius orbit of length $d\mid b$, the point value belongs to $\mathbf F_2(z)$, since $f$ is binary. Conjugate pairs $(z,y)$ describe the same polynomial residue. Different points in the chosen stream can represent the same factor, including on proper subfields. We keep every geometric occurrence on return. At either endpoint a fixed $L$-basis $v,w$ of $\ker T$ gives $z=\alpha+\epsilon+v\ell+wt$. Build the two lists of its binary-basis directions once, then enumerate an ordered plane by bit counters and a running field sum. An increment changes a mean of fewer than two directions. Each costs a scan and rewind of $O(b)$ bits. Alternatively, separate ordered field-offset lists for the two axes can be generated once and reused in nested scans. The outer normalization and representative tests cost $2^c\operatorname{poly}(b)$ as in S006.

## Deterministic recovery of the minimum polynomial and its residue

Write $K=\mathbf F_2[Z]/F_b$, with degree-$b$ monic $F_b$, and let $\Lambda$ take the constant bit of the power-basis representative. Thus $\Lambda(1)=1$ in every permitted ambient field. At $(z,y)$ compute

$$s_j=\Lambda(z^j)\quad(0\le j<2b),\qquad t_j=\Lambda(yz^j)\quad(0\le j<b).$$

Write $M$ for the minimal polynomial of $z$, of degree $d\le b$. The infinite sequence $s$ has minimal polynomial $M$. Indeed, an annihilating polynomial $P$ satisfies $\Lambda(z^jP(z))=0$ for every $j$. If $P(z)\ne0$, multiplying the span $\mathbf F_2[z]$ by $P(z)$ gives the same field, and this would force $\Lambda(1)=0$. Conversely $M(z)=0$ gives the recurrence. Thus the Laurent series $S=\sum_{j\ge0}s_jY^{-j-1}=A/M$ has $\deg A<d$ and $\gcd(A,M)=1$. A common factor would shorten the recurrence. Notice that $\Lambda$ is deliberately nonzero on every subfield. The unweighted absolute trace would vanish on subfields of even relative degree.

Use the available $2b$ moments with $P_0=Y^{2b}$ and $G=\sum_{j<2b}s_jY^{2b-1-j}$. Compute the first Euclidean remainder $R$ of $(P_0,G)$ satisfying $\deg R<b$, counting zero as degree $-\infty$, and its coefficient row

$$R=U P_0+V G.$$

Then **$V=M$ and $U=A$**. The predecessor has degree at least $b$, so the degree identity for successive Euclidean coefficient rows gives $\deg V=2b-\deg R_{\rm prev}\le b$. The same formula holds for a final zero row. Truncation of $S$ implies $E=MG+A P_0$ has degree below $d$. The identity $(UM+VA)P_0=MR+VE$ now has right-hand degree less than $2b$, so $UM=VA$. A Euclidean transformation is unimodular, whence $\gcd(U,V)=1$. Together with $\gcd(A,M)=1$ this proves the claims, since the binary scalar unit is one. This argument also handles $d<b$ and highly irregular Euclidean degree drops. At $z=0$, $s=(1,0,\ldots)$, $G=Y^{2b-1}$, and the first qualifying row has $R=0$, $U=1$, $V=Y$.

If $y=r(z)$ for its unique binary representative $\deg r<d$, put $T_y=\sum_{j\ge0}t_jY^{-j-1}$. Multiplying $S$ by $r(Y)$ and discarding its polynomial part gives exactly $T_y$, coefficient by coefficient. Hence, with $A_t$ the polynomial part of $M\sum_{j<d}t_jY^{-j-1}$,

$$r=A_t A^{-1}\bmod M.$$

The omitted moments do not affect the polynomial part, because their highest degree after multiplication is $-1$. The only inverse is in the small binary polynomial quotient. This gives a deterministic power-projection and inverse modular composition construction on all subfields, including both linear factors. The underlying power-projection method is due to Brent–Kung and Shoup. [Neiger, Salvy, Schost and Villard, *Faster Modular Composition*, §§1.4.2 and 1.5](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~eschost/publications/NSSV24.pdf)

## Tape price of the moments, Padé and reverse evaluation

Set $w=\lceil\sqrt{2b}\rceil$. Form the $w$ words $B_i=z^i$ and the giant powers $G_j=(z^w)^j$ for $0\le i,j<w$. Produce $yG_j$ as well. Each short field product or reduction costs $O(b\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ actual tape steps by binary coefficient guard packing, linear-time scans and a constant number of small integer multiplications of $O(b\log(b+2))$ bits, as in FS1. This charges $O(b^{3/2}\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ for the powers. Precompute the $2b-1$ bits $v_i=\Lambda(Z^i\bmod F_b)$ once. For a power-basis word $D=\sum D_u Z^u$, its linear functional $x\mapsto\Lambda(Dx)$ has coordinate row $h_v=\sum_{u<b}D_u v_{u+v}$, a fixed binary Hankel product. It costs one short binary polynomial multiplication and extraction of the middle coefficients. Apply this to $G_j$ and $yG_j$.

Multiply each $w\times b$ array of such rows by the $b\times w$ array of $B_i$ bits. The output at $(j,i)$ is precisely $s_{wj+i}$ or $t_{wj+i}$. Pad $b$ to a multiple of $w$ and split the middle dimension into $\lceil b/w\rceil$ consecutive square products. The fixed binary rank scheme of exponent $12/5$ takes $O((b/w)w^{12/5})=O(b^{17/10})$ steps. Its marked square work banks and bounded-size bilinear formulas work on one-head tapes as in the grouped-ambient-word proof. Converting the tall or wide arrays into tile-major order costs $O(bw\log(b+2))$ by ordinary sort of $O(\log b)$-bit addresses on the scalar entries using fixed tape passes, or $O(bw\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ even with finite binary headers. No field-word address lookup is a unit operation. [Fixed bilinear scheme](grouped-ambient-words.md)

The ordinary truncated polynomial products and extended Euclidean rows have length $O(b)$. The standard polynomial half-GCD computes the product of the Euclidean step matrices down to the degree cutoff, and extended GCD supplies $A^{-1}\bmod M$. Its divide-and-conquer algorithm over $\mathbf F_2$ uses a constant number of polynomial products, division-with-remainder, $2\times2$ polynomial matrix operations, and contiguous leading-part truncations per node, with two recursive calls on at most half degrees up to fixed rounding. Polynomial division by a current divisor uses reversed series inversion by Newton doubling, with linear coefficient scans and short products. All are physical contiguous-word operations with $O(b\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ time for a node of root-size $b$. Reusing near-origin work banks depth-first and saving only the ancestor arrays needed by its fixed number of children implements the recursion with $O(b\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ total time after raising the fixed logarithmic exponent. The first transition across a threshold, including a possible zero remainder, is retained; testing a degree costs a linear scan of the present word. Fixed small degrees use literal division. The finite replay below uses the ordinary Euclidean loop as an independent reference for the returned cofactors. [Van der Hoeven, *Optimizing the half-gcd algorithm*, §§2 and 5](https://www.texmacs.org/joris/gcd/gcd.pdf)

On return from FS1, $r(z)$ uses the same baby powers. Place the coefficients of $r$ in a $w\times w$ binary array, one row per consecutive coefficient block, with zero padding. Multiplication against the $w\times b$ array of $B_i$ gives all block values by $O(b/w)$ square products. Horner in $z^w$ combines them with $O(w)$ short field multiplications. This costs $O(b^{17/10}+b^{3/2}\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ for each point. Generating its key $M$ when only $z$ is input uses its $s$ moments as above. All local work banks and descriptors have polynomial size in $b$ and are reused for every point.

## Literal FS1 order and the whole-machine invoice

Use exactly the field, normal basis, degree ordering and normal-word bit convention of the FS1 compiler for each $d\mid b$. These can differ from the ambient field. Run its Duval enumeration in its original order. A next word can be formed on sequential tapes by periodically copying the present word to length $d$, returning the read head to its origin after each complete copy. Scan back over trailing ones, truncate them and flip the preceding zero if it exists. A marker at the target length and a marked current-word end suffice. Rewind the surviving prefixes. This takes $O(d)$ steps per produced word, also counting discarded shorter words. The total number of Lyndon words produced for this $d$ is $O(2^d/d)$, so enumeration costs $O(2^d)$.

For the retained length-$d$ words batch $d$ consecutive rows. Multiply their binary normal-coordinate matrix by the compiler's fixed normal-to-power matrix using the same $12/5$ scheme. The resulting per-word cost is $O(d^{7/5})$, with a padded last batch of cost $O(d^{12/5})$. Recover the minimum polynomial in the compiler's own field by the $s$-only moments and Padé procedure above at parameter $d$. Append the resulting monic polynomial and its serial rank. The result is the *same directory* as FS1, since the earlier construction multiplies the same conjugate factors in the same word order. Its total cost is $O(qb^{7/10}+q\sqrt b\operatorname{polylog}(b+2)+\operatorname{poly}(b))$. For proper $d\mid b$ one may bound the aggregate by $2^{b/2}\operatorname{poly}(b)$. Each field and normal-basis preprocessing is polynomial in $b$ as in the referenced FS1 setup. [FS1 order and original construction](../2026-11-26-S005/route-a/binary-factor-stream-compiler.md)

For the forward join, generate a point record $(M,r,\mathrm{serial})$ and one directory record $(u,\mathrm{FS1Rank})$ per factor. As binary monic polynomial bit strings $u$ and $M$ agree independently of field presentations. Sort by that string and record type; each group has exactly one directory row and at least one point. All its actual point residues agree, so emit one copy with the FS1 rank. Sort the replies by this rank and serialize precisely their degree-many increasing coefficient bits. For the inverse, read arbitrary residues with the FS1 directory in its existing order. Tag the generated point positions by $(M,z,\mathrm{serial})$. Sort the union by the same polynomial key with the directory record first, stream its residue to every matching point, evaluate by the reverse procedure, then sort by the geometric serial number. This returns the stated initial plane order, even where the same factor has several points in the input. The sum of the exported degrees is exactly $q$.

The number of distinct factors is between $q/b$ and $O(q/b)$, by counting full and proper orbits. Both joins handle $O(q/b)$ fixed-width $\Theta(b)$-bit records after padding keys, ranks and payloads, hence volume $O(q)$. Tal's Corollary 6.2 uses $u=\log N$, $\beta=\Omega(\log N)$, and costs $O(Vu/\min(u,\log^{(2)}\beta)^\eta)$ on such records. Here $u=\Theta(b)$ and $\beta=\Theta(b)$. The saving is $(1+\log\log(b+3))^\eta$. The improved tape interchange supplies every fixed rational $\eta<1/2$ in Tal's $\eta=1-\tau$ parametrization. There are a constant number of complete sorts. Each sort and every outer stream lives in fixed reusable $O(q)$ marked intervals; polynomial-$b$ local work and the $2^c\operatorname{poly}(b)$ representative setup are absorbed. This proves the displayed time and cumulative-space claims. [Tal, Corollary 6.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/331/download/) · [Improved tape interchange](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/interchange.tex)

## Independent finite evidence

The [checker](verify-factor-directory.py) builds genuinely different ambient and FS1 field presentations where possible. It enumerates the selected two-plane set and every FS1 factor, including zero and proper subfields. Starting with independent FS1 residues, it verifies them by an ordinary global CRT reconstruction, evaluates ordinary polynomials directly at sampled points, checks every geometric occurrence by independent conjugate products and ordinary division, and compares the exact output order in both directions. It separately starts from a new ordinary input polynomial. Its moment core uses actual Hankel multiplication, rectangular binary dot products, threshold Euclidean cofactors and blocked Horner. Across degrees $b=3,6,9,12$ it traces 2,376 point occurrences through 430 factors, with 1,946 duplicate occurrences, 84 proper-subfield points, 4,680 independent bits in each initial direction, and 3,568 additional exhaustive legal $(z,y)$ pairs in the two smallest ambient fields. Sorting factors by polynomial value instead of the exact FS1 order changes 412 list entries. The zero-remainder Padé exit is exercised at every degree. This finite program checks the output of the fast square-product and half-GCD calls against exact elementary arithmetic; it does not benchmark their large-parameter implementations.

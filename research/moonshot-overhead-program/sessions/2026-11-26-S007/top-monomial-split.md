# A cancellation requirement in the first monomial remainder split

The grouped encoding supplies the actual top-coset polynomial in monomial order. A natural way to avoid a length-$v$ change to the additive product basis is to keep these coordinates and split the ordinary polynomial directly into the two child remainders. The first such split already needs $\Omega(qb)$ bounded-fan-in bit gates if dependencies on entire input records are never cancelled. This is a restriction on this particular representation and circuit class. It allows arbitrary shared operations within one input record and arbitrary binary encodings of those words.

Take $b=3c$, $q=2^b$, $v=2^{2c+1}$, $h=v/2$, and the S006 additive flag inside $V=\ker S$, with $S=T^2+T$ and $T=X+X^{2^c}+X^{2^{2c}}$. Let $U=W_{2c}$ be its top proper subspace, so $1\in U$, and write $L(X)=\prod_{u\in U}(X-u)$. Its degree is $h$. For the chosen complementary direction $\beta$, put $\Delta=L(\beta)\ne0$. S006's subspace-polynomial recurrence gives $S=L^2+\Delta L$, and $L$ is linearized.

**Every admissible top split has all $2c$ nonleading offsets, with at most one exception, for $c\ge2$.** If $\Delta=1$, then $(L+T)^2+(L+T)=0$. The polynomial ring is a domain and both polynomials vanish at zero, so $L=T$. This contradicts $L(1)=0$ and $T(1)=1$. Write $\lambda_i=[X^{2^i}]L$. Comparing the $X$ and $X^2$ coefficients of $S$ gives $\lambda_0=\Delta^{-1}$ and $\lambda_1=(1+\Delta^{-2})/\Delta\ne0$. For $2\le i<c$, the coefficient of $X^{2^i}$ in $S$ is zero, so $\lambda_i=\lambda_{i-1}^2/\Delta\ne0$. Hence every $i$ from 0 through $c-1$ occurs, independently of the remaining basis directions. Also $\lambda_{2c}=1$, so comparison at $X^{2^{2c}}$ gives $\lambda_{2c-1}^2=1+\Delta\ne0$. Recursing backwards through the zero coefficients of $S$ gives $\lambda_i\ne0$ for $c<i<2c$. Only $i=c$ can be missing. In particular the sparse hyperplane $\ker T$ is inadmissible because it omits the first prescribed direction $1$.

The single possible zero is characterized exactly. Let $t=\Delta^{-2}$, $\phi(z)=z^2$, $\tau=\phi^c$ and $\ell_i=\lambda_i/\Delta$. If $a_i=[X^{2^i}]S$, then $\ell_i=\ell_{i-1}^2+a_i t$ with $\ell_{-1}=0$. Summing over $0\le i\le2c$ telescopes the contributions of the support pairs $(0,1),(c,c+1),(2c,2c+1)$, giving $0=L(1)/\Delta=t+\tau t+\tau^2 t$. Also $\ell_c=t+\phi^{c-1}t+\tau t$. Squaring and using this trace identity shows $\ell_c=0$ exactly when $\phi\tau^2t=\tau t$, equivalently $\phi\tau t=t$. Thus $t$ must lie in the subfield of degree $\gcd(3c,c+1)=\gcd(3,c+1)$. Since $t\ne0,1$, the exception is impossible unless $c\equiv2\pmod3$. In the latter case only the three nonzero trace-zero elements of $\mathbf F_8$ can furnish exceptional directions. This classification is supplementary to the bound below.

**The actual output on an independent subspace.** For each stored representative take $s=S(\alpha)$ and $E_s=\mathbf F_2(s)$ of degree $d_s$. The grouped boundary lets every parent be an arbitrary polynomial in $E_s[X]_{<v}$ independently at each representative. Restrict it to $F=X^h Q$, where $Q$ has degree below $h/2$, with arbitrary independent $E_s$ words precisely at indices $j\equiv0\pmod 3$ and zeroes elsewhere. Since every nonleading term of $L$ has degree $\le h/2$, ordinary division by the monic polynomial $L(X)-L(\alpha)$ has quotient **exactly $Q$**. The low child (the original anchor) has ordinary remainder
$$
  R=(L-X^h-L(\alpha))Q .
$$
The signs coincide in characteristic two. Observe only indices $n\equiv1\pmod3$ with $2^c\le n<h/2$. There are $N_c=\Theta(v)$ of them. No constant term contributes to these outputs. The support in independently variable input *records* of $R_n$ consists only of indices $n-2^i$ with $i$ even. For every even $0\le i<c$ this index is present, since $2^i\equiv1\pmod3$ and $\lambda_i\ne0$. Each present index independently injects its $d_s$ bits into the output field word by nonzero scalar multiplication. Any two distinct observed rows have at most one common input record. Indeed $2^i-2^j$, for $i>j$, determines $j$ by its 2-adic valuation and then determines $i$. Truncating a set of powers of two or dropping zero coefficients preserves this property.

**Circuit class and counting argument.** A Boolean circuit here has one-bit wires, gates of fan-in at most two and arbitrary fan-out. Constants and arbitrary single-record preprocessing are allowed for free. For the restricted input/output map, require that a gate reaching any output bit of a word $R_n$ has input-record ancestors only among the actual input-record support of $R_n$. This is true, in particular, for a record-support-monotone circuit for the full monomial split: after fixing all unselected input records to zero and removing constant-only subcircuits, the support of each full output word intersects the selected input set in its restricted support, by linearity on independent parent words. A circuit whose paths carry other input records and cancel their effects later is outside the class. The condition permits cancellation and nonlinear computation *inside* an allowed record set. It imposes no uniformity or tape assumption on the choice of circuit.

Call a gate mixed if at least two independent selected input records are among its ancestors. No mixed gate can feed bits of two different observed output words, by the pairwise intersection bound. This holds across different representatives as well, whose input records are disjoint. Fix one output $n$ and a contributing record $j$. Hold all other inputs fixed. As the $j$th input word varies, $R_n$ takes $2^{d_s}$ distinct values. The wires from single-record-$j$ ancestors entering mixed gates in the subcircuit for $n$, together with any directly singleton-$j$ output bits of $n$, must therefore carry at least $d_s$ bits. Cutting these wires separates every path from $j$ to the observed output. This argument allows arbitrary shared computation of those single-input signals.

Let $M_n$ be the number of mixed gates feeding this output. Summing cut-edge upper bounds over all contributing records counts at most $2M_n+b$ wires, since there are at most two inputs per mixed gate and $b$ direct output bits. Thus
$$
  2M_n+b\ \ge\ \lceil c/2\rceil d_s.
$$
The mixed gate sets are disjoint across the observed output words. The total required number is at least
$$
  \frac{N_c}{2}\bigl(\lceil c/2\rceil\sum_s d_s-bR_{\rm orb}\bigr)=\Omega(qb).
$$
Here $\sum_s d_s=2^{c-1}$, $v\sum_s d_s=q$ and $bvR_{\rm orb}=O(q)$ by the established orbit count. This is an actual bit-gate lower bound, even if all control, initial single-record injections and head movements are free. It extends the simpler $\Omega(qb)$ invoice for explicitly XORing a full $b$-bit translated word at every nonzero stencil location. A fixed-head implementation which executes this type of straight-line circuit at a bounded number of finite-alphabet gates per step inherits the obstruction. Arbitrary tape algorithms, input-dependent access, cancellations between other source records, and a change of output basis remain possible. There is no lower bound here on binary CRT or on multiplication.

The relationship between distinct differences and rectangle-free incidence matrices, and rectangle-free lower bounds for support-preserving calculations, are classical. The word-level information count above gives the required $d_s$-bit contribution for each incidence in this field-valued instance.

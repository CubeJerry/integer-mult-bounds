# Independent review of the modulo-four compiler and first carry

The [wide modulo-four multiplication](mod-four-batch.md) and its [one-split application to the first literal carry](first-witt-carry.md) give $O(qb)$ steps and $O(q)$ visited cells for the forward and inverse FS1 transform at precision two when $q=2^b$ and $3\mid b$.

**Lifting the Pascal word.** In the kernel of reduction modulo two, $(I+2M)(I+2N)=I+2(M+N)$. Over the residue field of size 16, conjugating a diagonal projection by the three-point Fourier matrix gives a matrix whose nine entries on that triple are all nonzero. Each ordered off-diagonal position has a different character on the residue diagonal torus; reversing an ordered pair gives a different character since the multiplicative group has order 15. Summing conjugates against the inverse character isolates a single matrix unit, with nonzero normalization. Changing the triple and retaining the ordinary diagonals gives all sixteen directions. Lifting the conjugations and multiplying the corresponding $I+2M$ terms corrects the actual four-coordinate word exactly. The source ternary kernel lives over $\mathbf Z[1/3,\zeta_3]$. A free finite rank repair module retains that rank after scalar extension, and an inverse transform uses the ring unit $3^{-j}$. The direct-sum sector layout and striped recursion are unchanged except for fixed-size record contents. [Universal ternary identity and specialization, lines 10–24, 283–291](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/kernel.tex#L10-L24)

**Large extension and local words.** A monic lift of a binary irreducible gives a free unramified extension of $\mathbf Z/4$. Lifting an odd-order residue root by one Newton correction preserves its exact order and all unit differences between distinct nodes. The geometric triangular factorization only uses these differences and diagonal units. A nonzero nilpotent shear coefficient $\lambda$ is $1+(\lambda-1)$, a sum of two units. This also covers nonunit entries encountered inside a radix triple's row operations. Every unit shear is diagonal-conjugate to the ordinary one. To enforce an inactive pair with two global calls, use $U_1U_{-1}=I$. The active choice $U_\omega U_{1-\omega}=U_1$ has both parameters units. The fixed ring used by the four-coordinate Fourier word can act on each pair of base bits independently; it need not embed into the large extension.

**Widths and the outer split.** For a requested batch $J\ge D^\gamma$, choose the fixed number $k$ of native prime axes per group at least $\max(k_{\rm source},4/\gamma)$. Take between $k$ and $2k-1$ axes in each block. Then $G\le (\log D)/(k\log(CT/2))$, $T=\lceil\log_2(D+2)\rceil$, and $\log_2\prod_gM_g\le G\log_2(2G)\le(1/k+o(1))\log_2 D$. Thus the residual lane width is at least $D^{\gamma/2}$, with polynomial-$T$ local tables and constant accumulated padding. This width matters at the first Cantor–Kaltofen split: its $2n$ products have length $s=\varphi(m)$, with $2n=s$ or $s/3$. Their total operand volume is exactly $2\varphi(M)$, while the global transforms and digit routing cost $O(\varphi(M)\log M)$. The short modulo-four products inside the batched compiler use the ordinary triadic recurrence and its independent $O(s\log s\log\log s)$ bound. This avoids any assumption of the single-product bound used at the top.

**One-time factor generation and inverse inputs.** The binary FS1 directory and its literal degree/Lyndon order are already priced by the binary transform. Generate the two local inverses and the lift constants by compact half-GCD and packed squares, in $O(d\operatorname{polylog}(d+2))$ per degree-$d$ factor and $O(q\operatorname{polylog}(b+2))$ total. All binary CRT uses are sequential on reusable $O(q)$ intervals. For arbitrary input residues $r+2s$, the inverse recovers $F_0=E^{-1}r$, then $F_1=E^{-1}(s+K(F_0))$. The monomial basis of every proper subfield is the specified one. The short-record directory sort is part of each binary CRT invoice.

**Mixed-ring check.** The independent [test program](check-mod-four-jets.py) works over $(\mathbf Z/4)[t]/(t^4+t+1)[z]/z^3$, using a genuine order-15 root for a $3\times5$ transform. It compared 2,880 field-basis coefficient positions of dense nonfield jets against direct cyclic convolution, 2,880 ordinary packed monomial pairs and 32 dense arbitrary coefficient pairs against signed integer schoolbook multiplication: 136,864 ordinary output coefficients. All 65,536 (scalar, source) cases for the two-unit shear passed, including all 15 nonzero nilpotent scalars. Omitting the inverse scale failed on each dense packed case.

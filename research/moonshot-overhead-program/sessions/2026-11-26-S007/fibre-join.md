# Joining the two relative-trace fibres

Let $b=3c$, $N=2^c$, $q=N^3$, $a=N^2$, $K=\mathbf F_{2^b}$, $L=\mathbf F_{2^c}$. Use the [S006 section](../2026-11-26-S006/stable-four-point-bundles.md) of $L/\mathbf F_2$ and its least binary representative $\alpha$ modulo Frobenius and complementation. Here $T=X^{a}+X^N+X$ and $S=T^2+T$. This note begins at the ordinary base-$T$ digits, in order $[j<N][k<a]$, and ends with true monomial coefficients of every selected $\alpha+V$, in increasing $k<2a$. The base-$T$ conversion from the actual depth-$r$ input and the monomial-to-additive-product basis at the other end retain their separate bills.

## Exact two-fibre formula

Write $f(X)=\sum_{k<a}X^k p_k(T(X))$, $\deg p_k<N$. The $a$ smaller full-field binary CRT inputs have total length $aN=q$, with no zero-filled second half. A child supplies ordinary polynomial remainders of $p_k$ modulo each degree-dividing-$c$ irreducible, in the [FS1](../2026-11-26-S005/route-a/binary-factor-stream-compiler.md) degree/Lyndon order. Let $A_k=p_k(\alpha)$ and $B_k=p_k(\alpha+1)$. Let $A,B$ denote these degree-$<a$ polynomials in $X$, and $Q=A+B$. The representative monomial polynomial is

$$R=A+(T+\alpha)Q.$$

Indeed $T$ has value $\alpha$ on one $\ker T$ fibre and $\alpha+1$ on the other. These two fibres are exactly $\alpha+V$ because $T$ is the identity on $L$. The displayed polynomial agrees on all $2a$ points and has degree $<2a$. Its ordinary coefficients are fixed by the orbit Frobenius of the unordered pair. Consequently they lie in $\mathbf F_{2^d}$ for $d$ the orbit length of $s=\alpha^2+\alpha$.

Conversely, write $R=C+X^a D$ with $\deg C,D<a$. Put $U=X^N+X+\alpha$ and $J(D)=\lfloor UD/X^a\rfloor$. Since $N\le a/2$, $J^2=0$, including $c=1$. The high half gives $Q=D+J(D)$. Now $A=C+(UQ\bmod X^a)$ and $B=A+Q$. Only the $X^N$ and $X$ shifts can contribute to $J$. A degree-$<a$ binary or field-coefficient high half need only access source suffixes of lengths $N$ and one for those two corrections. These formulas work on arbitrary field records. When $R$ is a legitimate independent $d$-bit-subfield polynomial, they yield precisely the admissible child pair described below.

**Finite-field orbit cases.** By definition of $d$, $\alpha^{2^d}\in\{\alpha,\alpha+1\}$. If it equals $\alpha$, the two values have distinct minimal polynomials of degree $d$ because a shorter Frobenius relation between them would shorten the quotient period. Hence $A_k$ and $B_k$ are independent $\mathbf F_{2^d}$ elements. If it equals $\alpha+1$, both values belong to one orbit of length $2d$ and $B_k=A_k^{2^d}$. The two then carry just $2d$ independent bits together. Conversely every allowed choice has a unique residue modulo the appropriate one or two irreducibles. The reverse formula from any $R$ over $\mathbf F_{2^d}$ yields members of the correct subfields: conjugating the ordinary restriction on the two fibres either fixes both polynomials or exchanges them. In either case it has the required relation since $R$ is fixed. Each degree-dividing-$c$ irreducible occurs in exactly one pair. Thus these joins and their inverses preserve every independent input. At $c=1$ the two factors are $Y$ and $Y+1$. Shorter orbits and the twisted case occur at larger even degrees.

**Local resources.** Each output coefficient is the xor of a bounded number of aligned source coefficients and the product of one source coefficient by $\alpha$. Multiplying two $b$-bit polynomial-basis field words, including reducing by the fixed field polynomial, costs $O(b\log^2(b+2))$ by [S006's local short-product proof](../2026-11-26-S006/stable-four-point-bundles.md#one-forward-and-inverse-butterfly-on-the-representative-stream). For each coefficient shift, clear a destination bank of length $2ab$, align source and destination, xor the allowed contiguous interval and rewind both banks. The forward and reverse use a constant number of such scans. If $R_c$ is the number of quotient orbit representatives, S006 proved $2abR_c=(3+o(1))q$. Hence the displayed polynomial formulas cost $O(q\log^2(b+2))$ time and $O(q)$ ever-visited cells for all $c$, using the same reset scratch between representatives. Parent $\alpha$ is in the fixed S006 section, including its nonzero translations.

For each appearing irreducible $h$ at a specified actual root $t=\alpha$ or $\alpha+1$, the ordinary binary remainder $r=\sum_{i<\deg h} r_iY^i$ evaluates to $r(t)$ by the matrix with columns $1,t,\ldots,t^{\deg h-1}$ in the ambient field basis. Gaussian elimination supplies a left inverse. In a twisted pair include both evaluation maps of the single factor or generate the second by a Frobenius matrix. These are fixed binary matrices of dimensions at most $2b$ and act on $a$ independent $k$ values. The separately audited batched binary matrix supplier applies here, costing $O(qb^{\theta-2})$ for any fixed available exact rank exponent $2<\theta<5/2$ (or its harmless polynomial-log variants), and $O(q)$ visited cells. It applies also to expressing each $R_k$ in the power basis of $s$. No embedding between the FS1 field and $K$ is required to evaluate a binary polynomial at the actual $t$. FS1's chosen polynomial representation affects record order and is accounted for below. Padding the local $i<\deg h$ rows to the next power of two and moving the at most $\lceil\log_2 b\rceil$ row bits across $k$ in either direction costs $O(q\log(b+2))$. Remove the same padding in reverse. A simple dense matrix implementation instead gives $O(qb)$ and is not used for the sharper local invoice.

## Actual conventional child order, including arbitrary field choices

The input $[j<N][k<a]$ must be transposed to run the $a$ scalar CRT calls sequentially. With $k=[u_c][w_c]$, the sequence $[j,u,w]\to[u,j,w]\to[u,w,j]$ uses two equal-width $c$-field interchanges. The output of these calls is $[k][h][i<\deg h]$. Flatten $(h,i)$ in the actual directory to one $c$-bit index (the degrees sum to $N$). The same two swaps, in reverse, make it outer, producing $[h][i][k]$. Reverse these permutations, in reverse order, on interpolation. Each costs $O_\rho(q c^\rho)$ time for every fixed $1/2<\rho<1$ and $O_\rho(q)$ visited cells by [OpenAI's exact interchange theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/interchange.tex). The existing [AQ written construction](../../contributions/explicit-overhead/AQ-P.md) gives $O(q\sqrt c\,2^{O(\Phi(\log(c+2)))})$, $\Phi(x)=(x^2\log_2(x+2))^{1/3}$. Neither invocation assumes a faster unproved multiplier. These schedules also price the original base-$S$ rectangular transpose by writing the larger field as two width-$(c-1)$ blocks and three residual bits.

The physical directory order needs a further permutation. Build metadata once. Enumerate the child FS1 factors in their own fields and attach the source index. In the ambient $K$ enumerate the S006 quotient orbits. Compute the actual binary minimal polynomials at $\alpha,\alpha+1$ by multiplying conjugate linear factors and attach the quotient representative index and branch. In a twisted pair emit only one record. Stable tape sorting and matching by the ordinary binary polynomial identifies sources and destinations without any assumed matching normal basis. The total metadata is $O(N\operatorname{poly}(b))$. Elementary binary-key radix sorts and all polynomial-size field calculations take $O(N\operatorname{poly}(b))=o(q)$ steps. This produces instructions in source order and in inverse source order. Match the actual integers representing polynomials of each degree, including leading zeros in any fixed-width key. Proper subfields can give differently ordered FS1 normal bases.

The count $F$ of child factors satisfies $N/c\le F\le N/c+2^{c/2}$ by summing the sizes of the fixed sets of cyclic shifts (enlarge constants at bounded $c$). Pad each factor record from $a\deg h$ bits to exactly $ac$ bits and append an $O(c)$-bit key from the sequential metadata. This has total volume $O(q)$, and the common record width is at least $F^2$. Sort the wide records by (quotient rank, branch). All records for one $\alpha$ are now consecutive. Restore initial FS1 order on inverse by sorting on the stored or regenerated source-rank key, deleting headers and padded zeros. [Tal, *Sorting in o(N log N) Time on a Multitape Turing Machine*, Corollary 6.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/331/download/) gives a fixed deterministic time $O(qc/(1+\log(c+2))^\eta)$ and $O(q)$ cells, for an absolute $\eta>0$, for such polynomially wide records and short destination keys. Its result is a preprint. Ordinary stable binary radix sorting instead gives $O(qc)$ with the same space. The sublinear-in-$c$ route is an imported tape algorithm. It does not reach the target budget just from this bound. A faster directory shuffle for compatible degree-$c$ normal coordinates follows below.

## A marked-word shuffle when the two full-degree normal bases agree

This sharper invoice applies to a legitimate deterministic choice of the S006 root normal basis and a given fixed FS1 child compiler. For an externally fixed incompatible pair use the preceding general invoice. Only the *degree-$c$* normal coordinates need agree. Smaller-degree records below are routed by their actual polynomial keys. This distinction matters because the canonical shortest rotation of a normal-basis word is a basis-dependent index.

To realize compatibility at construction time, embed FS1's chosen degree-$c$ field in $K$ and call the image of its normal element $e$. This can be done deterministically in $\operatorname{poly}(b)$ steps. Its given degree-$c$ defining polynomial splits in $K$. To isolate its roots, compute modulo the current factor the polynomials $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbf F_2}(uX)$ for $u$ in a binary basis of $K$, using repeated modular squaring. Gcd with these polynomials splits any non-singleton root set because nondegeneracy of the trace pairing says that some basis $u$ separates any two distinct roots. Choose a deterministic root, then evaluate the original normal element at it. The image is $L$. All operations have polynomial-size inputs in $b$ and a sequential simulation has polynomial cost. Start with any S006 normal $\gamma_0$ in $K$. Its trace $e_0=T(\gamma_0)$ is normal in $L$. The unique $A\in\mathbf F_2[t]/(t^c-1)$ with $e=A(\sigma)e_0$ is a unit because $e$ is normal. The factors $t^c-1$ and $t^{2c}+t^c+1$ are coprime in characteristic two. By ordinary polynomial CRT choose $C$ congruent to $A$ on the former and to $1$ on the latter. It is a unit modulo $t^{3c}-1$. Thus $\gamma=C(\sigma)\gamma_0$ is normal, and $T(\gamma)=e$. Construct all S006 directions and the stored-coset order with this $\gamma$ from the outset. The canonically matched full-degree FS1 factor index is then the lexicographically least rotation $\lambda$ of the same $c$-bit normal word. The assigned within-word bit order is the same at both generators. All bounds and the at-most-fourfold root padding continue to hold, as the argument requires no new condition on $c$.

For a primitive binary necklace $\lambda$ let $\nu=\min\operatorname{rot}(\bar\lambda)$, with bar denoting bitwise complementation. The two degree-$c$ irreducibles at $\lambda$ and $\nu$ form a quotient pair unless $\lambda=\nu$. Its destination anchor is $\min(\lambda,\nu)$. A self-complementary primitive necklace can occur only for even $c$ because its complementing rotation is the half-turn, since the square stabilizes a primitive word. There are at most $2^{c/2}$ such words. Send these and all proper-degree irreducibles through the ordinary exceptional route. The number of the latter is at most $\sum_{d\le c/2}2^d=O(2^{c/2})$. These records' shapes and inverse keys remain as described above.

For large $c$ put $h=\lfloor\log_2(c/[32(1+\log_2 c)^2])\rfloor$. Handle bounded $c$ or $h<1$ by the ordinary schedule. A word missing $0^h$ anywhere cyclically misses it in each of $\lfloor c/h\rfloor$ disjoint linear blocks, so their number is at most

$$N(1-2^{-h})^{\lfloor c/h\rfloor}\le N\exp[-2^{-h}\lfloor c/h\rfloor]\le N/c^4$$

for sufficiently large $c$. The same count holds for missing $1^h$. Route the resulting $O(N/c^4+\sqrt N)$ exceptional factor records by ordinary $O(c)$-pass key sorts. At $O(ac)$ bits per padded record this takes $O(q)$ time and cells. Classify an entire quotient pair as exceptional if it is short or self-complementary or either of its cyclic marker runs is absent. The tests are invariant under rotation and complement, so they never send the two members of an ordinary pair through different classifications.

The ordinary source stream retaining $\lambda<\nu$ is already in anchor order. It gives the $\alpha$ branch. On the other stream, each $\lambda>\nu$ begins with $0^h$. The greatest rotation of $\lambda$ begins with $1^h$ at a unique position $r$ (unique rotation, even if the run pattern is repeated). These known opposite runs do not intersect, so $h\le r\le c-h$ in the linear word starting at position zero. Stable grouping by $r$ takes $O(q\log(c+2))$ moves using its $O(\log c)$ key. Within each group the original $\lambda$ order remains. Write

$$\lambda=0^h U\,1^h V,\qquad \nu=0^h\,\bar V\,1^h\bar U.$$

The indicated $r$ is the shift from the original word to its greatest cyclic rotation. Output order within the group is increasing $[\bar V][\bar U]$. A sequential sweep over all choices $(U,V)$ inserts the marked source records at their lexicographic positions, filling others with one blank fixed-width record. The sorted source stream includes its original word label, or obtain that label from the parallel metadata. Every present test costs $O(\operatorname{poly}(b))$ per address, negligible compared with the width $ac$. The full dense matrix of just these two fields has $2^{c-2h}$ rows of bits at the record granularity. Exchange the two (possibly unequal) address fields, then complement their combined address by reading the array backwards and restoring the fixed bit order inside each record. A backward per-record scratch copy and rewind costs $O(1)$ per bit. Deleting dummy locations now yields a sorted $\nu$ run. Save the $O(c)$ runs sequentially and pairwise merge them in $O(q\log(c+2))$ time, including linear-volume rewinds. Keys have only $O(c)$ bits and are part of each wide record. A final linear join with the sorted $\lambda<\nu$ run keeps the original $\alpha$ and $\alpha+1$ branch roles. The relatively small sorted exceptional records merge by the same keys. Inverse sorting need not save the forward data-dependent history. Split roles, then apply the same complement-necklace involution to just the second branch. Metadata supplies its actual FS1 key. The normalizations place the exceptional and smaller-degree records at their old positions.

For the local bit-position rotation pad a record, including a presence bit, to $a\,2^{\lceil\log_2(c+2)\rceil+1}$ bits. View this as $O(a)$ binary addresses of width $\Theta(c)$ bits per record, preserving all intra-record address bits. The full address has $O(c)$ bits and the small record width is $\Theta(c)$, meeting the hypotheses of OpenAI's [binary-position permutation corollary](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/permutations.tex). If either field is empty the address exchange is the identity. For fixed $1/2<\rho<1$ it takes $O_\rho(V_r c^\rho(1+\log c))$ time and $O_\rho(V_r)+\operatorname{poly}(b)$ cells on the local filled array. Here $V_r=O(ac2^{c-2h})$. Across at most $c$ shifts the summed filled volume is $O(q(1+\log c)^4)$ because $2^h=\Theta(c/(1+\log c)^2)$. A single such array is $O(q(1+\log c)^4/c)=O(q)$, even though the sum of their volumes exceeds $q$. Process the $r$'s serially, resetting to common scratch origins, keeping only the genuine output runs. Thus the actual paired FS1 order costs

$$O_\rho\bigl(qc^\rho(1+\log(c+2))^5\bigr)\text{ time},\qquad O_\rho(q)\text{ cells ever visited}.$$

This proves an $o(qc)$ invoice by fixing, for example, $\rho=3/4$. More sharply, the explicit written AQ bit-position invoice may replace the published fixed-$\rho$ corollary wherever its thin-to-thick physical hypotheses are met. No such substitution is needed for the displayed result. No claim of an absolute fixed polynomial-log factor at $\rho=1/2$ follows.

The expensive general target-key sort is therefore unnecessary for the chosen compatible compiler. The remaining bills include the actual initial radix conversion, the two directional $N\times a$ aspect-ratio layout changes, the sum of the $a$ true child CRT calls, the displayed pairing cost and its full-word arithmetic, conversion of these monomial $R$ to the specified S006 $B_i$ product basis, and the final factor-directory and precision-carry costs of the outer integer caller. The $a$ child volumes add to exactly $q$, so their square-root-weighted sum at degree $c=b/3$ is $q\sqrt b/\sqrt3$. The endpoint still requires a child construction at general degrees $c$ and conversion of the grouped remainders to the complete individual irreducible directory.

### A square transpose in the actual necklace order

On infinitely many compatible degrees even this particular index permutation cannot be routed by a bounded number of stable whole-bank copy passes. Let $c=4\ell+13$, $\ell\ge1$, and encode each bit of an arbitrary length-$\ell$ word by $E(0)=01$, $E(1)=10$. For every $u,v$ take the normal-basis word

$$\lambda=0^4\;1E(u)0\;1^5\;0E(v)1.$$

Its unique longest zero run has length four and its unique longest one run has length five. The encoded strings have every internal run of length at most two, and their single-symbol guards cannot create a second marker. Thus the word is primitive, its displayed form is its lexicographically smallest rotation, and its greatest rotation starts at the length-five one run. Its complement-necklace representative is

$$\nu=0^5\;1\overline{E(v)}0\;1^4\;0\overline{E(u)}1.$$

In particular $\nu<\lambda$, so FS1's degree-$c$ factor at $\lambda$ is sent to the quotient block keyed by $\nu$. The $2^{2\ell}$ such factors are distinct. Their source order is increasing $(u,v)$, and their destination order is decreasing $v$ then decreasing $u$. This is the transpose of a square of side $s=2^\ell$ followed by reversing its record order.

In a whole-bank pass suppose each input occurrence is sent exactly once through at most some fixed $K\ge2$ one-way streams and within each source/destination pair all occurrences have one relative orientation, forward or reverse. Paths through $h$ passes partition these selected occurrences into at most $K^{h+1}$ monotone classes from initial to final order. For a square transpose, an increasing subsequence has length at most $2s-1$ (its row and column coordinates never decrease and their sum strictly increases). A decreasing subsequence has length at most $s$ (row strictly increases, column strictly decreases). Reversing the entire output exchanges the roles. Therefore $K^{h+1}\ge s^2/(2s-1)\ge s/2$ and $h=\Omega_K(c)$. Choosing one data occurrence from each selected wide record suffices for the path argument. If each pass materializes all $\Theta(q)$ payloads, these passes cost $\Omega_K(qc)$. The density of this constructed subfamily is not a lower bound on the time of selective partial passes. Rewriting, coding, and fusing the algebra with the order change are outside this assertion. The wide-sort theorem and the marked-word construction above are consistent with it. Prior work on square transposition under restricted copy models is surveyed by [Harvey and van der Hoeven, §1.4](https://www.texmacs.org/joris/transpose/transpose.pdf).

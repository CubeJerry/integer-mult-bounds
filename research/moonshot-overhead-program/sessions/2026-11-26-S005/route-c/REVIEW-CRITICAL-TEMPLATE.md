## 1. Premises are local and noncircular

CT-I is a finite exact reduction identity, a count of the actual incoming
volumes of its children, a paid physical schedule, and an allocation
contract. CT-W is a finite normalized Walsh identity, a count of its
children, a paid local executor, and coefficient/norm/allocation contracts.
Neither premise assumes the completed numerical or multiplication bound.
CT-W may use the completed exact interchanger, whose recurrence is closed
first from CT-I.

The exact input-volume convention matters. CT2 counts padding, reserve
calls and repair children on their actual data. Every transformed width
has a complete coordinate range, yielding the exponential-in-width
volume needed to absorb preparation. Native encodings require the stated
single constant-volume outer conversion. Initialized-only inputs,
symmetric-sector identities or an unspecified growing space coefficient
would not satisfy these contracts.

Two matters raised during independent review were corrected before acceptance:

1. The numerical family is now required at **every** even
   \(f\ge m^{K+1}\) with the stated complete spectator/batch shape.
   The bounded-ratio permitted-arity selector exists at every sufficiently
   large width. With \(\delta(K+2)<1\), the actual largest-even
   \(f\le a/A_m\) eventually satisfies
   \(f\ge a/(2c_s m)\ge m^{K+1}\). The assumed identities therefore apply
   to the algorithm's actual integer shapes; unspecified guards cannot
   make the implication vacuous.
2. CT4 now counts completed interchanger calls **after** XOR compilation.
   Its separate \(b_X\) includes the common root-logarithmic guard passes.
   The scalar exponent is \(b_S\), and
   \(b=\max(b_X,b_S)\) is used once. This resolves the former ambiguity
   between a precompiler invoice and actual completed-call volume.

All fixed polynomial degrees must be absorbed into \(K\) before selecting
\(\delta=1/R\); this is permitted and explicitly required. Increasing
the fixed constants to \(B_W\ge1\) is harmless.

## 2. Exact and numerical recurrences

For the exact recurrence, use the normalized induction envelope
\(\sqrt e\,(\log_2(e+2))^{p_I}\) above the fixed positive base.
After division by \(\sqrt e\), the actual child term is bounded by

\[
 B_I \left(\frac{\log_2(f+2)}{\log_2(e+2)}\right)^{p_I}.
\]

The bounded-ratio integer selector gives the strict logarithmic contraction
CT6. The choice \(p_I=\lceil\log(4B_I)/(-\log\rho)\rceil\) makes this term
at most \(1/4\). The normalized ordinary toll is
\(O(e^{\delta K-1/2})\). The separated construction cost is also absorbed:
for fixed \(d\), \(2^{O(e^{\delta K})}(\log V)^d/V\) tends uniformly to zero
for \(V\ge2^{ce}\). This proves a monotone **upper envelope** CT8;
no monotonicity of the actual execution time is assumed.

In CT4, multiplying the incoming-volume invoice by the completed
width-\(f\) exact envelope gives

\[
 \sqrt m\,(\log(m+2))^q I(f)
 \le C\sqrt{a+1}\,(\log(a+2))^{p_I+q}.
\]

Here \(f\le a/(2m)\), \(f\ge2\), and hence \(m=O(a)\).
The separately retained whole-active-width swaps also fit this bound.
The comparison does not require a false ordering of the actual
execution times at widths \(f\) and \(a\).

For numerical children,
\((\sigma_m/W_m)\sqrt{f/a}\le B_W\). Keeping the same global \(k\) at
all levels lets the root-logarithmic factor cancel when defining \(U_k\).
Thus CT9 includes both the ordinary toll and the physical delivery toll.
The displayed \(p_W\) pays the latter with a full extra logarithmic power
and contracts the recursive term to at most one quarter. This closes an
inhomogeneous recurrence, not merely a product of homogeneous losses.

## 3. Batches, preparation, numerical error and physical space

The full batch-product inequality follows from actual widths:

\[
 \prod_i m_i \le \prod_i A_{m_i}\le a_0,\qquad
 \prod_i W_{m_i}\le a_0^K.
\]

All factors \(W_m\) are powers of two. The original caller provides
\(2^{k-s}\) independent complete spectator rows with
\(s=O(k/\log k)\). Eventually the spectator count is divisible by the
entire batch product and therefore by every prefix. No repeated padding
is needed. The narrow Lean BatchPath.lean proves this abstract
product/divisibility implication separately; it does not construct
the physical schedule.

Polynomial branching is sufficient here because its degree is fixed:
after absorbing a constant per-node factor into a power of \(m\), the
product of branching bounds along a path is polynomial in \(a_0\).
The numerical node count is polynomial in \(k\). Preparation
\(2^{k^{1/8}}\operatorname{poly}(k)\), even repeated at those nodes,
is below their exponentially large actual numerical arrays.

The coefficient and instruction contracts give uniform bounds for
both input-to-intermediate and fault-to-later propagation. If a stated
height bound is used to derive a whole finite-word bound, its fixed
degree must first be absorbed into \(K\). After this convention, the
sum of local logarithmic gains along a path is
\(O(k^{1/16}\log k)=o(k)\). Common-ancestor propagation passes through
completed unitary children. The source's array-rounding argument then
needs only \(p=C_0k\) bits, including the \(\sqrt N\) coordinate-rounding
factor and polynomially many array operations. The completed identity
by itself would not justify this precision conclusion.

A common visited-cell cap requires more than a bound on live data.
CT parks payload only at contractions, releases it before full-volume
reserve descents, and reuses marked regions across siblings and phases.
The geometric parked-volume sum plus polynomial descriptors is \(O(V)\)
with an absolute constant. ArenaSchedule.lean checks the corresponding
abstract allocation pattern. Physical machine-to-allocation-tree
refinement remains a written premise, not a newly formalized source theorem.

## 4. Actual outer caller and logarithmic exponent

The primary source controlled-block proof charges three root-logarithmic
powers on top of its interchange envelope: guarded position operations,
batches, and network stages. Substituting CT8 gives per-layer cost

\[
 C Nk\sqrt{k}\,(1+\log(k+2))^{p_I+3}.
\]

Balanced CRT conversion has \(O(\log k)\) depths, giving \(p_I+4\).
Synchronized short Fourier construction also has \(O(\log k)\) layers.
Its selected-bit Walsh operations cost CT11, including initial and final
position changes, so their total exponent is at most \(p_W+b+1\).
Their selected width is actually \(O(k/\log k)\); using \(a\le k\) only
weakens the bound. Position-permutation layers and scalar phase streams
fit the maximum of these exponents.

The primary integer caller uses \(Nk=\Theta(n)\), \(k=\Theta(\log n)\),
tested short-axis and coprimality shapes, known short integer arithmetic,
and a fixed numerical margin before exact coefficient rounding.
Its phase setup is polynomial in \(k\); coordinate products and carry
propagation are smaller than CT13. New arity selection does not change
prime intervals or use an uncomputed cutoff as advice. Hence

\[
 C_*=\max(p_I+4,p_W+b+1)
\]

is a valid finite explicit conditional exponent. Setup is absorbed by
\(Nk\). Finite exceptional lengths use the stated exact linear-space
fallback. This is a complete conditional transfer to the full product
under the entire local physical and numerical contracts, rather than
a new unconditional machine.

## 5. Collision rank keeps the two fields separate

Within one projective source-direction class, CR1 forces every
\(\langle u_i,v_j\rangle\) to be nonzero. CR2 therefore forces that
principal block of \(P\) to be the identity over the **scalar** field
\(K\). This proves rank at least the largest class size without comparing
ranks over different fields. The block-row identity has dimension \(R\)
times that class size, so arbitrary scalar row mixing does not evade it.

For symmetric binary geometry, \(v\mapsto\langle v,v\rangle\) is a
nonzero linear functional. Its one-fibre has exactly \(2^{D-1}\)
elements, proving CR5. The source Fourier kernel has precisely the
identity-on-labels target, unit self-pairing directions, and free
rectangles vanishing whenever the geometric pairing is nonzero.
The all-ones target, nonfree support and changed interfaces lie outside
the theorem.

The \(D=2\) sharpness example checks: repeated \(e_1,e_2\) and
\(P=J_2\otimes I_t\) give rank \(t\) over every scalar field;
within-direction blocks are \(I_t\), and the remaining support is
geometrically free. This finite equality is not an unbounded template.

## 6. Adaptive positive-invoice floor

CR-I assumes a **lower** bill recurrence. It cannot be applied to an
actual runtime known only to have that expression as an upper bound.
Under its premises every local toll and the product of recursive
factors are bounded by \(R_0\), proving CR11.

When \(x_i\ge A(H+1)\), choosing
\(\beta>1/(2K)\) makes
\(\Delta x_i\le\beta x_i\), hence \(x_{i+1}\ge r x_i\).
For large \(x\), \(\log_2B+p\log_2y\) dominates a constant times
\(y\log_2x/x\), uniformly for \(1\le y\le x\):
large \(y\) uses \(\log y\), and small \(y\) uses \(B>1\).
CR8 changes this to a multiple of
\(\Delta x_i\,\log_2x_i/x_i\). On each interval,
\(z\ge r x_i\) and \(\log z\le\log x_i\), directly proving CR14.

Telescoping to scale \(A(H+1)\) gives CR15. If
\(H<(\log_2x_0)^2\), the removed term is only
\(O((\log\log x_0)^2)\), proving the square-log floor.
The fixed-power arity construction attains this order in the specified
bill model. The caveat for indefinitely repeated bounded small arities
is necessary and retained.

CR's cost consequence restricts the identity-on-labels and positive
dimension-charging certificate, including its ordinary-work price and
regular split. It is neither a universal tape lower bound nor proof
that a family attains a better complete multiplication bound.

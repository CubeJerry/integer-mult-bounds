# Spectral prices do not supply a free physical routing primitive

The companion's Euclidean-prices theorem assigns compatible scaled
logarithms of tensor spectral points to placed tensors. The wire and
shared-label prices are monotone invariants. Appending a shared label
inside the convex hull has zero additional price in this invariant. This
does not describe zero-cost tape copying, zero head movement, a storage
allocation, or a constructor. A physical endpoint operation cannot be
replaced by its spectral price in the multiplication upper-bound ledger.

The tagged-permutation shortcut has two separate import barriers. The
routing theorem in protocol.tex explicitly assumes a finite field with a
largest spectral-flat dimension k. The introduction identifies this as
the contradiction hypothesis used to prove flats of unbounded dimension.
It is not an available unconditional algorithmic premise. The approximation
theorem in width.tex is conditional on that routing conclusion.

Moreover its approximation is measured by a spectral-price gap for an
error tensor with catalyst wires. This is not a uniform numerical norm,
bounded coordinate error, sparse ordinary convolution residual, or exact
deterministic multitape routine. The stated size counts trivalent network
vertices; the routing control includes an advised RAM and a sum over
selectors. A physical use would need an applicable hypothesis, an actual
term-execution/selection algorithm, bit and precision bounds, and charged
movement and visited space. None follows from the displayed size or gap.

No such operation is imported into the exact interchanger or numerical
Walsh caller here. This closes this particular source shortcut, without
challenging the companion's proof or excluding a new tape algorithm.
The staged source audit is expanded, not relabeled complete.

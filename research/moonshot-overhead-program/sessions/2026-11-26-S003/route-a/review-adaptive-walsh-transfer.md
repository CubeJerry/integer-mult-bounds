# Cross-review of the lead adaptive Walsh transfer

Status: **accepted as a conditional implication on its AI/AW interfaces**.
A completed `nonstationary-rational-walsh.md` independently before reading
the lead's `adaptive-walsh-transfer.md`, then compared all five sections.
The independently obtained physical recurrence, spectator budget and
precision analysis agree. This review does not substitute for admission
of the exact interchanger or the separate complete integer-source audit.

The arity choices differ only by harmless rounding: the lead uses
m=2^y+1 and a floor logarithm; NW uses m=2^y-1 and a ceiling logarithm.
Both are odd and have log m=y+O(1), with y comparable to
x^(2/3)(log(x+2))^(1/3). The normalized binary routing loss is
2^O(sqrt(y log(y+2))). Both proofs compare it with the decrement of the
target exponent along the next active width. The local physical address
toll, including polynomial(m) ordinary work, is present in both.

The source rational template's batch is a power of two, and the product
of its widths along a path is polynomial in the root active width.
The already complete inactive spectator bits provide every split exactly.
This is a shared external resource with an explicit total budget, not
repeatedly borrowed fresh workspace. The direct remainder work is
polynomial(m) per volume and is included.

NW section 3 supplies AW's absolute local payload coefficient: process
free rectangles sequentially, retain their source subset across all
queries, accumulate the destination subset and perform its final partial
transforms once. Each subset is at most the original batch. Charged
rectangles have separately billed query kernels. The Newton/check/repair
program uses a fixed number of arrays, and contraction has child fraction
one eighth. Physical extraction and return are polynomially many complete
scans; the Fourier call sharing remains the source order.

The norm and rational-bit bounds also match. The sum of local polynomial(m)
logarithmic gains is k^o(1), and the total array/node count is polynomial
in k. Bounded-divisor preparation keeps its divisor at polynomial(m) bits,
so its per-record work is polynomial(m) times k. An absolute O(k) format
therefore remains compatible with the source global fault-propagation
proof. Arbitrary exact-interchange bit states are not included as
Euclidean numerical arrays.

The final integer transfer has the right formal scale: for accepted source
shapes with Nk=Theta(n), k=Theta(L(n)), the new exponent becomes
O(s(n)^(2/3)(log(s(n)+2))^(1/3)). Fixed logarithmic powers fit inside it.
The exact source shape, CRT, short-transform and carry interfaces are
owned by the lead's complete assembly and must remain linked in any final
claim. No endpoint or polynomial-s saving follows from this exponent.

# Independent review of exact modular Walsh lifting

**Decision.** The even-width rational Walsh circuit can run exactly over a single finite residue ring at the existing AQ time and absolute visited-space bounds. This removes the internal roundoff-propagation obligation for the integer caller. It changes the verification of the existing envelope, with no claimed exponent improvement or formal machine verification.

## A single modulus and a uniform physical operation

For each actually used arity $m$, the compact rational template bounds the bit length of its coefficients by a fixed polynomial in $m$. More is available for the denominators. The shift count $W_0$ and Newton degree $d$ are polynomial in $m$. Newton divides by $1,\ldots,d$. The Vandermonde nodes are $i/W_0$, so the Lagrange inverse and its transpose introduce only prime divisors of $W_0!$. Each actual inverse gap divides by the nonzero integer
\[
 \gamma\sum_{h=0}^d(-2)^h {c-u\choose h}-z
\]
with $0\le c\le m$ and $\gamma,z\in\{-1,1\}$. There are at most $4(m+1)$ candidates of polynomial bit length. Zero candidates are skipped. The remaining address-independent coefficients can be enumerated in polynomial time and have polynomial bit length. In particular the source rectangle weights are dyadic, and its column phases are signs. If $U_m$ is the product of $2,(W_0+d)!$, the nonzero absolute gaps and the denominators of this fixed list, every emitted reduced coefficient lies in $\mathbb Z[1/U_m]$. This statement concerns final coefficients. Exact coefficient construction may use elimination over $\mathbb Q$ and then reduce each result. An arbitrary intermediate rational pivot does not have to be a unit in a composite ring. [Pairwise prediction and division repair, §§3–4](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/69632d87c99b5a75d0b44a7d964e6550e457e016/research/moonshot-overhead-program/routes/B/pairwise-newton-division.md) · [Compact rational Walsh, §§2–3](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/69632d87c99b5a75d0b44a7d964e6550e457e016/research/moonshot-overhead-program/sessions/2026-11-26-S003/route-a/compact-rational-walsh.md).

Let $U$ be the product of the $U_m$ along the actual width chain, including the bounded direct gates. All child slots of a given numerical width use that same arity. The finite repair tree adds only the listed gates. The NW width estimates give $\log_2 U\leq\sum_i\operatorname{poly}(m_i)=k^{o(1)}$. Thus $U$ can be constructed in $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ bit time and space. For signed $w=O(k)$-bit input mantissas and $s$ selected bits, $M=1+2^{k+w+s+2}U$ is odd, coprime to $U$, exceeds $2^{w+s+1}$ and has $O(k)$ bits. The ring map $\mathbb Z[1/U]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/M$ transports the entire rational matrix identity, also when intermediate values wrap. Selectors depend on exact address bits rather than numerical data. A prime above every $B$-bit denominator would also work, but a generic trial-division search costs $2^{O(B)}$ and does not by itself prove polynomial-in-$k$ setup. [NW width family, schedule and coefficient bounds](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/69632d87c99b5a75d0b44a7d964e6550e457e016/research/moonshot-overhead-program/sessions/2026-11-26-S003/route-a/nonstationary-rational-walsh.md).

For canonical $0\le z<M$ and short positive divisor $d$ with $\gcd(d,M)=1$, return $z$ if $d=1$. Otherwise compute $r=(-z)(M^{-1}\bmod d)\bmod d$, with $0\le r<d$. Then $(z+rM)/d$ is integral, lies in $[0,M)$, and is the required residue. Scanning the $O(k)$-bit words against a $B$-bit divisor costs $O(kB)$. The short inverse by schoolbook extended Euclid and short scratch can conservatively be charged a fixed polynomial in $B$. Forming $rM$ and multiplying a residue by a signed short numerator use at most $B$ scanned shift/add steps, followed as needed by per-step modular subtraction. The final exact long division retains a $B$-bit remainder on a separate tape. Each record therefore costs $k\,\operatorname{poly}(m)$ at that arity, including head returns to the local modulus and record roots. The modulus is reused rather than recopied into an array per row. A bounded number of $\Theta(k)$-bit records and reused $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ scalar scratch preserve NW's geometric spatial allocation and its bound on all cells ever visited.

## Normalization, retrieval and the source caller

The source proves that its $2D$ normalized axis calls supply $2^{-Df}$ on both endpoint constructions. A complete spectral-conversion normalization commutes through the row map and cancels its inverse. The direct four-point coefficients are $\pm1/2$. NW chooses even numerical child widths and keeps its widths that way below the root. Thus the even part has rational matrix $2^{-a/2}H_a$, where $a=s-(s\bmod 2)$ and $H_a$ is the unnormalized sign matrix. These identities apply unchanged after the S002 predictor replacement because the actual endpoint calls and conversion normalizations are retained. [Pinned binary implementation proof](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/binary.tex) · [Pinned selected-bit tape interface](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/walsh.tex).

Left-shift every mantissa by $a/2$ bits once at the root and run the rational even-width circuit. For odd $s$, apply an unnormalized one-bit sum/difference in the residue ring. The integer outputs are now $H_s x$, each of magnitude at most $2^{w+s}$. Centered representatives recover them uniquely. The initial shift is $O(k)$ bits, and division by $2^{a/2}$ at exit is a binary shift with the requested rounding. For odd $s$, after this shift and the input binary-point scale the pre-normalized vector has norm at most $\sqrt2\,2^{Lk}$. Use a single certified dyadic approximation to $1/\sqrt2$ of error at most $2^{-P}$, with $P\ge (L+D)k+3$. The scalar error then contributes at most $\sqrt2\,2^{Lk-P}$. One final rounding on each real component contributes at most $\sqrt{2N}\,2^{-p}$ for $p\ge (D+1)k+3$. These give absolute Euclidean error below $2^{-Dk}$. The dyadic reciprocal square root can be prepared by polynomial-time integer-square comparisons once. Retain all bits in the division/multiplication until that final rounding. Each final odd scalar application uses the source $O(k\log(2+k))$ short multiplier and reused polynomial scratch. The empty selection is the identity, and the Walsh adjoint is the same transform.

The outer integer proof uses the Walsh result for represented inputs of norm at most $2^{13k}$ and layer error $2^{-50k}$. It uses the intermediate **width** guarantee separately. Modular internal states meet the width contract directly. The real-operator internal fault-amplification statement in the source lemma is not a new guarantee about modular states. At layer boundaries the exact target stays unitary, so the source's error accumulation, exact convolution rounding and carry argument still apply. [Pinned integer proof, “Precision for one transform”](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/integers.tex).

## Adverse tests and scope

An actual non-dyadic gap matters. At $m=63,W_0=64$, take all-zero key and seed. Then $c=0,u=15,d=33,\gamma=-1,z=1$ and the gap equals $-4510048854467219030016$, divisible by $73$. Every Newton divisor and Vandermonde spacing is a unit modulo $73^t$, but this gap is not. The separate gap product is essential.

The independent exact-integer replay passed 16,984 cases of the modular short-divisor formula over four composite moduli, all residues, and divisors up to 70 that are units. It also replayed the actual S002 predictor/repair at nine finite keys for $m=31,63,127$. The two empty defect sets require no repair; seven nonempty cases checked 576 individual output rows, including a four-defect Vandermonde case. The deliberately bad $73^5$ modulus was rejected. These controls use dense finite arithmetic and do not execute the tape multiplier.

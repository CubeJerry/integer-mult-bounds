# The finest Frobenius-compatible grouping of complete four-point blocks

Let $K=\mathbb F_{2^b}$, $q=2^b$, $\sigma(z)=z^2$, $\eta\notin\mathbb F_2$ and $U=\langle 1,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb F_2}$. A group of blocks contains **all four** remainder coefficients for every included $U$-coset. Partition all the $U$-cosets into such groups. Within each group allow an arbitrary invertible $K$-linear change of its coefficient basis. Require Frobenius to permute the group subspaces. The finest possible groups are the cosets of
\[
 V=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\sigma^i U.
\]
Consequently each admissible group contains a multiple of $2^{\dim V-2}$ complete four-point blocks. The groups may have unequal sizes.

## Necessity and sufficiency

In the coordinate system of all point evaluations, the four coefficient rows for the remainder on $A$ are the inverse interpolation matrix on $A$. Their span is the full coordinate dual supported on $A$. An invertible change within a group with point set $B$ therefore retains exactly the full supported row space on $B$. Binary-reachable evaluation tables span $K^q$ over $K$, since the full evaluation matrix is invertible on the binary standard monomials. On these tables $y_z^2=y_{\sigma z}$. Squaring a row's output is consequently represented by the linear row which squares each weight and moves its supporting point by $\sigma$. The transported subspace on $B$ is exactly the row subspace supported on $\sigma B$. Two different supports cannot give equal subspaces even on the reachable binary inputs.

It follows that Frobenius permutes the group subspaces exactly when it permutes their point sets. Write $x\sim y$ for membership in the same group. Since the groups contain whole $U$-cosets, $x\sim x+u$ for all $x$ and all $u\in U$. Applying every Frobenius power gives $x\sim x+\sigma^i u$ for every $x,i,u$. Transitivity yields $x\sim x+v$ for each $v\in V$. Conversely, $V$ contains $U$ and is Frobenius-stable, so its coset partition attains the claimed subspace condition. This direction establishes algebraic grouping. A local field basis, stored representation, or cheap conversion between different groupings is a separate construction.

## Exact minimum over elements of a prescribed field degree

Fix $d=[\mathbb F_2(\eta):\mathbb F_2]\ge2$, with $d\mid b$. The span of the conjugates and of $1$ lies in the actual subfield $\mathbb F_{2^d}$; hence its dimension is independent of the larger ambient degree. Write $d=2^a m$, $m$ odd, and let $c(0)=0$ and $c(a)=2^{a-1}$ for $a>0$. Then
\[
 \min_{\deg(\eta)=d}\dim V=
 1+c(a)+
 \min_{\substack{E\subseteq\{e>1:e\mid m\}\\{\rm lcm}(E)=m}}
       \sum_{e\in E}\operatorname{ord}_e(2).
\]
The empty set is allowed for $m=1$. Here $\operatorname{ord}_e(2)$ is the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $e$.

By a normal basis, $\mathbb F_{2^d}$ under Frobenius is the cyclic $\mathbb F_2[T]$-module with defining polynomial $P=T^d-1$. Each monic divisor $f$ of $P$ is the annihilator of the vector $P/f$ in this module. Conversely the span $W_\eta$ generated by a vector of annihilator $f$ has dimension $\deg f$. Its orbit length is the least positive $n$ with $f\mid T^n-1$. This length equals its field degree. The unique one-dimensional fixed space is $\mathbb F_2\cdot 1$. It lies in $W_\eta$ precisely when $T+1$ divides $f$, since then $f/(T+1)$ represents a nonzero fixed vector in the cyclic module $\mathbb F_2[T]/f$.

If an irreducible $h$ has a root of multiplicative order $e$, its degree is $\operatorname{ord}_e(2)$ for $e>1$, by the size of that root's Frobenius orbit. Order $1$ gives $h=T+1$. Every odd divisor $e$ of $m$ occurs by cyclicity of the multiplicative group of a finite field containing an element of order $e$. For odd $u$, $T^u-1$ is squarefree; in characteristic two $T^{2^j u}-1=(T^u-1)^{2^j}$. Thus for $f=\prod h_i^{n_i}$ to have orbit order $2^a m$, the odd orders have least common multiple $m$ and, when $a>0$, at least one $n_i$ exceeds $2^{a-1}$. The fixed line costs at least one dimension, either already in $W_\eta$ or appended to it. Each distinct nontrivial odd order costs one irreducible of the stated degree, and the mandatory repetitions cost at least $c(a)$ further dimensions. This proves the lower bound.

For attainment, take one irreducible of order $e$ for each member of a minimizing cover, and take $T+1$ with multiplicity $1+c(a)$. The product divides $T^d-1$. Its order is exactly $d$, its degree equals the displayed quantity, and its cyclic span contains $1$. The normal-basis construction above supplies an element of the required field degree. This gives the minimum for the complete-support grouping, not an implementation at that minimum.

These ingredients are classical. The normal-basis shift is stated explicitly in [Waldschmidt, *Finite Fields*, Theorem 34](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michel.waldschmidt/articles/pdf/FiniteFields.pdf). The dimension of the conjugate span and the divisor characterization of possible Frobenius orders appear in [Huczynska, Mullen, Panario and Thomson, *Existence and properties of k-normal elements over finite fields*, Theorems 3.2–3.4](https://people.math.carleton.ca/~dthomson/Research/pdfs/DThomson-k-normal.pdf). We use these facts with the distinguished fixed vector and the prescribed field degree. No priority claim is made for the resulting minimum.

## Consequences and scope

For an odd prime $b$, every nonbinary $\eta$ has $d=b$, so $\dim V\ge1+\operatorname{ord}_b(2)$. There are at least $2^{\operatorname{ord}_b(2)-1}\ge(b+1)/2$ original four-point blocks per group.

For $b=3^t$ and full degree $d=b$, a cover with least common multiple $b$ must contain $b$. One has $\operatorname{ord}_{3^t}(2)=2\cdot3^{t-1}$. Indeed $4=1+3$, and cubing a number congruent to 1 modulo $3^j$ but not modulo $3^{j+1}$ raises that valuation by precisely one. Raising it to a power prime to 3 preserves that valuation, as its geometric sum is a unit modulo 3. This gives $\dim V\ge1+2b/3$, or at least $q^{2/3}/2$ four-point blocks per group.

For an explicit equality case, take a normal basis $e_i=\sigma^i\alpha$ and $\eta=e_0+e_{b/3}$. No nonidentity power of the odd cyclic shift can preserve this two-point support: its stabilizer would have to fix both points as its induced permutation has order dividing two. Hence the field degree is $b$. The translates generate exactly the even-parity subspace on each of $b/3$ disjoint triples, of total dimension $2b/3$. The fixed vector $1$ is the all-ones coordinate vector and is independent of them. A normal $\eta$ instead gives $V=K$, hence one group containing the entire field.

The whole-block assumption is essential. After local interpolation to actual evaluation coordinates, singleton point subspaces are permuted by Frobenius even when $V=K$. This construction splits each complete original four-row block. The bounded-size assumption is also essential: if the entire field is one group its row subspace is automatically preserved. A large admissible group need not cost its cardinality times the width of a field word at every location. In fact the independent $3$-power representative layout implements one local butterfly on a linear-bit store. This theorem rules out a uniformly bounded number of whole four-row blocks per group in all degrees. It does not lower-bound binary CRT time or space, and it supplies no carry.

## Finite checks

The companion checker implements equivalence-class merging under Frobenius directly and compares the eventual point partition against the separately computed span cosets. All nonbinary elements of every field of degrees 2 through 9 passed. It separately checks complete actual interpolation matrices and invertible local row changes. Exhaustive enumeration in independently constructed polynomial and cyclic normal bases gives the following minima in successive degrees 2 through 12:
\[
                  2,\ 3,\ 3,\ 5,\ 4,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7,\ 6,\ 11,\ 5.
\]
An additional normal-basis enumeration at $d=15$ gives five, with a single order-15 factor. At $d=21$ it gives six, since separate order-3 and order-7 factors beat a single order-21 factor. That enumeration covers all 2,097,018 full-degree elements by rotational orbits. Exact cyclic-module checks for the sharp $3$-power witness give dimensions $3,7,19,55,163$ at $b=3,9,27,81,243$. These are controls for the proof; dense finite enumeration is excluded from the tape algorithm.

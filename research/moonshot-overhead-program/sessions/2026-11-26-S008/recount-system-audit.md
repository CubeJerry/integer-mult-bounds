# Independent end-to-end review of the fixed degree-240 integer machine

The final $R=240$ dispatcher uses at most $2^{32}n$ visited tape/address pairs. Its [all-length clock](all-lengths.md) has smooth coefficient 63. At the sharper fixed exponent, [the complete historical comparison](routing-label-walsh.md) gives $T(n)\le n(\ln n)^{501/1000}$ whenever $\log_2\log_2n\ge7{,}519{,}517{,}465{,}920{,}988$. This note reconstructs the final adjacent numerical signs and the actual 29 earlier weights, checks the occupied data and recursive interfaces, and [follows the recovered coefficients through physical carry and raw output](recount-carry.md). The earlier smooth comparisons below preserve a separate derivation of the same large-input execution.

Fix the native root degree at $R=240$. The input consists of two independent most-significant-first words of the same positive length $n$, including any leading zeros. Put $L=\log_2n$ and $\Phi(x)=(x^2\log_2(x+2))^{1/3}$. The completed numerical suppliers yield the conservative, coefficient-one clock

$$
 T(n)\le n\sqrt L\,2^{71\Phi(\log_2 L)} .
$$

It holds on the fast domain specified below. In particular

$$
 \log_2\log_2n\ge 55\cdot71000^3
 =19{,}685{,}105{,}000{,}000{,}000
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T(n)\le n(\log_2n)^{501/1000}.
$$

The machine returns the full $2n$ product bits. The [whole-family visited-cell inventory](routing-outer-space.md) gives at most $2^{36}n$ tape/address pairs visited on this fast branch. This is an independent review of a written physical construction. The kernel-checked statements cover individual arithmetic or tape transitions and the numerical reduction, as described below.

## Independent addition of time and space

Write $b_0=\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil$. The ordinary arithmetic, input conversion and output together cost $k^{850}+2^{100}b_0Nk^{3/2}$; there are at most $3\cdot2^{29}b_0$ layers across three transforms. A block permutation costs $2^{72}b_0^3(2^{10}Nk)(1+J(k))+k^{1120}$. All three CRTs together cost $2^{75}b_0^4(2^{10}Nk)(1+J(k))+k^{1125}$. One selected Walsh layer costs $2^{18}A_W b_0Nk\sqrt k\,2^{70\Phi(X)}$, $X=\lceil\log_2(k+2)\rceil$. The $2^{10}k$ outer record and $100k$ residue staging are distinct physical intervals of comparable size. Reformatting a bounded number of their words by monotone sweeps costs at most $2^{19}Nk$ per conversion, inside the stated layer constant. Summing and allowing the raw length dispatcher gives the sufficient bound

$$
 2^{120}A_W b_0^4 Nk\sqrt k\,2^{70\Phi(X)}. \tag{1}
$$

All additive terms together are less than $2^k$ because $1200\log_2k\le k/32$ in this range. For example the permutation layers have joint coefficient below $2^{115}A_I$ using $1+J(k)\le3A_I\sqrt k\,2^{61\Phi(X)}$. The Walsh-layer coefficient is below $2^{49}A_W$. No exponent from an outer position permutation has been substituted into the inner $f$-slot estimate.

For $b\ge16$, $2^{b-1}<n\le bh\le b\,2^{k-2}$ gives $k>b+1-\log_2b\ge b/2$. Also $4h\le 8n/b\le n/2$, so $k\le\log_2 n$ and $Nk\le8hb\le16n$. Put $x=\log_2\log_2 n$ and $t=\log_2k$. Whenever $x\ge 2^{48}+1$, $t\ge x-1\ge2^{48}$ meets every uniform fast guard, including the improved short-axis sieve. Then $b_0\le t+2$ and $X\le t+2$. As $\log_2(t+2)/t$ is decreasing, $\Phi(t+2)\le(1+2/t)\Phi(t)\le\Phi(t)+2$. For $t\ge2^{48}$, $\Phi(t)\ge t^{2/3}$ and $\log_2(t+2)\le\sqrt t$, so $4\log_2(t+2)\le\Phi(t)/64$. Also $125+\log_2 A_W+140<390\le\Phi(t)/2$. Equation (1) now gives the stronger $2T(n)\le n\sqrt L\,2^{71\Phi(x)}$ by $t\le x$. The raw dispatcher in [the schoolbook construction](schoolbook.md) always selects the fast path at these lengths.

Let $D=71000^3$ and $T_0=55D$. One has $T_0+2<2^{55}$, $D\cdot55=T_0$, and $2D<T_0+2$. Since $\log_2(x+2)/x$ decreases, at every $x\ge T_0$ we have $D\log_2(x+2)\le x$. Cubing shows $71\Phi(x)\le x/1000$. Exponentiating yields the coefficient-one 0.501 inequality. The same sufficient binary-log cutoff also gives $T(n)\le n(\ln n)^{501/1000}$ in natural-log notation: $\ln n=L\ln 2$ and $(\ln 2)^{501/1000}\ge\ln2>1/2$. This computation gives a sufficient cutoff for one fixed machine.

For space, a call-by-call total alone does not bound the cumulative union if different heads reach their extrema on different calls. The [individual-head accounting](routing-outer-space.md) uses fixed origins and maximal marked intervals. Its two routing families are separately bounded on $1024Nk$ and $100Nk$ bits. An older spatial stack frame is never crossed to issue a child; the [exact contracted handoff](recount-handoff.md) restores every word and interior head displacement. Even with consecutive equal-volume reserves, data are parked only on contracted edges; the volume sum stays geometric. Finite levels contract by $1/16$ or $1/8$ as appropriate. Parked control lengths are independently bounded through full reserve paths. The total visible receipt is below $2^{32}Nk\le2^{36}n$, with initial positions of dormant heads and reused output intervals included.

## Adjacent certified inputs for the sharper invoice

The audited whole sum can also use the stronger published multiplier $2^{55}$ in [the final integer ledger, equation (1)](integer-clock.md#complete-price-of-the-three-transforms). Indeed every permutation coefficient, even with all three sets of layers, is below $2^{130}$ before $b_0^4 Nk\sqrt k\,2^{70\Phi(X)}$; $2^{54}A_W>2^{176}$ covers it. The joint Walsh coefficient is below $2^{49}A_W$. This sharpened addition, together with $Nk\le16n$, gives a logarithmic excess at $x=\log_2\log_2n$ of

$$
 M(x)=169+\log_2 5121+4\log_2(x+2)
       +70\big((x+2)^2\log_2(x+4)\big)^{1/3}.
$$

The full sufficient condition is $M(x)\le x/1000$. For $x\ge2^{48}+1$, $M(x)/x$ is decreasing. Indeed the ratio for the last term cubed is $(1+2/x)^2\log_2(x+4)/x$, the product of nonnegative decreasing factors. The other terms use the same logarithmic monotonicity.

The [independent cutoff checker](recount-sharp.py) handles each transcendental comparison by extracting its binary logarithm with integer interval arithmetic. Normalize an integer $n=2^j v$, $1\le v<2$. The next digit of $\log_2v$ is zero or one according as $v^2<2$ or $v^2\ge2$. In the latter case divide the new mantissa by two. Square and renormalize dyadic endpoint brackets with outward rounding throughout; if an endpoint straddles two, repeat with more precision. After 112 digits, the resulting rational endpoints enclose the true logarithm with gap $2^{-112}$. Applying this independently to $5121,x+2,x+4$ allows positive residuals to be cubed and compared exactly. The resulting adjacent signs are

| Logarithmic invoice | First integer $z$ with a certified positive margin, with a negative margin at $z-1$ |
|---|---:|
| Earlier fixed additive $2^{25}+220$ from the [separate atanh-series certificate](routing-outer-transfer.md#the-root-degree-240-machine) | $18{,}536{,}231{,}398{,}600{,}959$ |
| The actual $M(z)$ above | $18{,}536{,}127{,}975{,}009{,}233$ |
| $M(z)+1$, retaining one full extra binary bit | $18{,}536{,}127{,}975{,}012{,}315$ |

In the second row, all $n\ge2^{2^z}$ satisfy the binary-log leading-one bound. The last row also admits the natural-log leading-one bound at its own endpoint. These first integers belong to the named sufficient invoices. They have no lower-bound implication for the actual run.

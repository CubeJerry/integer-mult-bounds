# Independent review of the all-length integer clock

For the three-branch machine, write $L=1+\log _2 n$, $x=\log _2 L$ and $\Phi(x)=(x^2\log _2(x+2))^{1/3}$. The input consists of two arbitrary equal-length binary words separated on one tape. The $2n$ bits of the unpadded product, including leading zeros, are written starting at the origin of a blank output tape. The joined estimates are

$$
T(n)\le n\sqrt L\,2^{71\Phi(x)}\quad(n\ge2),\qquad T(1)\le4096,
\qquad S(n)\le 2^{32}n\quad(n\ge1).
$$

This review checks switching, signed data, numerical accuracy, physical scans and the use of lowered guards in the spatial bound. It uses the written fast transform and the exact algebraic identities cited by its suppliers. The Lean check at the end concerns the scalar inequalities. The direct tape replays concern the particular programs and finite fixtures described there.

## Length and width dispatch

The initial raw branch applies through $n=2^{48}$. Counting and head restoration are included in its $4096n^2$ invoice. This is at most $2^{60}n$ on that interval. For $n\ge2$, $x\ge1$ gives $\Phi(x)\ge1$. The 19-state schoolbook machine and its dispatcher meet the time and cumulative-space bounds at $n=1$ as well. [Raw program and proof](schoolbook.md)

For larger $n$, set $\ell=\log _2n$, $z=\log _2\ell$, $b=\lceil\ell\rceil$, $h=\lceil n/b\rceil$, $k=\lceil\log _2(4h)\rceil$ and $N=2^k$. The program forms $k$ by counting digits of $4h-1$. Its canonical binary length is at least 4097 precisely when $k\ge2^{4096}$. From $n\le b2^{k-2}$ and $\log _2 b\le b/2$ we obtain $b<2k$. In particular $k\ge32$. Also $4h\le4(n/b+1)\le n/2\le 2^{b-1}$, giving $k\le b-1\le\ell$. Since $n\ge b(b-1)$,

$$Nk<8(n/b+1)(b-1)\le8n.$$

These arguments cover the dip in $h$ at a power of two. They position every input in a branch even when successive lengths do not increase $h$. Computing $b$, binary division, the width test and input restoration take less than the polynomial setup and raw scans on each transform branch. ## The ordinary radix-two branch

Let $q=k+1$, $Q=2^{128q}$ and $\eta=1/Q$. In forward decimation in frequency, the sum half computes the even Fourier coefficients and the positive-root-weighted difference half computes the odd Fourier coefficients. Iterating gives $RU$, with $U$ the positive normalized DFT and $R$ bit reversal. Inverse twiddles before each sum/difference in ascending stage size yield $U^*R$. Consequently two forward results may be multiplied in their stored order. The final factor $\sqrt N$ removes the normalized-convolution scalar, and $2h-2<N$ prevents circular overlap. This argument allows any occupied complex vectors in the individual transforms. [Numerical source](address-dyadic-caller.py)

For the half-angle cosine, rationalizing the square-root difference gives coordinate perturbation at most the incoming $\delta$ plus one truncation $\eta$. The new exact cosine is at least $1/\sqrt2$ and the represented one exceeds $1/2$ when $\delta\le1/8$. In the sine quotient the incoming numerator contributes at most $\delta$, the changed denominator at most $2(\delta+\eta)$, and truncation another $\eta$. Thus the two coordinate errors at the next level are bounded by $4(\delta+\eta)$. Starting at the exact root $i$, the complex error of every primitive root is below $\delta_0=2^{2k+3}\eta$. This verifies the small-perturbation premise for all $k\ge4$. At a stored rounded power the error obeys $E'\le(1+\delta_0)E+\delta_0+2\eta$. Since $N\delta_0<1/2$, summing gives $E\le2N(\delta_0+2\eta)\le 2^{3k+5}\eta<2^{-100k}$. Both square-root scalars, $1/\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt N$, have absolute error below $\eta$. [Root computation and rounding](address-dyadic-caller.md)

At incoming norm $2^{13k}$, perturbing a twiddle diagonal costs at most $2^{-87k}+2\eta\sqrt N$. An approximate normalized Hadamard layer costs at most $\sqrt2\,\eta 2^{13k}+2\eta\sqrt N$. Both bounds are below $2^{-85k}$ for $k\ge4$. Composition with the exact unitaries across at most $2k<2^k$ layers gives error below $\epsilon=2^{-84k}$. The norm reserve is preserved for initial norm below $2^{12k}$. The digit norms are below $2^{5k/2}$ because $h\le N/4$ and $b<2k$. Their rounded pointwise product has error at most $(2\cdot2^{5k/2}+\epsilon)\epsilon+2\eta\sqrt N<2^{-80k}$ and norm below $2^{6k}$. The adjoint adds at most $\epsilon$. Multiplying by the represented $\sqrt N$ leaves error below $1/4$ in the whole vector. Each real integer coefficient therefore rounds correctly. A coefficient is at most $h(2^b-1)^2$ and a carry at most $h(2^b-1)$ by induction. The reverse raw readout returns the exact promised width.

For a stage of length $m$, the first input half of each block is parked and read beside the second. First outputs enter the next stream immediately, and the parked second outputs follow. The matching $m/2$-entry twiddle row returns to home at each block. Each stage table exists once in the archive of $\sum_{j=1}^k2^{j-1}=N-1$ records. Preparing a stage can scan the entire archive once, and the total table-read volume for any stage is linear in the array. This avoids one archive search per butterfly. A complex record has $512q$ cells. Schoolbook word multiplication/division in the $8192q$-bit work registers uses at most $2^{14}(8192q+1)^3$ transitions. A square root uses at most $2^{18}(8192q+1)^4$ by high-to-low bit trials. Across $3kN/2$ butterflies and $k+2$ square roots the complete invoice is below $2^{90}Nq^4$. The 64 bulk and 128 bounded register heads use their own fixed origins. Their full two-sided intervals and dormant heads cost under $2^{21}Nq+2^{14}<2^{28}n$. The union includes archive construction, both parked spectra and the final bit reversal for raw output. [Physical execution and constants](address-dyadic-caller.md)

The coarser envelope $2^{160}Nq^6\le2^{170}nk^5$ suffices for the join. For $k<2^{4096}$ we have $5\le z<4097$. On $[5,8],[8,32],[32,64]$, respectively, $\Phi(z)\ge3,5,8$, while $170+5z\le210,330,490$. For $64\le z\le4097$, $z\le512\log _2(z+2)$ follows on splitting at 2048, giving $\Phi(z)\ge z/8$ and $170+5z\le71z/8$. The desired clock follows, even without its square-root factor on this branch.

## The coprime branch

All local outer arithmetic accepts $k\ge2^{256}$ and outer permutation setup accepts $k\ge2^{32}$. The successful axis search throughout that latter range comes from the updated short-axis certificate. Their hypotheses therefore hold at $k=2^{4096}$. The two Walsh subbranches cover $X=\lceil\log _2(k+2)\rceil$. [Outer arithmetic](outer-layers.md) · [Permutation setup](routing-outer-permutations.md) · [Coprime axes](address-axis-window.md)

* For $X<2^{26}$ take one exact integer group and $M=1+2^{k+w+s+2}$ with $w=85k+1$. Apply one unnormalized modular $H_2$ to each named bit. Separated selected positions and arbitrary occupied suffixes are allowed. The modulus still fits the $100k$-bit record. Every exact numerator is below $2^{w+s}<M/2$, which determines its signed centered lift. The final midpoint test normalizes it once. The physical volume term before this normalization is at most $2^{28}Nk^2$. The smaller threshold for the direct coefficient follows from $X/\log _2(X+2)\le2^{26}/26<140^3$, which yields $\sqrt k\le2^{70\Phi(X)}$. No finite adaptive descriptor is allocated on these inputs. [Direct butterfly source](node-walsh-review.md) · [Complete Walsh caller](walsh-node.md)
* At $X\ge2^{26}$ we have $\lceil\log _2(X+2)\rceil\le28X/2^{26}$. Since $62^3>28\cdot8192$, the largest adaptive $y$ obeys $128y\le(63/64)X$ after paying its integer rounding. Hence $m^{128}\le2^{X-7}\le k/32$, and $2000X\le k/32$. It follows that every lazy finite setup and associated polynomial can be paid within $2^{k/4}$ steps at every nonempty contracted role. Both the adaptive finite stage and its width guards apply down to this reduced breakpoint. The active spectral file has $(64(m+1)+128)8(m+1)^2(y+2)<2^{14}m^4\le m^{18}\le m^{55}\le k/32$ bits, since $m=2^y-1\ge2$ and $y\le m$. This replaces the superseded old guard $m^{128}\le k^{1/16}$ in the cumulative-space argument. [Adaptive toll and spectator guard](walsh-node.md) · [Updated spatial ledger](routing-outer-space.md)

Both Walsh branches have the same public coefficient $A_W=2^{110}\cdot5121$ and leading profile coefficient 70. The complete volume ledger bounds $\log_2[T/(n\sqrt\ell)]$ by $182+4\log _2(z+2)+70\Phi(z+2)$. For $z\ge4096$, $\Phi(z+2)\le\Phi(z)+2$ and $64\log _2(z+2)\le z$. The latter implies $4\log _2(z+2)\le\Phi(z)/4$ by cubing. Also $\Phi(z)\ge512$, so $182+140+1\le3\Phi(z)/4$. This yields $2T\le n\sqrt\ell\,2^{71\Phi(z)}$. Replacing $\ell,z$ by $L,x$ preserves the inequality. The two disjoint routing families, all outer banks and fixed homes contribute a total below $2^{29}Nk<2^{32}n$. The middle and small branches independently meet that same cap. [Global clock](integer-clock.md) · [Analytic join](all-lengths.lean)

## Independent computation

`python -I -B compiler-review-all-lengths.py` uses mpmath with 160 guard bits beyond the fixed-point grid, an explicit positive DFT in $O(N^2)$ operations, and explicit integer bit reversal. It checked 1,016 forward or adjoint complex outputs on arbitrary signed complex data and 501 stored twiddles for $2\le k\le8$. The largest observed error was below $2^{-382}$. A separate test ran the fixed direct two-point port through 2,321,651 original transition steps, over 26 selected-bit masks. All centered lifts matched independent signed dense sums, and all scratch heads returned home. The same script checked 68 exact rounded integer shapes near powers of two. The independent suite also reran the dyadic supplier's 51 original integer products through 4,097-bit operands and every reconstructed coefficient, and the schoolbook supplier's 1,414 original input pairs.

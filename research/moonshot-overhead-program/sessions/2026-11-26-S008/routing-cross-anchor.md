# Cross-anchor delivery on occupied tapes

## Question and result

At every source the adaptive interchanger uses the same data in many directed rectangles. Can one reserve preparation serve their destinations while a tape head traverses the source once? We tested two ways to share this work. The first keeps the canonical rectangles and passes a prepared copy between them. The second groups destinations across the original anchor letters before moving any data.

1. Every source in the canonical construction has at least $M=(q-1)^d(t-1)^{d(d-1)}$ mutually incompatible endpoint requirements, where $q=2^t$. A stream can serve at most one of these requirements using only prefix-preserving address changes. Every tree of whole-stream views in the usual spatial-call compiler must therefore pay at least $(M-1)V$ total child volume for a pencil of encoded volume $V$. This applies to nonlinear reserve-only preparations as well as the linear star bases. The obstruction already holds modulo 3 at every positive field width.
2. There is a better partition across anchor letters. In each product of first-difference dyadic halves, pin both sides in a narrowest coordinate. Its exact local load is optimal at every vertex among refinements confined to these dyadic boxes. It improves the actual number of endpoint preparations by an unbounded factor on a rank-admissible family. Its optimized logarithmic invoice still has the same order as the previous one.

Write $u=\log_2m$ for the local log arity and $\Phi(u)=(u^2\log_2(u+2))^{1/3}$. Neither construction lowers the full-integer upper envelope. The lower bounds in this note concern the indicated schedules and partitions.

## Endpoint partitions under a shared preparation

Use the [source pencil and its endpoint layouts](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/interchange.tex) and the [dyadic realization](../2026-11-26-S003/route-b/explicit-dyadic-geometry.md). The data algebra has characteristic two. Address arithmetic here is over $R=\mathbb Z/3^f$ for arbitrary $f\ge1$. Fix one query and all its unchanged spectators. An oriented endpoint $i$ uses an orthonormal pair $p_i=(u_i,z_i)$, reversed at a destination. Its two physical main columns map to $p_i$. Its active reserve directions lie in the orthogonal complement of $p_i$. Displaced spares are independent coordinates, so the common address is in the active ambient space plus a fixed spare space. Ignore the spare component in all pairings.

A **view** of one entire endpoint row uses a bijection of its occupied early and late reserves, independent of both main values. Source and destination may use different such bijections. An address map preserves the two prefix partitions if it and its inverse preserve equality of the early prefix, and equality of the early prefix together with both main values. The source's triangular maps have this property. Allow any intermediate bijection with this property. Distinct rectangles may use different common charts. Each member must still deliver its real endpoint row through the same chart as every other member of that rectangle, at the same common address.

**Lemma 1.** Suppose several rectangles incident to a physical source $v$ use the same two prefix partitions at $v$ in common-address space. Every pair of oriented endpoints in their union has mutual Gram block $I_2$ modulo 3.

*Proof.* Select the coarse block $B$ containing the zero common address in the first partition. Inside $B$ take all the fine blocks from the second partition. Project addresses modulo 3, retaining spare coordinates. Let $W$ be the linear span of differences of projected points within a common fine block, taken over all fine blocks in $B$. Let $F$ be the span of all differences of projected points in $B$. These are invariants of the partitions. Every endpoint of any one rectangle has the same two partitions in its ambient chart, because its change to common layout preserves the two prefix cuts. At $v$ the partitions agree across the chosen rectangles, so $W,F$ are the same for all their endpoints.

At any endpoint $i$, fixing one early prefix and both main values leaves just a set of reserve coordinates, translated by those two main columns. Thus $W$ is orthogonal to $p_i$. As the main values range independently over all of $R^2$, the fine blocks inside $B$ are the translates of a *single* reserve set by the plane $P_i=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_3}(p_i)$ after reduction. Equivalently the set before reduction has one translate for each main pair. It follows that $F=W+P_i$. This step uses the independence of the reserve preparation from the main values, and holds even if different fine blocks overlap after reduction.

For each $i$ the true same-rectangle Gram with $v$ is $I_2$. Write either oriented column of $i$ as a combination of the two columns of $v$ plus an element of $W$, using $F=W+P_v$. Taking its two pairings with $p_v$ determines the combination: it is the corresponding column of $v$. Thus the respective columns of $p_i$ and $p_v$ agree modulo $W$. Since $W$ annihilates every other pair $p_j$, their mutual Gram is $I_2$. This proves the claim. No common totally isotropic extension or linearity of the reserve permutation was assumed. ∎

## An incompatible family at every source

Use the ordinary parameters $d=2^r\ge2$, $h=2d-1$, $q=2^t$, $t\ge2$. Labels are $h$ outer words of $h$ letters each. Write $k(i,j)$ for the number of outer positions at inner Hamming distance exactly $d$. The unoriented Gram block is $(1-k/d)I_2+(k/d)S$, with $S$ the transposition matrix. Thus a same-side pair with $k=1$, or a source/destination pair with $k=d-1$, violates the oriented identity also modulo 3, since $d$ is invertible there.

At an arbitrary source $v$, fix $d$ outer changing positions. In each fix $d$ inner changing positions. The first of these is the anchor. Freeze every other letter outside the selected positions at its value in $v$. At the $d$ anchors choose any opposite letters. At each of the $d(d-1)$ remaining selected positions choose a first-differing-bit depth $b\in\{1,\ldots,t-1\}$, numbering bits from most to least significant. These choices give $M$ canonical rectangles incident to $v$. At one letter $x$ of $v$, the source half $L_b(x)$ contains letters agreeing with $x$ through bit $b$. The destination half $K_b(x)$ contains letters agreeing through bit $b-1$ and differing at bit $b$.

**Lemma 2.** Any two different rectangles in this family are incompatible in Lemma 1.

*Proof.* If their depth vectors differ, choose a place with unequal depths $c<b$ and take the source side of the shallower rectangle and destination side of the deeper one. The inclusion $K_b(x)\subset L_c(x)$ allows one word of the former source to equal a destination at this place. Take the source to be $v$ except for this one replacement. The destination still differs from $v$ at all prescribed positions. This source/destination pair has $k=d-1$ even if the two rectangles chose different anchor letters.

If their depths agree, select an outer position at which their destination anchors differ. In that position take two destinations differing at every nonanchor changed letter as well. Each relevant $K_b(x)$ has at least two letters because $b\le t-1$. In every other outer position use the same nonanchor letters. Its two words then differ in at most one anchor letter, fewer than $d$. This destination/destination pair has exactly $k=1$. Lemma 1 excludes common preparation in both cases. ∎

For comparison, if every nonanchor depth equals $t$, the corresponding halves are singletons. For fixed $v$ this entire family of opposite anchor letters really can form one larger destination set under the same source: each pair of its inner destination words differs at most at the anchor. This is a positive control for the quantifier in Lemma 2. Rectangle repartition can take advantage of it.

### Physical invoice and parameter range

The tested traversal allows copies, a trie, arbitrary ordering and grouping of labels in a single call. Each live source view still represents a complete, independently populated label row with its entire spectator range. Copies and invertible operations preserving both partitions do not introduce a new partition at that label. In the [elementary address compiler](../2026-11-26-S003/route-b/explicit-dyadic-geometry.md#7-physical-elementary-operations-and-radix-compatibility), an operation that crosses one of these cuts on this view uses at least one spatial exchange of its full width-$f$ fields. An exchange can introduce at most one new view per participating full row. Starting from one view, recording $M$ incompatible views in a chain or branching tree therefore issues at least $M-1$ such exchanges per row. Batching rows as spectators adds their full bit volumes. Summing gives at least $(M-1)V$. Giving group selection, branching copies, source scans, rank scatter, restoration and all head returns free in this estimate only decreases the bill. The inequality is for this physical call list. An algorithm recomputing a crossing with ordinary tape steps, splitting the row into separately routed fragments, or combining independent sources before furnishing complete views has a different bill.

One corresponding fixed-tape upper schedule enumerates the outer and inner supports, the nonzero offset of each opposite anchor from its source letter modulo $q$, and the first-differing depths. At a fixed signature its rectangles partition the endpoints on each side. The prefix, half and anchor at each endpoint follow from its actual word. Small signatures are enumerable in polynomial $m$ time and space using the current $N\le m^{O(1)}$ guard. No runtime sort of the payload is needed. One can always undo the previous view on a full row and apply the next via two $O(D)$ lists. The simpler serial rectangle order in [UP](../2026-11-26-S003/route-a/uniform-payload-shared-pencil.md) gives the same upper estimate. It gathers each source copy by a paid scan of the complete marked bank, sends the actual query sum through destination maps and restores each prepared accumulator. Its second pass returns the low-rank contributions. The rank arrays have combined volume $mrV/N\le V$. Extra source copies, partial sums and restored accumulators use only constantly many marked full banks, reusing one rectangle at a time. [AQ-P](../../contributions/explicit-overhead/AQ-P.md) gives the stack and complete-prefix returns. Thus the normalized recursive volume upper bound for the retained execution is $C D(1+\lambda)(1+mr/N)$, with ordinary work $m^{O(1)}V$ and compact descriptors. The lower bound survives the optimistic prefix-sharing variant of this schedule.

Set $u=\log_2 m\ge16$ and require the source's sufficient rank guard $t d^2\ge u+2d^2-2$. Then $t\ge3$. Put $d_0=(u/\log_2u)^{1/3}$. If $d\le d_0$, then $\log_2 M\ge d(t-1)\ge u/(2d)\ge u^{2/3}(\log_2u)^{1/3}/2$. Otherwise

$$
\log_2 M\ \ge\ \tfrac12d^2\log_2(1+(u-2)/d^2)
\ \ge\ \tfrac12 d_0^2\log_2(1+(u-2)/d_0^2)
\ \ge\ \tfrac16u^{2/3}(\log_2u)^{1/3}.
$$

Here $s\log(1+c/s)$ increases with $s>0$ for $c>0$, and $(u-2)/d_0^2\ge u^{1/3}$ in this range. The ordinary parameter choice in [C2](../../routes/B/characteristic-two-label-geometry.md) attains logarithmic cost $O(\Phi(u))$. The new inequality accounts for actual changes required by the proposed view traversal. It is stronger in this model than the older [opposite-anchor isotropic obstruction](../2026-11-26-S003/route-b/common-isotropic-preparation-obstruction.md), which priced only the $(q-1)^d$ choices and explicitly left the depth factors and view-transition charge open.

## Regroup first, then visit the narrowest coordinate

The changed trial drops the old first-coordinate anchor in the grouping stage. Fix the changing coordinates of an inner pair at distance $d$ and all common outside letters. For all $d$ changing letters take their unique first-difference directed dyadic halves. This gives disjoint coarse boxes $L_1\times\cdots\times L_d$ against $K_1\times\cdots\times K_d$, with disjoint $L_i,K_i$ and equal side lengths $s_i=2^{t-b_i}$. The coarse box alone may contain two far words within one side. Choose the first coordinate attaining $c=\min_i s_i$. Refine the box into $c^2$ rectangles by fixing the source and destination letters separately at this coordinate. In every such rectangle each side varies in at most $d-1$ positions. All cross pairs remain at distance $d$ and each pair still occurs exactly once. A fixed source uses exactly $c$ rectangles in this box, as does a fixed destination. When this coordinate differs from the former anchor, many old opposite-anchor letters can share a real traversal in the new rectangle.

**Lemma 3 (pointwise optimum inside a dyadic box).** Any cover of this coarse box by rectangles whose sides have no pair at distance $d$ has at least $c$ incidences at every source and every destination. Disjointness and product sides are unnecessary for the lower bound.

*Proof.* In the destination box choose $c$ words with $c$ different letters at each of the $d$ coordinates. Every two of these words differ in all $d$. A legal destination side contains at most one. For any fixed source all $c$ edges must be covered, so that source belongs to at least $c$ rectangles. Interchange source and destination for the other inequality. The preceding pin rule attains both pointwise. ∎

It follows that the exact regular incidence per side at the inner level is $\binom{h}{d}A_d(t)$, where

$$
 A_d(t)=\sum_{(b_1,\ldots,b_d)\in[t]^d}2^{t-\max_i b_i}
       =\sum_{b=1}^t 2^{t-b}\big[b^d-(b-1)^d\big].
$$

There is one coarse box for each depth vector at a fixed vertex. Lemma 3 applies to each independently when refinements stay in these boxes. The retained outer product therefore has exact regular load $\lambda_{\rm new}=\binom{h}{d}^{d+1}A_d(t)^d$. This optimality allows the anchor to move between coordinates and each refined side to be correlated. It concerns box-confined inner refinements followed by the source outer product. Rectangles crossing complete dyadic boxes can have smaller loads.

The old factor was $A_{\rm old}=(q-1)t^{d-1}$. Indeed pinning coordinate one after the full dyadic split gives this value by summing $2^{t-b_1}$ over all depth vectors. Thus $A_d(t)\le A_{\rm old}$ pointwise after summation. For $d=2$ the new exact value is $3\cdot 2^t-2t-3$. The [E002 cross-pinned partition](../../results/epsilon-uniformity/sessions/E002/review-a/CROSS-PINNED-RECTANGLE-PARTITION.md) has a different scope and can beat this value, including at $d=2$. Its normalized inner factor is $(q-1)t^{d-2}$.

**Unbounded comparison.** For every $t,d\ge1$,

$$
 \max(2^{t-1},t^d)\le A_d(t)\le q\,2^d d!.
$$

The lower estimates count the all-one depths or one contribution from each depth vector. For the upper estimate use $b^d\le d!\binom{b+d-1}{d}$ and $\sum_{b\ge1}\binom{b+d-1}{d}2^{-b}=2^d$. Now let powers of two $d$ tend to infinity, take $t=8d\log_2 d$ and $m=2^{d^2(t-2)}+1$. This $t$ is the least that passes the actual rank guard. With $u_0=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ and $v_0=\lceil\log_2(u_0+2)\rceil$, one has $u_0/(d^3v_0)\to8/3$. Thus the [AI selector](../2026-11-26-S003/route-b/adaptive-interchange.md) chooses this same $d$ eventually, and $m$ is a legal odd arity. On this family

$$
 \frac{A_{\rm old}}{A_d(t)}\ge\frac1{2t}\left(\frac{t}{2d}\right)^d.
$$

The old/new outer incidence ratio is consequently at least $2^{\Omega(d^2\log_2\log_2 d)}$. Dividing the inner ratio by $t$ compares the E002 cross-pinned load to the new load and gives the same outer order on this family. For example, $d=4,t=64,m=2^{992}+1$ passes the literal selector. Exact integer comparison gives $2^{47}\le\lambda_{\rm old}/\lambda_{\rm new}<2^{48}$ and $2^{23}\le\lambda_{\rm cross}/\lambda_{\rm new}<2^{24}$. These ratios count the real endpoint-row occurrences in the two lists. They are not ratios of measured tape runtimes or of the completed multiplication costs.

**Physical costs.** Construct the plan before processing payload. The finite generator can enumerate the $N^2$ ordered endpoint pairs, test the supports, compute the literal first-difference bits and the narrowest-coordinate pin, and deduplicate the resulting descriptors by scans of the finite table. Listing memberships and rank incidences takes polynomial $m$ time, cells and description size under the same $N\le m^{O(1)}$ guard. Only these finite lists need grouping. The same inner word support and within-side condition give exactly the old scalar $P_*$ and rank $r$; its rank basis and dyadic address dimension $D=2(2+h q^h)$ are unchanged. Each outer side is a product of disjoint inner sides at changing positions, as required by the explicit reserve-only layout. For each new rectangle one common-address sum serves all its prescribed destinations at each query. UP's scanning, occupied-spectator conversions, second rank pass, head returns and fixed-bank restoration apply verbatim to the actual lists. They give recursive child bit volume at most $C D(1+\lambda_{\rm new})(1+mr/N)V$, child size at most $V/N$, ordinary work $m^{O(1)}V$ plus polynomial descriptor arithmetic, and a constant multiple of $V$ in reused payload banks. All copies and local basis changes are paid. Enlarging the degree in the finite constructor before fixing the root parameter $R$ preserves AQ-P's direct-space bound.

**Why the rate test fails.** The best positive incidence invoice in this box-confined method still contains $d\log_2 A_d(t)\ge\max(d(t-1),d^2\log_2t)$. At $u\ge16$ the split at $d_0$ used above gives $d\log_2 A_d(t)\ge\tfrac16u^{2/3}(\log_2u)^{1/3}$. Taking the old valid parameters gives the matching upper order. Separately the Walsh construction already uses the unpinned first-difference boxes and needs only cross orthogonality; this change gives it no additional aggregation. Its [reviewed call-list bound](../2026-11-26-S004/route-a/shared-fourier-invoice-scale.md) retains a logarithmic loss of order $\sqrt{u\log(u+2)}$. Keeping its positive polynomial-arity routing toll at the square-root width scale also retains the [nonstationary certificate bound](../../results/epsilon-uniformity/sessions/E003/route-b/WALSH-TOLL-INVOICE-BARRIER.md) of order $\Phi$ even under an ideal interchange supplier. These are comparisons of specified upper-bound calculations; none is a lower bound on unrestricted multiplication.

The first unresolved operation for a log-free construction is delivery that can cross the full split-depth boxes *and* combine arbitrary independently occupied rows before requesting all their incompatible prepared views, or a different construction of both arrangements. The prior [JR conditional transfer](../2026-11-26-S004/route-a/joint-rectangle-two-scale-transfer.md) supplies the exact unchanged targets. Close these two proposed trials. The actual complete written bound before and after is $T(n)=O(n\sqrt{L}\,2^{O(\Phi(x))})$ with $L=1+\log_2n$, $x=\log_2L$, and $O(n)$ visited cells.

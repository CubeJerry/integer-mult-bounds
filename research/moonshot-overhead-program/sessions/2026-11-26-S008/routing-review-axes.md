The ordinary inclusion–exclusion degree in band $j$ is $4+2j$. Its weighted infinite sum is $12$. The tail beginning at $J$ is $2^{-J}(12+4J)$ by the constant and linear geometric sums. In one expression at most one band replaces its even truncation by the single next elementary symmetric polynomial. This adds at most one to the weighted exponent. The residual contains at most six primes, so the largest actual squarefree divisor is at most $U^6z^{13}/U^{12+4J}\le 2^{-44}z^{13}$. If $s$ leading bands have lower exponent at least 16, $s\le\log_2(B/16)$ and their survival is at least $5^{-s}\ge(16/B)^3$. Every subsequent prime is below $2^{32}$. The auxiliary product through $2^{32}$ exceeds $(10\cdot7\cdot5)^{-1}>1/384$, by the finite product through 256 and the next two band inequalities. Some auxiliary primes may already occur in a leading band. Using the product over all primes through $2^{32}$ only lowers the remaining subset's survival.

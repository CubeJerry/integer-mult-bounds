# Independent audit of the synchronized occupied Fourier transform

The [source algorithm](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/integers.tex) at revision 3344da5 supplies the mathematical construction. I independently expanded its gates, audited [the dyadic coefficient generator](outer-phases.py), and checked its interfaces to [streamed products](word-toom.md), [position permutations](routing-outer-permutations.md), and the [outer-layer ledger](outer-layers.md). The parameter $k$ is the log of the padded record count $N=2^k$. Bounds below hold at the ledger's declared $k\ge2^{2^{100}}$. The layer count, accuracy, and fixed-tape table-construction bounds already hold for $k\ge2^{32}$ on shapes of the indicated size.

## Count and occupied storage

Write $w=20\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$, $b_0=\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil$ and $r=2^q$ minimal with $r\ge2a-1$. Then $q\le w+1\le20b_0$. A normalized positive-sign radix-two transform consists of one bit reversal and $q$ rounds. In each round of size $m=2^j$, multiply the upper member of pair $(i,i+m/2)$ in that radix group by $\exp(2\pi i(i\bmod(m/2))/m)$, then apply the normalized two-point Hadamard to the pair. This gives at most $2q+1$ raw layers. The adjoint reverses that sequence and conjugates the twiddles. A radix transform restricted to the first $r$ rows of the virtual tuple is the identity on the other $r$ rows.

The lifted $V$ requires $4q+5$ raw layers. Its two transforms, two outer chirps and one matched-pair operation suffice. The 64 reflection products in $B$ require 129 occurrences of $V$ or $V^*$ and 128 signs. A sign $2P-I$ on a tuple extends by $+1$ at physical positions outside that tuple. Complement cancellation $D_1B^*D_2BD_3$ thus takes at most $1032q+1549$ raw layers. Each matched layer takes two position permutations, three shared Hadamards and three phase layers. The inactive identity pair has explicit factors
$$
D_{\rm out}=D_{\rm in}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-i),\qquad
D_{\rm mid}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(e(1/8),e(-1/8)\bigr)
$$
with $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. For a pure Hadamard all three diagonals are the identity. Hence replacing every raw layer by at most eight elementary layers is safe.

For $d\ge4$, write $d=4v+t$, $0\le t\le3$. Use $v-1$ blocks of four and a last block of size $4+t$. The other axes of a block each have at least one bit, giving at least eight fibres even when the final axis has a short width. The fibre count is a power of two. Eight equal batches of $v'$ fibres exist. Each active fibre needs $2r-a<7a$ extra sites. The other $7v'$ fibres supply at least $7v'a$, and include both padded and already transformed values. Their allocated sites are distinct. On each virtual tuple, $B=U_a\oplus B'$ implies $B^*(D_2\oplus I)B=(U_a^*D_2 U_a)\oplus I$, with the rightmost diagonal $D_3$ and leftmost $D_1$ both extending by identity. Thus every borrowed value returns in ideal arithmetic, including arbitrary nonzero complex spectators. An approximate execution is analyzed globally by its elementary unitary layers.

In at most three slots each unfinished block advances. The block contains at most seven axes, each has eight batches, and initial tuple assignment and its inverse cost at most two extra permutations per batch. Therefore one full forward or adjoint costs at most
$$
3\cdot 56\{8(1032q+1549)+2\}<2^{25}b_0<2^{29}b_0.
$$
A selected Walsh layer uses at most one bit per block. A block position permutation is supplied as a complete dense bijection on at most $7w\le140b_0$ bits with empty external controls, including its unused padded rows.

## Independent pointwise accuracy audit of the supplied code

Let $\upsilon=2^{-p}$ and $e_g=2^{-g}$. The code truncates toward zero. At most $g+3$ nonzero grid terms plus a first omitted alternating term give $20(g+4)e_g$ error in its Machin approximation to $\pi$. Reducing the numerator modulo the positive denominator puts the computed Taylor angle in $[0,7)$, with absolute error at most $41(g+4)e_g$. Its complex successive terms are alternating real or purely imaginary. Each step injects at most $e_g$ error. Through the absolute sum of all future terms this is at most $e^7 e_g<1100e_g$, since $i!/(i+t)!\le1/t!$. A computed zero may be silently continued by zero to the loop limit $J=16p+80$. At indices $j\ge14$ the exact terms decrease by a factor at most $1/2$, and the fourteenth is below $e^7$. The tail is at most $2^{26-J}\le e_g$. The true exponential is 1-Lipschitz in a real argument. Before final truncation, the total complex error is bounded by $2^{16}(p+10)e_g$. With $g=p+2\,\mathrm{bitlength}(p+8)+40$, the final componentwise truncation makes the returned complex error less than $2\upsilon$.

The integer square root used for $a^{-1/2}$ introduces less than $\upsilon$. An exact sum of at most $2a$ roots, followed by normalization and complex truncation, has absolute error at most $12(a+1)\upsilon$. The source proves the exact multiplier has modulus at most 40. Multiplication by the rounded $\sin(\pi/258)$ yields an error $\epsilon_z\le128(a+1)\upsilon$. When $\epsilon_z\le2^{-16}$ both arguments of the completion square root stay above $0.73$. Its perturbation, including the integer square root, is at most $2\epsilon_z$. The four entries of the rounded $HL$ differ from their ideals by at most $E=8\epsilon_z$, including the approximation to $1/\sqrt2$ and complex truncation.

The exact magnitudes $c,s$ are at least $(\sqrt3-1)/(2\sqrt2)>0.258$. The computed magnitudes differ by at most $2E$. For a unit quotient $v/d$ the rounded complex division error is at most $(|\Delta v|+|\Delta d|)/|d'|+2\upsilon$. At $E\le2^{-16}$, $\gamma$ and $Q_{10}/(is)$ each have error at most $14E$. The computed $is\gamma$ differs by at most $20E$ from its exact value. Its magnitude, as well as the other rounded divisor magnitudes, exceeds $1/4$, satisfying the program's actual division assertion. The last quotient $Q_{01}/(is\gamma)$ has error below $86E$. Hence all local phases have error below $2^{20}(a+1)2^{-p}$, including the identity-pair constants. With $p=2048k$ and $a\le(2k)^{20}$ this is below $2^{-100k}$ and establishes both perturbation prerequisites. An ideal phase and a partial prefix both have modulus one. Rounding local cells and products to $80k$ fractional bits gives $u=4\cdot2^{-80k}$ and
$$
E_j\le2((1+u)^j-1)\le4ku<2^{-70k}\quad(j\le k).
$$
The Python memoization and arbitrary precision DFT serve the replay. Only the value of $\pi$ needs to persist for the actual grid compiler.

## A deliberately loose independent physical upper bound for setup

For $k\ge2^{32}$ every block has at most $D=(2k)^{140}\le k^{145}$ sites, each axis length is below $k^{21}$, and $r\le k^{22}$. There are at most $k$ blocks and at most $k^2$ elementary layers. A dense table for all layer/block/site triples has at most $k^{148}$ triples. Thirty-two word slots per triple allow a partner, forward and inverse indices, fibre and virtual indices, gate type, phases, and running marks. Two active scratch lists and a schedule descriptor use fewer than another $64k^3(D+1)$ slots. The entire marked arena fits $k^{150}$ words. Adjoint traversals can reuse it. The separate routing supplier prepares its own guarded permutation data.

A fixed rule can decode a virtual wire $(f,j)$ without searching over subsets. If $j<a$, it addresses the active fibre $f$. Otherwise the donor has ordinal $u=f'(2r-a)+(j-a)$ in the pool outside the current batch, with $f'$ the ordinal within that batch. Quotient and remainder by the padded axis length select the donor fibre and its local coordinate. Skip the active contiguous batch in fibre order. Ordinary division by the products of the at most seven preceding widths encodes and decodes a local block index. For a radix pairing its stage bit determines the mate, and for a lifted pairing the mate lies in the opposite half. Each virtual and idle pair is routed, in its selected orientation, to indices $2i,2i+1$. Pair up any remaining physical sites in increasing order. For a radix pair the first member is the low-stage-bit wire; for the lift it belongs to the first virtual half. Sorting the pair by physical index without preserving this orientation would change the gate. The inverse is produced from the same pair list. Complete table scans suffice for locating any ordered pair. Even testing all $D^2$ pairs individually for every one of the $D$ requested output rows fits the following allowance.

Allow $2^{24}(D+1)^2(k+1)^4\le k^{296}$ bounded-word instructions per output row for gate/address setup. A loop over each ordered pair has at most seven field quotient/remainder and stride operations per index, at most seven corresponding encodes for its mate, and at most 32 comparisons, sign, type or counter updates. An additional scan over all at most $k^2$ layer descriptors per candidate still fits the allowance. Idle-pair completion and search for the requested row take at most two more such passes. A coefficient needs at most $2a+4$ roots. Each root has fewer than $2^{16}k$ Taylor terms and two arctangent loops below $2^{12}k$ iterations with fewer than 32 word instructions per iteration. Integer square roots take at most $2^{16}k$ trial bits with fewer than 16 word instructions each. Dividing, multiplying and comparing words count as individual instructions at this level. Recomputing the constants for each coefficient separately fits $k^{26}$ instructions per row. All rows and initial loops use fewer than $k^{446}$ instructions combined.

A signed slot of $W=2^{19}k\le k^2$ bits suffices for the $2048k$-bit fraction, Taylor products, square-root remainders and grid-division numerators. Even constructing $239^{2g+7}$ explicitly needs fewer than $16(g+4)<W/4$ bits. Addresses for all $k^{150}$ slots fit. Use a fixed set of marked tapes and store the slots consecutively. An indexed access compares or counts down the ordinal while sweeping the occupied arena, copies a constant number of slots, then rewinds. Even a separate $W$-step address comparison per bit costs at most $2^{12}k^{150}(W+1)^2$ steps per instruction. A marked binary shift/add, schoolbook product or long division, including operand transport, heads returning and clearing scratch, fits $2^{12}(W+1)^3$ by scanning bounded rows at most $O(W)$ times per processed bit. One can implement this by fixed loops over at most $W+1$ result bits, $W+1$ trial bits, and eight monotone marked sweeps of length at most $8(W+1)$ each. Together the bounds are less than $k^{160}$ per instruction. Writing or erasing the arena fits one further complete pass; after it is rewound, its future intervals are identical. This yields below $k^{620}$ transitions and $k^{155}$ distinct cells for the Fourier tables and schedules. It verifies the proposed $k^{850}$ budget independently. Enumerating the coprime axes and constructing the controlled CRT switch tables have their own [numerical bounds](routing-outer-permutations.md).

## Stream placement, rounding, and recovery

For a fixed global diagonal, copy the current block's complete coefficient table once to a bank with marked start and end. Read the current prefix tape from its first to last coefficient. Hold one coefficient on a short-word bank, scan the entire local table while appending products in order, then rewind just that local table to its marked start. Advance the prefix tape. Alternate two prefix banks after each block. Bank lengths at least double, so fewer than $2N$ new products are written across all blocks and exactly $N$ are applied. The total volume of local table scans and local rewinds is bounded by their emitted record volume. Only the once-per-block setup needs access to the polynomial-size global table archive. Tails of earlier longer banks can be erased on their prior extent or hidden behind an unambiguous active limit, charging a constant number of full-bank sweeps per layer. Retain all unscheduled input/output banks at fixed origins.

At the common $72k$ data grid a represented boundary component below $2^{13k}$ has fewer than $86k$ magnitude bits. Local streamed coefficients use $80k+2$ bits. All streamed multiplications, including unscaled coordinatewise products, partial phases and final scaling, therefore use at most $96k$ magnitude bits per input. Four unsigned calls with signs implement a complex product, with the two additions and exact fixed-point grid conversions supplied by monotone sweeps. The [short-word interface](word-toom.md) charges $2^{28}(w+1)^{3/2}+128(w+1)$ per signed retained multiplication. Even allowing $2^{12}(w+1)$ further steps for both accumulator sums, directed truncation, output, and return gives at most $2^{32}(w+1)^{3/2}$ per complex product. Thus the three streams altogether cost below $2^{45}N k^{3/2}$ per diagonal, with $w\le96k$; address setup and selected-Walsh calls are separate. The common data grid bounds complex rounding below $2^{-70k}$. A phase layer on an input of norm below $2^{13k}$ has total Euclidean error at most $2^{-70k}(2^{13k}+\sqrt N)<2^{-50k}$.

For $b=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\ge4$, $n\le b h\le b2^{k-2}$ and $\log_2 b\le b/2$ imply $b<2k$. A digit array has norm below $2^{5k/2}$. The above layer count obeys $2^{29}b_0<2^k$ at $k\ge1024$. On any represented input of norm at most $2^{12k}$, each exact layer is unitary, so the total error is below $\epsilon=2^{-49k}$ and every boundary norm remains below $2^{13k}$. The forward errors produce coordinatewise-product error at most
$$
\epsilon(2\cdot2^{5k/2}+\epsilon)+\sqrt N\,2^{-70k}<2^{-45k}.
$$
The represented product has norm below $2^{6k}$. The adjoint adds at most $\epsilon$. Scale by $\sqrt S\le2^{k/2}$ approximated to absolute error $2^{-70k}$, computed by an integer square root of $S$ shifted by twice the desired fraction width. Including the scaling perturbation and final record rounding, the full Euclidean error is below $1/4$. Ideal transforms preserve the valid-address tensor subspace. The occupied cyclic convolution length $S\ge2h$ prevents wraparound, so nearest-integer rounding recovers every ordinary coefficient after inverse CRT. This argument also bounds any numerical leakage to padded sites.

Each true coefficient is at most $h(\beta-1)^2$ for $\beta=2^b$. Starting at zero, the incoming carry is at most $h(\beta-1)$ by induction: if the bound holds, $(h(\beta-1)^2+h(\beta-1))/\beta=h(\beta-1)$. The valid address $2h-1$ exists, so the last emitted nonzero carry digit is included even for two all-ones inputs. Higher rank coefficients are zero. The final binary product occupies at most $2n$ bits, and trimming or zero-padding the leading end produces the required fixed length.

## Independent finite replay

Run python3 -I -B address-review-outer.py in this directory. The [checker](address-review-outer.py) uses only the Python standard library and its own matrix operations. It tests the six-reflection squarings, the sign and parity of both chirps, complement cancellation and three-Hadamard factors for $a=1,2,3,4,5,7,8,9$. The largest row-sum error of the restored matrix was $2.27\cdot10^{-13}$ and the largest off-block leakage $5.48\cdot10^{-14}$. Seven explicit bit-reversal/twiddle/Hadamard transforms through radix 64 differed from their positive-sign DFT by at most $5.04\cdot10^{-15}$ in maximum row sum. Five arbitrary complex occupied arrays in blocks of four through seven axes, including multiple active fibres, had at most $3.72\cdot10^{-13}$ pointwise error. A 1,024-site box with cyclic axis lengths $3,4,5,7$ and 420 valid residues recovered all 145 convolution coefficients for two 73-digit inputs and their exact carried integer product. Using ordinary double precision the largest valid error was $1.55\cdot10^{-8}$. The unbounded parameter and true-precision arguments above are independent of those finite floating-point comparisons.

# An explicit overhead for integer multiplication on fixed tapes

## Numerical bound for the complete product

One fixed deterministic finite-alphabet multitape machine takes two \(n\)-bit words, permits leading zeros, and writes their complete \(2n\)-bit product on a fresh tape. Set \(L=1+\log_2n,\ x=\log_2 L,\ \Phi(x)=(x^2\log_2(x+2))^{1/3}\). For every \(n\ge2\) its worst-case transition count satisfies
\[
                         T(n)\le n\sqrt L\,2^{71\Phi(x)} .
\]
At \(n=1\) it takes at most 4096 transitions. Every positive input length satisfies the same bound of \(2^{32}n\) on distinct tape/address pairs visited before halt. On the explicit range \(\log_2\log_2n\ge11{,}665{,}715{,}140{,}844{,}862\) the same program satisfies \(T(n)\le n(\ln n)^{501/1000}\), including the leading coefficient. The published asymptotic theorem and the fork's earlier extraction are credited below. This proof replaces unspecified constants and onset with an all-length formula and a numerical sufficient threshold. ## Complete factor transforms at each fixed precision

The [complete factor transform](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S007/binary-transform-and-product.md) now converts from contiguous binary coefficients to every literal monic factor remainder and inverts arbitrary independent residues. For $b=3c$, $q=2^b$, its clock is $O_\eta(qb/(1+\log b)^\eta)$ with $O(q)$ cells ever visited, for each fixed rational $0<\eta<1/2$. The [first carry](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S007/first-witt-carry.md) identifies the actual new high bits for the same factors modulo four. It reduces to one ordinary divided square and a fixed number of binary transforms. The [converse](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S007/first-carry-converse.md) recovers ordinary modulo-four multiplication using this carry. [Truncated Witt coordinates](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S007/fixed-witt-precision.md) extend both directions to every fixed $h$ over $\mathbf Z/2^h$ with the same time order and constants depending on $h$. The integer theorem below retains its previous explicit overhead. [Decision and external-review scope](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S007/FINAL-REPORT.md)

**Theorem.** One deterministic machine with a fixed finite alphabet, a fixed number of one-dimensional tapes and one head per tape computes the full $2n$-bit product of all pairs of unsigned $n$-bit strings, for every $n\ge1$. Inputs and output are contiguous in the prescribed order. The output tape is blank outside the product. Absolute constants $A,B,S$ bound the worst-case steps and the distinct tape/address pairs visited through halt by
$$
 T(n)\le A n\sqrt L\,2^{B\Phi(x)},\qquad \mathrm{space}(n)\le S n,\qquad
 L=1+\log_2 n,\quad x=\log_2 L,\quad\Phi(t)=(t^2\log_2(t+2))^{1/3}.
$$
The upper bound is unconditional. This is the S003 adaptive extraction with the proof refinements below. It is a written AI-assisted construction. The programme also has an independent same-order construction using a different field layout and a chunking wrapper. [S003 theorem and admission](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S003/lead/ADMISSION-AQ.md) · [E002 construction](../../results/epsilon-uniformity/sessions/E002/lead/variable-profile-theorem.md)

The published tape theorem already gives one machine with $\log_2 H=o(x)$ in $T=O(n\sqrt L\,H)$. Here the quantitative upper envelope is $\log_2 H=O(\Phi(x))$ and $\delta=(\log_2 H)/x=O((\log_2(x+2)/x)^{1/3})$ as $x\to\infty$. Both results give every fixed excess above exponent $1/2$. The adaptive proof changes the finite template representation and prices the resulting algorithm. Choosing an input-dependent exponent in a fixed-parameter estimate alone would leave its constants and thresholds uncontrolled. Priority for this rate remains unestablished. ## 1. Exact movement of independent bits

Let $V$ be the incoming bit volume of two complete width-$e$ ternary address fields with arbitrary complete prefix, gap and suffix ranges. Interchanging those fields restores occupied spectators. Let $K$ bound the local compiler degrees. Choose a fixed integer $R\ge4$ with $K/R\le1/8$. Above a fixed base, use the largest odd $m\le e^{1/R}$. Write $\ell$ for the field and prefix-batch width and $a$ for the reserve parameter. The child width satisfies
$$
 f=\left\lfloor\frac{e-\ell}{m+2a}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 f\le e/m,\quad \log_2 f\le\theta\log_2 e,\quad \theta=1-\tfrac1{2R}<1 .
$$
The source scalar identity works on independent inputs. Its adaptive implementation has $N_{\rm lab}\le m^5$ labels, $mr/N_{\rm lab}\le1$, and $\log_2(D_g(1+\lambda))=O(\Phi(\log_2 m))$, where $r$ bounds rank, $D_g$ is an auxiliary dimension and $\lambda$ bounds rectangle incidence. The last estimate balances $\log(m)/d$ against $d^2\log\log m$ at dyadic $d$ of order $(\log m/\log\log m)^{1/3}$. Only one rectangle's subsets are needed at once. All query/rank accumulators together fit one input volume. A full block of independent bits packs bijectively into field coordinates. A spatial child gets the raw bits and can choose a different field. Each local finite repair uses four sequential subproblems on one sixteenth of its parent data.

For $h=\lceil(\log_2 m)/4\rceil$, $D_p\le24\sqrt m$ and $R_{\rm pen}=O(D_g(1+\lambda)(1+mr/N_{\rm lab}))$, the main child volume divided by $V$ before leading padding is at most $Q_m=8D_pR_{\rm pen}+3m4^{-h}$. Leading padding is less than two, and is removed before the $2a$ full-volume reserve calls. Thus actual total child volume with multiplicity is at most $V\sqrt m\,2^{O(\Phi(\log_2 m))}$. Every main spatial child has at most $V/2$ bits. Reserve calls take the full current result one at a time. Local work including setup, scans and returns is $O(m^K V)$. [Exact compiler, finite suppliers and parameter bounds](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S003/route-b/adaptive-interchange.md) · [Published interchange identity](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/interchange.tex)

Use $P(V,e)=V\sqrt{\max(1,e)}\,2^{C\Phi(\log_2\max(1,e))}$. Since $\Phi(\theta t)\le\theta^{2/3}\Phi(t)$, $f\le e/m$ cancels the $\sqrt m$ in the volume bound. For one sufficiently large $C$ the sum of child potentials is at most $P/4$. The local-work ratio is bounded since $m^K/\sqrt e\le e^{-3/8}$. Induction gives time $O(P)$. Full-volume returns reuse fixed tape intervals. The two stacks are accessed at their ends, and only contracted calls park payload. All marked regions together occupy $O(V)$ distinct cells. A fresh origin at every full-volume reserve would use $\Omega(V\log\log e)$ cells on such a path. Binary fields are padded once to ternary ranges, increasing volume by less than nine. The resulting cost per input bit at binary width $u$ is
$$
 J(u)=\sqrt{1+u}\,2^{O(\Phi(\log_2(u+2)))}.
$$
The [physical proof and strict child allowance](AQ-P.md) cover arbitrary prefixes, repeated calls, offset restores and base cases.

## 2. Walsh rows, exact internally

Set $N=2^k$. The integer caller selects $s\le c k/\log_2(k+2)$ address bits for a normalized Walsh transform on $N$ complex records with $O(k)$-bit components. Move the selected bits into place by guarded permutations. At an even active width $a$, put $x_a=\lceil\log_2(a+2)\rceil$, $v=\lceil\log_2(x_a+2)\rceil$, $y=\lceil(x_a^2v)^{1/3}\rceil$ and $m=2^y-1$. Use the actual $2m\le A_m\le4m$ and the greatest even $f\le a/A_m$. Direct four-point butterflies handle the remaining $a-A_m f<2A_m$ bits. These choices have fixed integer algorithms.

The template batch $W_m$ is a power of two bounded by $m^K$. With $\sigma_m$ spatial calls, the binary label and incidence counts give
$$
 (\sigma_m/W_m)\sqrt{f/a}\le 2^{C_1\sqrt{y\log_2(y+2)}}.
$$
On a path $\sum_i\log_2 m_i\le\log_2 a$. Its batch product divides the actual $2^{k-s}$ root spectator count for large $k$. Each child carries $1/W_m$ of the current volume. The local cost per bit is at most $m^K(1+J(a))(1+\log_2(k+2))$, including the exact interchanger for its nonrecursive address operations. The next logarithmic width decreases by at least $y/2$, and integration gives $\Phi(x_a)-\Phi(x_a-y/2)\ge c_0\sqrt{y\log_2(y+2)}$. A fixed large exponent coefficient in the induction pays the recursive multiplier and the entire local cost. The two outer position moves and fixed powers of logarithms fit $J(k)$ after increasing its exponent constant. Half-sized spatial descendants and reuse of marked regions give $O(Nk)$ visited cells. [Rational template and coefficient compiler](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S003/route-a/compact-rational-walsh.md) · [Width recurrence, batch proof and routing](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S003/route-a/nonstationary-rational-walsh.md)

For signed mantissas $|u|<2^w$, $w=O(k)$, compute the exact integer Walsh sums in this layout. At each arity collect the primes in the denominators using a product of the seed factorial, the explicit ordinary coefficient denominators, 2, and all nonzero Newton gaps over the possible counts and signs. This gives an integer $U$ with $\log_2 U=k^{o(1)}$ on the entire width chain, obtainable in polynomial$(k)$ steps. The inverse Vandermonde denominators divide products of the node spacings. An actual Newton gap at arity 63 has a factor 73, so spacings alone are insufficient.

Use $M=1+2^{k+w+s+2}U$. All required rational instructions specialize to $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$. A short denominator $b$ need not be inverted as a long word. For $0\le z<M$ compute $r=(-z\bmod b)(M\bmod b)^{-1}\bmod b$ and return $(z+rM)/b$. This uses exact division by a short integer. The [fixed-head implementation](modular-scalar-machine.md) takes $O(KB+B^3)$ steps and at most $7K+16B+58$ visited cells for word width $K$ and short coefficient width $B$. Consequently the local work for a short numerator and denominator is $k\,m^{O(1)}$ per record. Shift the input by half the even active width in bits once to cancel the root normalization. An optional final unnormalized butterfly handles an odd selected width. The signed sums are smaller than $M/2$ in magnitude and hence uniquely recovered. For normalized output, even widths need a binary shift; odd widths use an exact squared-integer midpoint comparison after one scaled approximation. Componentwise rounding to a grid of mesh $2^{-p}$ gives complex Euclidean error at most $\sqrt{2N}\,2^{-p-1}$. All intermediates use $O(k)$-bit residue words regardless of their corresponding rational magnitudes. The same transform supplies the adjoint. The [kernel-checked row theorem](formal/ModularWalshExact.lean) proves that the composite-modulus calculation returns the exact signed sum for OpenAI's even-width Walsh target at every bank size and address width, whenever twice the input bank's $\ell_1$ mass is below $M$. It also proves the compositional statement for rational rows and their unique centered outputs. The sparse adaptive schedule and its tape costs are established in the written argument. The new obligation discharged here is the internal fault-propagation and coefficient-approximation argument. Exact outputs by themselves could also be recovered from a sufficiently accurate version of the earlier numerical theorem. ## 3. The integer caller and its thresholds

For $n\ge2$ set $b=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, $h=\lceil n/b\rceil$, $k=\lceil\log_2(4h)\rceil$ and $N=2^k$. The inputs are coefficient lists in radix $2^b$. A cyclic convolution of length at least $2h$ suffices. The short axes all have width $w=20\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$, except for a possible fully occupied final power-of-two axis. Search downwards from $2^w$ through a window of length $\lfloor2^w/k^2\rfloor$ for each odd axis coprime to the earlier ones. The [elementary interval proof](coprime-axes.md) certifies success for every prefix when $k\ge 2^{2^{100}}$. Multiplying the retained fractions gives occupied volume $S\ge N/2\ge2h$. This is the original gcd search in the released Lean source. The manuscript used primes from a short interval, and the Lean source used a quadratic Jacobsthal theorem to guarantee the gcd search. The elementary proof supplies this one dependency with the original widths and test. The numerical cutoff above concerns this search only.

Place the input digits on the first valid mixed-radix addresses and zero-pad. A balanced tree of controlled rotations takes mixed-radix ranks to scaled CRT residues; reverse its stages and signs for inversion. At every depth the controls use at most $k/2+O(\log k)$ bits. A block has $O(\log k)$ address bits and polynomial$(k)$ local tables. Guarded field swaps restore borrowed occupied coordinates. The source transforms each short cyclic axis by a chirp construction with a fixed number of reflections on neighboring fibers, processes axes in groups of four to seven, and synchronizes the block permutations, phase diagonals and selected-bit Walsh operations in $O(\log k)$ layers. Exact amplitudes are unitary at the layer boundaries. Precision is needed only there and at separate phase/product operations when using the modular Walsh implementation. [CRT, short transforms, phase streaming and recovery](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/integers.tex) · [Guarded field permutations](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/permutations.tex)

Use an absolute working grid fine enough for phase errors at most $2^{-70k}$ and Walsh boundary error at most $2^{-50k}$ for inputs of Euclidean norm at most $2^{13k}$. The grid for Walsh alone may use $52k$ fractional bits, and accepts a finer common grid. At layer boundaries $O(\log k)$ such errors accumulate additively by unitarity. The input norm is below $2^{5k}$ on accepted shapes. Forward-transform errors below $2^{-40k}$ leave the rounded pointwise product accurate to $2^{-30k}$ by $\|v\odot w\|_2\le\|v\|_2\|w\|_2$. Its norm remains below $2^{12k}$. The adjoint uses the same boundary allowance. Scaling by $\sqrt S\le2^{k/2}$ with a certified phase/scale grid leaves absolute error below $1/2$ in every convolution coefficient. Restore rank order by the inverse CRT permutation, round the resulting components to integers and propagate carries in radix $2^b$. Coefficients have magnitude below $h2^{2b}$ and carry words have $O(k)$ bits. Write exactly $2n$ contiguous output bits and clear temporary output marks. After the fixed-degree setup, the complete time bound is $O(Nk\sqrt{k}\,2^{O(\Phi(\log_2(k+2)))}(1+\log(k+2))^4)$. The absolute visited-space bound is $O(Nk)$. ## S006 scope for review

The modular Walsh execution, literal root-axis search and head/stack schedule make the proof dependencies more specific. The separate exact-CRT experiment found a materialization obstruction for the standard four-point additive stage. At extension degree $b\ge16$, writing a separate scalar/Frobenius representative for each class costs at least $2^b d/16$ bits, where $d$ is the degree of the chosen second direction. This rules out that layout for full-degree directions at the required space bound. Other encodings and fused stages remain open. At the S006 checkpoint the full CRT and first carry remained open. S007 supplies both, as linked at the top. [All-degree obstruction](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S006/four-point-all-degrees.md)

The [finest compatible bundles](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S006/four-point-bundle-coarsening.md) are the cosets of the additive span of all conjugates of the original directions. For every degree $b=3c$ and $q=2^b$, take a normal element $\alpha$ and the full-degree direction $\eta=\alpha+\alpha^{2^c}$. The quotient by the resulting stable subspace is a normal binary word of length $c$ modulo complementation. Keeping full field words on one representative of each bundle orbit costs $(3+o(1))q$ bits, including even $c$. This layout performs $\Theta(\sqrt b/(\log b)^2)$ consecutive ordinary additive levels in either direction within $O(q\sqrt b)$ steps and $O(q)$ visited bits. Rounding the integer caller's degree up to the next multiple of three enlarges its address space by at most four at every input length. The [Lean evaluation and interpolation proof](formal/AdditiveTraceLayers.lean) covers arbitrary independent ordered directions over any characteristic-two field. At every depth it identifies the forward map with evaluation in the unscaled product basis and verifies both inverse compositions. The [Frobenius transport theorem](formal/BundleTransport.lean) reconstructs the unique binary polynomial remainder on a conjugate coset, in its actual translated order. Applying the inverse automorphism restores every source coefficient. This result verifies the meaning of the retained representative; it supplies no bound for converting to the integer caller's external order. Both physical endpoints use the stated representative ordering. A reversible [coefficient prefix](../../sessions/2026-11-26-S006/stable-four-point-bundles.md#the-contiguous-binary-coefficient-input) performs up to $\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor$ radix splits directly on the caller's contiguous input in $O(q\sqrt b)$ steps and $O(q)$ visited bits. Each ordinary level of this implementation traverses a full stream. Running its $2b/3+1$ available levels would cost $\Omega(qb)$ even with free local arithmetic.
